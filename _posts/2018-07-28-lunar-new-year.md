In many Eastern countries, including Vietnam and China, Lunar New Year (the new year of the moon) often begins in the mid-February days of the standard calendar.

On these days, much like Christmas, people gather together with their families, enjoying a well-deserved break after a year of hard work. Particularly in Vietnam, where Lunar New Year is called "Tet," people eagerly anticipate this holiday and use this time to not only visit their parents but also reconnect with old teachers and friends. Additionally, it's a chance for travel, a getaway from the routine of daily life.

Why is Tet so special and joyful like that?

Firstly, Tet brings an extended period of time off for many people. They can stay at home, unwind, and do as they please. These days provide the perfect opportunity to catch up with friends, complete household chores, or simply rest after months of toil. Time slows down during Tet, allowing individuals to reflect on the past year and gather energy for the new one. Families often travel together or individuals take solo trips, making the most of this leisure time. All of these factors contribute to why Tet is so cherished by many native Vietnamese.

Tet is unique because it is traditionally infused with myths and culture. From special fruits and vegetable jams called "mứt" to "bánh chưng" and "bánh dày," white sticky rice cakes filled with baked beans or ground meat, many people view Tet as an opportunity to make a living or to focus on their primary work. Selling Tet specialties like botanical flowers (cherry and yellow apricot blossoms), the holiday becomes a critical period for these sellers to maximize their profits. If their sales are not successful, they risk significant losses. Fruits, beverages, and animals like chickens are also in high demand during this time.

Vietnamese people engage in a variety of activities during Tet, some of which are listed below:
* Playing card games
* Cooking stuffed sticky rice cakes and rounded sticky rice cakes
* Giving their homes a fresh look (cleaning, organizing, etc.)
* Decorating for Tet (hanging signs, calligraphies, buying Tet flowers)
* Visiting relatives, friends, and teachers
* Giving out lucky money
* Going back home with families
* Going out to the shops
* Participating in traditional games

Because Tet is a longstanding, special holiday, it's inevitable that superstitions continue to influence our lives significantly.
