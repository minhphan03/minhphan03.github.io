So I got my first internship offer two weeks ago. Truthfully, it’s not my first one, but it’s the first one I ever applied myself without any references. In a way, it makes every effort seems more validated and worth it. It isn’t like I absolutely detest connections and look down on people who get offers over their daddies’ phone calls or anything, but it’s simply much much harder to get a position in the job market nowadays without referrals and everything. That’s the truth, and it sums up the whole point of this post. I mean, my last summer internship was through a referral, but this one is not, and I’m so damn proud of it. I was so exhilarated reading that offer email like I was on cloud’s nine. I am not exaggerating when I say it was the best day of this year for me, considering how desperate and terrible I felt about my lack of experience for years as a college student comparing to my peers. There was this girl who got a return offer from LinkedIn and her twin sister’s from Salesforce, and that was nice and all, but holy shit the peer pressure was astronomical. I know this internship offer for me is not comparable, because it’s not from a tech company like any of them, but god it felt good to have SOMETHING. I never asked for much, so my expectations for everything stay as low as it can get. I told my therapist that I could not help but feeling anxious and nauseous just thinking about opening my emails to hear the decision. I deterred doing it for almost four days until I met her, which was something I have never done before, simply because I am as obsessive with checking emails as many teenagers with social media. I was afraid to imagine how I would react if I did not get it, and I coped with that by simply blocking it out. 

But I TRIUMPHED it. I was happy for exactly two whole days. Then the doubts started to creep in again. I was planning to work remotely, so I asked myself again whether it was a good decision. I projected my insecurities from my part-time job, thinking about how desperately I want to quit and find another one. And then graduation, and finding a 9-to-5 full-time job after that: they all ruined the fun and made me miserable. I think there's a valid, bubbling negative undertone in all of my posts like this: I can never keep myself happy for long. It's the same for all the things I have always found myself enjoying: traveling (how soon it's going to end), food (how I am about to finish the whole thing), or friends (how we are about to say goodbye and probably will never see each other again). I know I should be grateful being offered this opportunity, because I know how more desperate I would have been had I not gotten it. I dreamed about it all along. The joy was fleeting, but it was something bright in my life for once. All these responsibilities and pressure on my shoulders, I wish I could put it for another day, but you should understand how difficult it is for me to loosen myself up and enjoy my company for a day. I have all these valid reasons to not do it, and it's hard to break out of the loop I have finetuned so precisely. I impugned everything and everyone's motives and actions as I build a wall wrapping around myself. It's a coping mechanism. Oh how so emotionally grim I give and how desperately I need the world. I always look forward to fully bathing myself in the moment, but when it happens I always think about what's about to come down next; what is the next big thing that I have to sacrifice for in exchange of this simple, happy moment?
