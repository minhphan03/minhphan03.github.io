Another year passed, and now June’s coming back again with Pride. Per usual, Pride has always been controversial for unhinged (silly) reasons from ignorant people, so this year I want to talk a little bit about Pride, exclusive or not exclusive of flags, meaning, and history.

First, I want to concretize an opinion of mine that Pride is not outrageous or shown-off because it is not. It is about spreading positive opinions and celebrate the beauty of diversity in society — the message that people can be different and be themselves. Pride is not about privileges comparing to heterosexuals, or any issues of heterosexuals questioning why there is not a “straight pride” to begin with. From the Stonewall riots fighting for liberty and equality to the LGBT+ community in the 1960s until now, the community still faces discrimination, gaslighting and micro-agressions in their daily lives. One observation that my cousin mentioned that I found very thoughtful was the behaviors of many heterosexuals whenever they see an LGBT+ person: they were observed as a special type of people, and treated differently (being over-analyzed, under different observation, gaining unnecessary attention). They were treated as rare breeds, and it is because of these behaviors that exposures are crucial more than ever. We host Pride every year to show that our colors and identities are completely normal and there’s nothing to be considered “rare”. Breaking stereotypes are hard, but Pride events, being public attention grabbers and inclusive, are more than effective to break down these stereotypes. By exposing people and bringing newer generations into the acknowledgement that Pride parades are normal and fun and engaging, more closeted members of the communities would feel safe and allies would feel more motivated to protect our communities.

Next, I also want to talk about this redesigned Pride flag below

![progressive pride flag](/assets/images/progressive-pride-flag.jpg)
<p style="text-align: center">Progressive Pride Flag</p>



Unlike the normal Pride flag with only six colors, with this flag you can also see the triangular stripes on the side, representing Black and non-binary communities. While some loved the flag because of inclusion, many others condemned it for bringing another unrelated topic (race) into the pride flag. I think this is not a matter of race, but a matter of significant. The black and brown colors are not random. We need to trace back to the origins of Pride to understand why they deserve it. For a long time, they have been severely marginalized from the Pride movements while being the most progressive and contributory to our present. Furthermore, the arrow-like design represents progression and mission to push forward, which is also very interesting. (Lang, 2020) It should not be a matter of race anymore because for a long time white counterparts have been the prominent part of the pride image, and it lacks the diversity we need. By including POCs, we are recognizing a large contribution to our concurrent times, as well as sending a message of progression and development to bring our communities closer together.
So, the next time you think about Pride, think about how progressive you have been to all the changes that happened to the community. Everything happens for a reason, and it’s not the end of the world if people are represented in honor of their legacies.

Once again, happy Pride! 🌈
