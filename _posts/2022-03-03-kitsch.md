*Just some pieces of thoughts in my drafts that I rediscovered, tracing back to when I was reading Mila Kundera’s “The Unbearable Lightness of Being"*

Life is the result of sequential events and bits of randomness

I still haven’t finished The Unbearable Lightness of Being yet, but I got new ideas to write about lessons I’ve learned from the book. Here’s one of them:

I think this is the main idea of the book, that is “life is supposed to be random, so assigning values on randomness makes it an unbearable lightness of being” At first I regard randomness as light and the act of “assignment” as weight, but coming towards the end of the book prompted me to reconsider my ideas. That lightness is the belief that life only occurs once, and weight is the thought that life is recurrent. Who believes that life only occurs once will not be haunted by “einmal its keinmal” meaning “what only happens once may not happen at all” and therefore will live his or her life to the fullest, knowing that it’s acceptable to think that imminent events are always unpreventable and hence should not go to lengths to prevent the bad from happen. On the other hand, people who believe the latter notion are kept captive in unbearable thoughts that their future relies in their decisions one and all, and when they go to re-experience their life once again, they will have to base all of their decisions at any moment in their life to bring out the brightest results available. They are the unbearable beings living in lightness of life, which we already know that life is as light as a feather. What happens today may not happen again tomorrow, and carefully chosen objectives are not surely going to carry out successfully.

One does not have the ability to understand if his or her actions will bring out any charm. What a carefully outlined plan may go backfire with unprecedented outer factors interfered, and there’s no hope to prevent that interference right from the outset. The interference may come as randomness as a person wandering into the ghetto and getting killed, or accidents on road that leave someone’s mom handicapped for the rest of her life. I agree that in behind the hindsight of randomness comes some form of logic, like the habit of walking near the ghetto of that man or the drunk state of the driver, but all in all randomness still manage to play a big role in our life: the  appearance of a man during a time at a point on the street, for example. People sometimes just act on their instincts, so there is no surprise that life can be so inscrutable at times. One cannot satisfy himself if he wants to know what would happen if he knew everything beforehand, because if everyone has the ability to do so, one would try to alter the future and then the future would universally distorted. That’s why actions are destined yet random. There’s a certain expectation from them, but the remaining relied purely on luck. Praying is a perfect example for this: you pray because you believe in a certain outcome if you do so, which helped you gain self-belief and in one way or another boost your confidence. It then actually prompt you to achieve it, but the outcome you expect may or may not be there. Based on multitudes of factors, randomness of distinct objects may cost your dedication and there’s nothing you can do about it.

Kitsch is the painting of fake happiness, ingenue positiveness and hollow beauty. Kitsch accords with reputation. Public figures 

Nobody can escape kitsch. It is an integral part within our conscience.

Kitsch is weight, is the distorted, hallucinating illusion that people always believe in, that they always destine to be. Since nobody can live without kitsch (as it works as a motivation for humans to develop themselves), whoever tries to cut kitsch of their bodies only makes his life unbearable (hence the unbearable lightness of being). If one is able to live without kitsch, he is devoid of melodrama but the raw remaining is worthless to him. Sabina is an archenemy to kitsch, and look where it led her: becoming a side mistress, fleeing to alien lands and living an aimless life. Tomas only pursue the rawness of eroticism, and while he gained pleasure and excitement, at the end of time he had to step down from it and settle down in toxic bubbles of kitsch.

Love is pure kitsch. Love has failed expectations. When people talk about love, they think about betrayal as much as affection. If they look at a happy couple, a lot would mock as if the emotions they have when they are together are just parts of a big facade behind. That’s why both of the un-kitschy characters in the books — namely Tomas and Sabina — never fell in love. With love comes with responsibility, repercussion, giving and taking, etc. which are sated with weight and constraint. With love comes disappointment and hesitations. With love comes drama and abysses. People paint love as magical and powerful, but behind the scenes of love reveal a pure two way exchange mechanism. It cannot work if there is no exchange. If you cannot invest in your relationship, it’s doomed to fail.

The Grand March can be defined as the march of the human race to complete happiness. They put on facades and banners and signs, thought as act of patriotism and justice, only to promote themselves, talk down modes of government and create stunts. 

But isn’t believing in un-kitsch a kitsch? Isn’t an aromantic proponent a kitsch, too? Then Sabina and Tomas only live in disillusion all these time?
