I had my first watch in 2017, in 8th grade. Funny how it was an Apple Watch, because I only bought it out of the fact that it was cool at the time (still cringe at how shallow I was in secondary school) and I was such an Apple sheep (still am, but I would not go all the way THERE to peacock to people how obsessive I am with a capitalistic company). It was a birthday present, and I just happened to think that that if I have an Apple Watch that would be so cool and since it was not a phone, I practically could use it in class... Believe me, I was very naive at the time, despite how true that statement was. As predicted, many of my friends were impressed with it and asked to use it all the time, and I had an exciting, useful time trying out the features on the small wearable device, from the Activity rings to the camera remote, but it was a real hassle when it comes to charging and distraction. I had the urge to check on it all the time in class, and felt empty when it died from staying on-screen for too long. It was not very durable, for the matter: how could a cheap $250 watch sustain the active life and carelessness of a ninth grade child? I brought it into my showers despite fully knowing it was not waterproof, rubbing it against the hardwood desk in my classes while rushing through homework, and countless other incidents I don't attempt to recall it now. I still think that it was a cool device for those who need it, and the reason I only stopped using mine was because the screen cracked terribly one day and my parents decided that it was too old and not worth for fixing the damage. I could have asked for another newer version (probably Series 3 at that time?) but I did not, as news on academic misconduct using smartwatches blew up all over social media at the time and school authorities ban the use of smart watches in classes. I stopped using a watch since then. I missed how it motivated me to work out and stay active, but to be frank I could live without it just fine and I still had to walk miles a day from my bus stop back home every afternoon because my parents decided I was old enough to be able to safely treading along the streets and maybe could buy snacks if I wanted. I also have not mentioned I had extra classes at the time that would no doubt mess up and annoy their evening downtime, the only time they could recharge their sanity levels. Okok back to the watch... that's the gist of the story. It was a good watch, but unfortunately ill-fated. I did not really know what I wanted at the time, and I got entangled to the temptation of the masses. However, if it was not for its existence, I would never see my current watch in the daylight. 

Purchasing a new watch has been in the back of my mind for a while as Apple introduced the new generations of its best-seller product. I almost gave in to buy one of them when they put Pride watch bands on sale. It was a charming one, for sure: the braided, elastic loop, so strong yet so personalizing and free. After all I still did not buy it, albeit the lure was strong. I could not justify spending over five hundred bucks for an item I would feel bad every time I glanced at it for how distracting and addicting it was. I learned my lessons better. So I waited and waited, until I came across some watches of a brand call Mondaine on a webstore. I fell in love with them at first sight. They was strikingly simple in a good way, all pure elegance. And they're cheap (because they are quartz watches). Have I told you how I am literally fawning over how sleek and lovely their design is? Check them out for yourself... Oh Apple Watch you're bones!

Update:

I pulled the trigger and bought this used Mondaine watch on eBay for a little more than $100. It was much more beautiful in person, althought I did not like the strap one bit. It was made of leather and so was undurable. Well, at least I got the watch I wanted for so long :) This is a perfect birthday present for myself, and I am going to cherish it forever!!!
