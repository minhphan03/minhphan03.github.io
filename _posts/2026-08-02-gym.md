Never thought I would spend time detailing how I happened to start going to the gym and actually found it somewhat gratifying, yet here we are. I finally admitted to myself that maybe this is not so much as an overblown trend that one simply finds themselves somehow having the urge to improve their physique and excruciate their body just to feel worn out afterwards. I did, and psychologically it was actually very rewarding — that I was putting my body through hoops so that I could keep it in shape and flexible. I, by no means, magically and suddenly became a religious gym rat; however, driving to the free school gym after my volunteer shift every Tuesday and did some exercises happened to be a good use of my time instead of driving back home in the traffic to do nothing and lie in bed afterwards. 

It’s all about mentality, I suppose. People say that it’s always harder to decide to do something than getting along with it. Once you made that move, it’s easier to establish a rhythm and develop a habit. Not that it’s any easier to do so, but when you have fear about something, it takes courage to try. That’s how I felt about the gym. I waited until the perfect opportunity arose to made the move, and I thought that was rather pathetic at first, but looking back I was glad that at least I did it. I used to joke that my goal was going to the gym once a year, given how disinterested I was towards the gym culture, but now that I am willing to look past it by stopping belittling what should be encouraged to a trend and starting to focus on myself, my introspection into my own physical proved to be rather positive. And it looked like everyone at the gym thought so, too. They minded their own businesses. They came for exercises and/or buffing up their bodies with their headphones, (most of the time) scrolling on their phones in between, then left. My paranoia thought I would be made fun of or silently judged, and despite having heard the absolute opposite, stories of harassments and gay cruising would not leave me alone. Therefore, it greatly helped that staying busy and getting worn out from exercising did wonders to my mentality on top of the physical benefit. Stepping out of the gym after, I never failed to feel lighter and worthier despite heavily sweating from head to toe in the scorching summer heat. Maybe that’s the secret high that these gym rats were seeking: a boost of endorphins and eventually serotonins. 

Because of this, I aim to stick to this “habit” for one or two days a week to keep myself in shape, going on in the future. As I have established a set to follow (alternating between leg and arm exercises) when I visited the gym, let’s just say this is a decent success story. While the stepper and treadmill work wonders on my heart and legs, the assisted chin, chess press, and pull down machines are the ones I find useful for developing my chest and arms. I still need to add some exercises for my hind muscles, but that’s not a priority for now. The important thing is, of course, not letting this success story be a dud. I do hope so, because I don’t have much going on with myself anyway.

P/S: I’m still scared of the weightlifting rooms, though. I tried lifting once and never again. Maybe one day. Or never.
