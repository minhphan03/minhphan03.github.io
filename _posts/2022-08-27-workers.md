I just came back from a 5-day trip to Northern Vietnam, and while I was delighted with how the trip turned out to be (countless pictures, lots of places visited, and memories overflown), I have become more fully aware of the wealth gap between the rich and poor in Vietnam, and even more between Vietnam and the developed parts of the world.

The story began during our canoe trip in Trang An, Ninh Binh. We were visiting the caves when my father asked the paddler how much she got paid for a three-hour journey we were on, to which she replied 200,000VND (around $9), and we were stunned. The fact that she had to paddle on her own, as a woman, without any muscle support (we did help her afterward) was alarming. Why was life so unequal to so many? You see these celebrities living in riches and people at the bottom of the ladder barely living by. The worst thing is that the $9 is not guaranteed most of the time. She told us that they rarely have two trips a day, and hundreds of boats were waiting by the water station for passengers. The only time they can thrive is during holidays, when many tourists are around. I could not even imagine how she could support her family working like this, but she did not attempt to satiate my curiosity. Obviously, Trang An is a rural area; hence its cost of living should work with around $9 a day (fortunately, we haven’t accounted for her husband, but I doubt he can make much more than that). Still, another issue arises: shouldn’t we call this labor abuse? We paid like $30 a ticket per person, yet she could never get to see half of that while being both our guide and “driver.”

Yes, I have to account for taxes, marketing, maintenance that can gouge out the charges, and blah blah blah, but don’t you think it’s ridiculous how tourists never really get to reap their experience from those elements? It’s very much like stories of servers in big cities living off tips, as their base salary starts at $5/hr. Even if the cost of living in the local area could justify $5 as a good starting point (which is absolutely not), I don’t think it is a reasonable compensation for how much labor these people had to give to deserve this. Chimed in this part, I did not believe the paddler could be lying, and I still don’t. Even with tips, this model of employment is simply unsustainable. Tourism is 100% dependent on the seasonal market, and the people who rely on this business can never truly settle down financially. I wonder how they can survive when I barely live on my own, even with my parent’s financial support…

I have never denied how privileged I am to have been born and living in a big city with a comfortable life, but sometimes I forget how striking it is comparing it to many others. Our psychological beings are rendered (again, blaming social media) to look up to more affluent lifestyles yet wildly learned to ignore and be insensible on less fortunate beings. Therefore I regarded this opportunity to listen to other people’s stories (thanks, dad) as a necessary wake-up call on the wealth gap that we seemingly forget (or try to) and call for action on working towards a better future for all. This is not a story about how we should redistribute wealth because it is inherently impossible if one to look up how capitalism works, but about how we should bring equity to all. I barely do anything at work for $17/hr, and I feel bad when I ponder over these people. How can somebody sustain a life like that, working their blood, sweat, and tears off to be so disgustingly underpaid? I always believe that one deserves (and should be able) to reap the fruits they have tediously worked towards, and I hope everyone does, too. Many give up fighting for equality, quoting that the world has always been unfixably unequal; does it mean that we collectively should give up, too? No! I know this is all words and no action, and it’s just how life works most of the time. Still, I hope that through these words, you will remember the next time you see somebody who is less fortunate, be gentle and respectful. Don’t let the ugly side of the news blind you that maybe they were faking being poor for sympathy, or poverty and beggars are the results of laziness. If they are willing to show you their lowest, treat them with kindness because not everyone dares to do it. And yes, exploiters are gonna exploit just like fakers are gonna fake, but aren’t we all condoning many unjustifiable things in our lives already? In a way, they are working. Even if you don’t do anything, don’t shit on them. And the next time you visit a place, don’t hesitate to support the local communities directly to the people who work on serving and making sure you have a good time there.
