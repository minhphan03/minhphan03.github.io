I would like to tell you about the trip to Hue City - the old capital of Vietnam - all the way to the North - to Quang Binh province, passing through Quang Tri province. The trip started on July 23rd and ended on the 26th, 2018.

**DAY 1: HCMC - HUE CITY**

So at 4 A.M., we had to wake up early to arrive at the airport on time, complete boarding passes, and go through security checks. For anyone who has been to Vietnam, the sight of people queuing up for security checks is unforgettable. If someone is too late, there's a hidden machine to expedite the process - quicker than waiting in the sea of people. It took about 1 hour and 30 minutes for the flight to Hue City, landing at Phu Bai Airport. The airport is notably smaller than HCMC’s, with only a few flights departing and landing there. Just to clarify, our trip was neither a guided tour nor a self-planned journey. We had relatives working in a travel company who reserved hotel rooms and drivers for us. During these 4 days, we were driven in cars by 3 different people.

After being picked up at the airport, we went to Hue rail station to meet our grandparents. Our first stop was the Tu Dam Pagoda, a famous one in Hue known for its age, built in the eighteenth century, and various ceremonies including the Establishment of the Buddhist Sangha of Vietnam, etc. Later, we stayed at Muong Thanh, a 4-star resort (quite reasonably priced compared to HCMC). After enjoying local specialties like che bap (a sticky beverage with minced corn) and “banh nam,” we visited Quoc Hoc High School for the gifted, where Mr. Vo Nguyen Giap, Secretary of the Central Military Commission of the Communist Party of Vietnam, had taught history. This school was one of the first universities built by the French in Vietnam.

In the afternoon, we visited two tourist attractions in Hue: Minh Mang Tomb and Khai Dinh Tomb. While Khai Dinh Tomb had French influence (because King Khai Dinh lived during the period when the French were actively trading with Vietnam), with iron doors and photographs, Minh Mang Tomb had Chinese cultural inspiration with red and yellow as the main colors, adorned with dragons and waves carved beautifully on stones. Minh Mang Tomb had a significantly larger area than the other one, with lakes, bridges, and hills. Both tombs represent the eras they were built in, displaying flawless architecture and carvings, showcasing human ingenuity. Since the tombs were located far from the city and there was only one route to reach them, we spent approximately 3 hours visiting both tombs, as we had to walk inside the tombs, which was unavoidable.

Dinner was at Nieu Rice, featuring rice cooked in small clay pots, accompanied by typical Vietnamese dishes. Quite mouth-watering, right?

**DAY 2: HUE CITY**

Our first stop the next day was Thien Mu Pagoda, a nationally renowned pagoda in Vietnam, famous for its history and superstitions. As Hue is divided by the Huong River (also called Perfume River), we visited the Trang Tien Bridge - a historical relic built in 1889 and designed by the famous architect Gustave Eiffel. Thien Mu Pagoda also had towers, the tallest one having 7 stories. The area where Buddhists live and pray is in the middle of the loft, surrounded by houses. The sanctum was surprisingly larger and more ornate than expected, given its small, ancient, and simple front gates.

We then took a boat to visit Hon Chen Shrine, which is accessible only by waterways. The shrine was small and ancient, reachable via steep stairs. During the trip, we noticed numerous baskets of fruits along the way (later we learned they were used for offerings or squandered). I wasn’t sure about the specific reason for this worship, but it was a carefully organized activity, involving votive paper burning (similar to burning offerings for the deceased). It was a religious practice, perhaps more for show, but who cares; it’s a tradition that should not be casually banned without careful consideration.

A long boat trip along the Perfume River isn’t unique, but “Ca Hue on the Perfume River” was. For those unfamiliar, Ca Hue refers to the special performances of local songs in Hue, including “Li” - folk songs sung by peasants in old communities. Performers wore Ao Dai and were divided into two groups: singers and instrumentalists using traditional instruments like the two-chord fiddle and Dan Tranh (a 16-chord zither).

That concluded our day. The following day, we would head to the North.

**DAY THREE: HUE - QUANG TRI - QUANG BINH**

Unlike the previous day, the third day of the trip started late, at 9:00 A.M. Obviously, we were excited for the day when we would explore the Ho Chi Minh road for the first time. Odd as it might seem, the Ho Chi Minh road is a crucial route used by soldiers and aid workers to transport supplies to the North Vietnamese army force in the south. This road holds historical significance for the army and volunteers who worked tirelessly to keep the route safe, allowing aid to be delivered. This aid helped our brave soldiers continue fighting against the rival force, which was the US army, leading to the remarkable victory - the Liberation of South Vietnam and the Unification of Vietnam. (Lol)

Before heading to the famous route, we visited the Ancient Citadel of Quang Tri, located in Quang Tri province (lol, obviously 🙄). The historic site gained fame for the Second Battle of Quang Tri, which lasted 81 days and nights, resulting in over 7000 casualties for both sides (for anyone who forgot, that’s between the North Vietnamese People’s Army of Vietnam and the Republic of Vietnam, with financial and weapon aid from the US). *You can find more on Google.* Inside the Citadel was a monument and a museum full of documents and items, including bombs and bullets.

But there was more to see than just the Citadel. There was the renowned Hien Luong Bridge, known for marking the temporary borderline between North and South Vietnam (it's exactly located on the 17º latitude — find it on Google too, lol). The bridge has two colors indicating the respective sides, with blue for the North and old-hue yellow for the South.

Moving along the Ho Chi Minh Route was serene and desolate. There were woods on both sides and very few small houses, which made me wonder why people had built houses in these rural areas. Perhaps for rubber tree plantations and forestry? That could be an acceptable answer.

We arrived at Dong Hoi City around 3 P.M., the only city in Quang Binh, where we spent the night. After checking in and taking a short rest, we had dinner 🥘 by the riverside, near the sea. Seafood and local dishes were on the menu. That’s when I discovered that scallops were pretty irresistible! Especially when grilled, with spicy chili sauce and fried minced shallots (omfg, I’m starving just thinking about it). The specialties included soft noodles (rice vermicelli) with seafood (imagine having vermicelli instead of seafood porridge). It was a fantastic dinner, the best I’d had in a long time.

We took a drive around the city, and that concluded the third day.

**DAY 4: QUANG BINH - HUE**

We had to pass through Quang Binh to return to Hue, but there was no planned stop there, except for getting some takeaway (I will talk about this later).

This, to be precise, was the best of the 4 days, and I’m eager to share what I explored in the land of Quang Binh - a province with a dry, humid climate located in the unlucky coastal lands of Central Vietnam. If you wonder why it is ‘unlucky,’ it’s because there are strong storms and floods every year, coupled with little mineral resources and a humid climate during the summer.

However, the exploration of Phong Nha - Ke Bang (and Son Doong, for sure) has significantly boosted the economy of this province. The power of tourism has revitalized the region, but it’s a two-sided situation. If they can preserve the site, it will help improve the lives of the local people. However, there’s a downside: the loss of nature and economy, reaching rock bottom (with the helplessness of other economic sectors) — yes.

The reason why Phong Nha Cave (I only visited that site) is so powerful lies in the story of my visit there and witnessing one of the best natural heritages in the world. That award is widely accepted, so don’t doubt it.

When we arrived at Ke Bang town, it was pouring rain. I was anxious because I thought we might have to cancel the highly anticipated trip. My dad had talked about it every day before the trip, which made me excited too. That’s why I was so unprepared for the possibility of cancellation. I was such an idiot to have believed that. In fact, because it was a cave, the rainwater did not penetrate deeply inside, and the water level wasn’t high enough to make visiting impossible. So, despite the rain, we were able to go to the station to mark the beginning of our journey. No one was less excited, not even my granddad. For safety reasons, we had to wear life jackets on the way to the cave, crossing a river. It — was — magnificent. The cave emerged before my eyes. A primary forest atop the mountain, and below lay the cave. We traveled into the cave by boat. The boundary between light and darkness in the cave signaled that we were almost there. Darkness enveloped us. Electric lights illuminated the wonders inside. There were formations sculpted by nature’s magical hands, with thousands of different shapes, interconnected to leave us in awe. I saw what appeared to be a lion but was just a twist in perspective, a bunch of falling leaves. A rocky crocodile on the rough wall or a toucan, all right before my eyes. It was wonderful and left me speechless. It was indescribable. You have to see it for yourself, with your own eyes. And then you’ll understand how nature works. A waterfall, a miniature rocky jungle, a pair of poles, and thousands of unique objects (if your creativity is active) in an area of 3km² (lol, we could only access this much). All this, aided by the strategically placed lights. The lights were positioned near *recognizable* objects (to help visitors understand what they were looking at easily).

At this point, I feel like I’ve revealed too much about this trip, so maybe I should move on to the end of the journey. But before that, we also visited Vo Nguyen Giap’s tomb, situated on a small hill with a great view of the seashore. There are so many interesting facts about these three provinces I visited on this trip. For instance, although these provinces have a hot climate, there are pine trees!

From Quang Binh, we returned to Hue in the same car. Then we had a Hue-style dinner with Bun Bo Hue, and afterwards, we bid farewell to our grandparents at the Hue station. All parties must end. And so, we said goodbye to Hue. At Phu Bai Airport, the trip came to an end. It was a wonderful and unforgettable experience. There were many discoveries, different cultural rituals, and beliefs that opened my eyes. That’s why my life is dedicated to traveling. I love to travel."
