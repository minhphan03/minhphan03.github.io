*SPOILER ALERT! PLEASE DO NOT PROCEED IF YOU PLAN ON WATCHING THE FILM 🙂*

I don’t think I have watched a movie that disturbing and unsettling as this one since Midsomar, and it was 2 years ago. The fingers took me out in the worst way and reminded me of my cutting finger incident recently. I felt ill. I’m still feeling ill as I’m writing this. The extreme to which a person who come to do to remove somebody out of his life is boundless, dare I say. When Padraic voiced out his thoughts of returning Colm’s finger back to him, never have I ever felt so sick to my stomach. WHY WAS HE SO DUMB? But again, he was just a character, and they could act dumb all they want.

I wrote all this out with the sole purpose of telling you how moving this movie is.

Colm wanted nothing to do with niceness and kindness because he concerned with a legacy, for only with a legacy and not niceness would one need to be remembered (Mozart, i.e.). Padraic, on the other hand, perplexed of the sudden cold shoulder from his then-considered good friend in this boring small town, demanded to find a resolution to his restlessness. He was an affectionate man, and rightfully so: his sister kept him sane through the times he witnessed how Colm, despite saying not wanting to do anything with his “wasted” past life with Padraic, conversed with the bigoted policeman of the town and the owner of the pub they frequently visited. If I were him, I’d probably go feral, too. Loud silence never sits right with me, but again, if he sent me a finger with fresh blood still dripping, I’d shut right the hell up.

The concept of alienation is strong here, and I don’t care about the context of the movie (which is set around the Ireland Civil War, which I have no clue about) enough to how it plays out in the main themes. In my simple interpretation, I reckon that Padraic’s reactions were humane. Witnessing his pet donkey died because of the finger(s), his sister leaving (good for her), and his so-called “friend” abandoning him can really push an already closed-off man to the verge of insanity. Hell, I’d be reduced to tears if someone told me I’m just a waste of their time, a bummer of the past they no longer want to be a part of. I am no stranger to lonesome, but luckily I never have to witness such drastic switch of a situation. Revenging by setting Colm’s house on fire turned out not really solved anything: Colm was still alive, and he still had to face his biggest fear for the rest of his life: this man who has turned his life upside down, sending him fingers (GOSH I HAVE TO EMPHASIZE THIS THING AGAIN) and indirectly causing his sister to leave. If only he would have stayed in the house and died in there would he finally feel peace at last! 

But in the end, turned out the best revenge is to watch your “enemy” suffer: “There are some things you cannot move on from.” That’s very petty of him, but to an extend it’s understandable. I’d spare the explanation to this, as you can deduce from my reasoning above. Simply said, we cannot go back in time to fix some of these decisions, but at some point you will realize that making these questionable choices may serve a greater purpose than you think. Things that you may not normally spill out of your mouth and make others misjudge you (an example is when Dominic expressed his disappointment over Padraic’s outbursts of emotions over Colm’s fiddler), but as the movie unfolds, these moments lead to a burst of climax, enough to draw a somewhat satisfying reaction from Colm himself after the one-sided silent feud that drove everyone insane. 

We all do questionable things in life in searching for answers. We all think about the meaning of life and our legacy after our death. We all experience emotions, departures and losses. We are humans, and existentialism is one hell of a question. The word “Banshees” in the title of the movie signifies a defiance of this notion by putting this idea of self-determination into the hands of some external, alien force. The dichotomy, along with many others throughout the film, together have painted up a powerful picture of human emotions and existence in a dramatic yet realistic way. I’d probably not watch this movie again given how emotionally draining it is, but it is certainly an unforgettable one.
