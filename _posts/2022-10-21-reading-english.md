On reading English articles & playing crosswords.

How random this title can be, isn't it? What does reading English articles have to do with playing crosswords? But it does! So here I am, as I suddenly felt an urge to write this piece after reading a couple of random articles published on Gawker after visiting a gay writer Twitter account (well, he was also kinda hot, but it's totally not worth mentioning, and his pieces were extravagant, so I was stoked to see what else he had written, so the following button was not difficult to click) while skipping my boring Zoom lectures. Actually, at this point I'm writing here the class is still going on and I still have not checked the lecture out yet. I could watch it later and take a couple of notes on later, I assume. 

Back to the pieces of writing that he wrote. The first one I wrote was the newest piece he shared in a tweet. It was about "Gay Sincerity". I was enchanted. How fancy the words and grammar structures he used to demonstrate and express his opinions. When I reached the word "baton", I thought it was some kind of underwear but turned out it was a type of wand. I still don't remember what is the actually word that I mistake it with, but I'm not bothered to look for it at the moment. I'll eventually come across it again one day, that much I know is expected. The rueful thing is that I would not remember this part right here to visit and update it.

I (mentally) chastised myself on not trying to learn more vocabularies to understand these words, because every time I tried to comprehend what the writer was saying (by breaking down the sentences into words I knew and did not) I kept getting distracted by the impulse to look up the latter part. In my mind was a mindset that if I cannot figure out how they necessarily contribute to the content, I can never fully savor and comprehend the lessons themselves. It messed up with my head that even the words that I thought that I have encountered countless times and I should have remembered their meanings by now, but I did not. I was so paranoid after encountering so many words I was confident I have never seen them before that I thought the ultimatum would be something like "supercalifragilisticexpialidocious" and until then I would relinquish any attempt to reading English texts. I was so obsessed with looking them up that I completely forgone demystifying what the author was trying to convey. Thankfully in the original tweet some guys did hint some clues: basically he was saying that our gay media content is obsessive over depicting the dirtiest, most naked truth in the gay, in particular, or queer, in general, community. And in the world that has been so stricken with issues, these publications only serve to worsen and distort our already less than optimistic worldview (on literally everything). For so long of human history the queer community was (and still is) oppressed, and what we want now more than anything are pieces that actually lift up our hopes (not in a blindly way) not ones that give out self-homophobia, blantant cheating and rendezvouses (I'm looking right at you Brokeback Mountain)

With that being said, learning a language is like herding cats: you cannot control what you can learn. Every time you thought you master something, another challenge come up. Being a fluent speaker does not mean that you can be mistaken as a native. The way you practice English can tell you right away if you are an English native speaker or not. Look at how native people talk in movies. Their dialogues seem more elite than normal and have a different feel to it: why would they constantly reference some type of cultural identities that the writers themselves clearly know all too well that not all of us can relate or point connections to. At this point I was too exhausted of selecting and searching, so I disregarded and hoped that they would reveal some clues later on. In dialogues I have with my native Vietnamese speakers I never include so many references like these. Or maybe English people just converse in a completely off-the-wall way with their rich collection of mass media, that they feel the urge to veil their knowledge away from other cultures by cramming all these unnecessary details I swear even themselves would not recall after forty-seven seconds.

(I don't know if this is a widely accepted belief or not but) I believe that if you want to be a successful writer, you have to read extensively and experience the world in all of its forms. I barely read, and my life experiences, in my opinion, are quite limited. I mean, what's the better way that stumbling upon phrases that, fortuitously got repeated over and over again in the books you've recently read? If "panache" -- a word that I vividly remember appearing in one of my SAT writing course vocabulary sheets I had to memorize in preparation for weekly quizzes -- popped up in my brain more frequently, I would certain know it by heart right now. In fact, I only registered it while reading another piece on Gawker I was curious to scan through after reading the title (turned out it was only a boring fiction about a publisher manager and the influence of parents -- and parenting styles -- on her social perceptions with her friend Rachel).

The later part of the title come from my recent habit to play the NYT crosswords. I only opened them occasionally when I was bored at work and decided nothing else would be more worth it than training my brain with a couple of wordplay and, again, references. I knew right from the beginning that I was an absolutely terrible solver, and so whenever I encounter clues that I have... no clue (oh my god they were insanely ambiguous) and begin to think anyone whom was able to figure them out must be encyclopedia personified (bad attempt at personification I know) I would start smashing my keyboards until I figured them out. A lot of times I came across regional phrases that I would never dream of using but it was nice to know their existence anyway. How ridiculous it is for it to take such a long time to dawn on me that no matter how much I attempt to be excellent in English, there is still such a big road to venture. I am absolutely star-struck when I met people who can master (not know) like 3 languages or more because I’m here struggling with English and solving crosswords only add to that self-acknowledgement. I paid New York Times over $30 just to feel like a dumbass, such a laughing stock I was. 

(Update one year later: I stopped the subscription and moved on. So much for a learning curve!)

Also, let's move on...

"America (or, should I say the United States?) is a melting pot, and the fusion of cultures never fails to reprimand me (or, educating me) why I cannot ever act right in front of strangers. Somehow my actions would inadvertently offend someone. I apprehended communicating and was grateful for the days when I could hole up in my dorm room, eating whatever crossing my eyes and killing my time by either reading eroticas, busying my homework or surfing social media, which fills with nothing but people complaining about their exasperating unethical working environment, capitalistic living expectations, and how Joe Biden could wipe off the student loan with just a lift of his finger (by signing some acts and papers, I assume) but he would not do so because he was a manipulative liar."

I looked up the thesaurus and my personal dictionary five times just writing this piece (I was not certain that busy was a verb, so I double-checked it too), and unashamedly I would later copy these texts into Grammarly and watch in awe as it points out how many errors I have violated. I would copy them back, post somewhere else for further reading and posting. And then I will be back, in situ. And I doubt this is advanced English writing (which I know only the elites, bookworms, academia people or English writers and journalists can understand) that I tried my best to accomodate. I would fix them all, accepting most of its suggestions so the final product would result in a clean piece with no underlines. Nothing changed, and I still have not learned any new phrases. I'll probably forget this after a while just like other issues I have written before, thanks to other ones dashing in, I'll be constantly reminded how crazy reading comprehension skills required can be sometimes. But one thing I know, that reading English articles is like playing crosswords: one needs to look up the dictionary constantly if they wish to understand what they are actually looking at. And it's frustrating as hell!
