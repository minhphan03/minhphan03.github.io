I kind of hit the point where a birthday does not matter as much anymore as it used to be in the past, which was a warning that I pointed out to myself a couple years ago when this tradition of posting a birthday blog became a thing. In fact, I was writing this three days (and finally finished it six days) after my birthday when I have nothing else to do and it’s just too early to go home and I need to do something for about half an hour before hitting the movie theater to rewatch – what I already expected would be bad – "Love Actually," a “holiday classic” that many people non-jokingly enjoy. It's a coincidence that the movie was released on the same year I was born. To me it’s a silly movie, but on this December cold Tuesday night I don’t really have any other better thing to do except going home and holing myself up in the darkness of a lonely bed. Also, it’s been a while since I went to the theaters anyway, so I figure I should not let my subscription go to waste. 

Being twentytwo is funny, because before I hit twenty I have always looked forward to singing 22 by Taylor Swift when the time comes, as if it was the only memorable thing one could do when the year hit. On that day I blasted 22 (Taylor’s Version) in my car all the way to work, all one hour and a half of it, working a boring labor job that Karl Marx would detest, because the worker is always alienated to his work and finds absolutely no meaning in a capitalist working environment. If I’m honest with myself, I do this for the money and money only, because what else could I have done? I got fired at my on-campus job which I don’t even feel enthusiastic about, so I can only cling to the only means I can do to grasp some kind of income. So lives have changed and I have changed a year later since I last turned twenty-one over a year ago. I think it’s nice, really, when birthdays don’t hold such a big value anymore because it just saddens me to think about where I am standing in life at the moment. There are just so many restrictions on my life right now I hope I can do something other than lamenting but I just keep on lamenting about what a pathetic life I am leading right now. I’m sure my parents (at least my mom) would dispute otherwise, but peer pressure in this day and age is not the same as it was twenty years ago. In other words, just all around “happy, free, confused, and lonely at the same time.” When Taylor sang this line out loud, it seemed strange: I’m technically all of that, but at the same time I’m not. I’m happy (not entirely happy, but still somewhat happy), free (so free, but directionless), confused (now this one is one hundred percent correct), and lonely (double down on that, but I’ve always been a loner, and I know spirit-wise I have some support but when it comes to everyday life I have to manage everything on my own). So even if twentytwo hits or twentythree hits or twentyfour, some of these elements will still follow me and as much as I lied to myself that I’ve gotten so used to it, the truth is, it still hits me like a brick sometimes. 

Maybe I need therapy again. Two years ago I did. It did get better, then it got worse as soon as I stopped going. I am not suicidal, but at the point where almost everything is outside of my control and I got so intimidated and paranoid about everything and nothing in my life, the years sort of blend together into this mindless stream of unconsciousness that I just follow through every day. I know I should break out of it, but you know there’s only so much you can do except to wait and let fate decide where your life will bring. Raw-dogging life like this without drugs or money or physically close friends is not for the weak, mind you. But I’m still here somehow, haha.

But enough of the depressing thoughts. Here are some of the “achievements” I have done for this past year. Isn’t it pretty convenient how my birthday is at the end of the year and I can just combine this post with a neat recap?

**1\. I lost my virginity**

This should not be that much of a big deal, because virginity is such a socially inept concept, but to me it marked the fact that sexually I am neither a prude nor a weirdo. I enjoyed intimacy with other bodies and it felt liberating to be open to other people in such a different way that I have never done before, to be vulnerable and show different versions of myself I have never experimented with or experienced with anyone. All my teenage years I have been so reclusive and disconnected sexually, and now that I know what my body likes, I feel more confirming and confident in who I am as a person. I might have gone through some shit dealing with people while hooking up, but it’s just part of the game everyone has to deal with. Even I am to blame for it sometimes, so who am I to victimize myself?

**2\. I had a car trip for the first time**

This September I drove my car for more than eight hours across six states, from New Jersey to Ohio, to attend a concert with my friend. Weird thing, I know, like who the heck would drive that far just for one concert and back? But it did happen, and I’m glad it happened. In specific ways it marked a newfound maturity for me, some kind of cultural achievement that people frequently talk about in those young adult, contemporary, coming-of-age movies. I have risked myself not buying car tow insurance for the car if something happened while way overdue for an oil change, but nothing happened, and so I was super lucky I managed to do it. On the way back it rained heavily, and so I was also lucky that I made it home that night too, despite being way way later than expected. For someone who, over a year ago, did not know how to properly drive despite having a driver’s license for over two years, I call it a grand feat. Especially so if you look at my car. Not like it looks that bad, but it’s definitely not one you would feel comfortable driving for long periods of time.

**3\. I switched my major**

When I graduated at my alma mater UW-Seattle last year, I knew right then and there that somehow, it was not right. My degree was somewhat useless in that I did not see what I could do with it except for pursuing even higher education, so I did what I needed to do: I took a year off, worked in manual labor, appreciated life more as a student, and then I made the switch and went back to school. I got a good scholarship for this accounting undergraduate program which paid for about a half of my tuition, so I decided to take a second bachelor’s instead of a master’s, which I already know will cost much much more. I am not going to lie and say that it’s a perfect decision, because sometimes I wonder if I did the right thing and not take the leap to get a more advanced degree so I can stand out more in this already bloodbath of a job market, but if it isn’t for it, I would not have gotten this internship where I’m typing these words down at right now (which of course I got through connections). As far as what I’m seeing, I enjoy these classes more than I did with those stats-heavy classes.

**4\. I’m healthy**

The truth is, everyone should be grateful that they are alive and healthy. Not everybody has to thrive everyday to believe that their lives are worth living for. I don’t know what my own has in store for me in the future, but all I know is that I have dealt with so many crushed dreams and disappointments to know that whatever is going to happen, I will surpass it, definitely not with flying colors but with gratitude, and come out more experienced and grounded than ever. I should be grateful that in these cold days I have somewhere warm to stay in and enjoy my days cozying in the bed and have a job to do and have something to look forward to. Life being an international student sucks, I know, but I also do sometimes appreciate it for what it has given me throughout these past five years: learning opportunities, growth, and a self-reliance spirit that no one can ever take away from me. I could have been a very different person if I have stayed in Vietnam, probably less mentally ill and seeing the world with more rose-colored glasses, but probably more naïve and less experienced. Sometimes, I wondered if my parents sent me here so that I would figure out what it feels like to be out and sexually liberated. I'm not that dared to ask, but if that is the case, then I'm grateful to be alive here.


Here’s to a better twenty-second year on this planet (despite the whole shitshow going on)!
