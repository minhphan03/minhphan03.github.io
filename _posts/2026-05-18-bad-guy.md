<h3>Part I: Bad Guy</h3>

When a guy I was casually texting sent me the final message, a whole mini-essay explaining why he would not want to see me anymore, it was then that it finally dawned on me that, perhaps, I was the bad guy in a story. This "enlightenment" did not come easily. I forced myself to swallow my anger and swept it aside into the back of my mind to focus on driving when that message popped up on my phone screen. It was a long story, not long enough to make it into some drama that verifies my karma, but damaging enough to keep me awake till this time, writing down these words.

A thirty-something-year-old guy, I hooked up with him probably three to four times. Of course, it was never anything more than that. We were just sexual beings, and in heat, and like many homosexuals who were in heat, we found each other on Grindr. I was not even remotely physically attracted to him. We just wanted to use our bodies. But I guess he did. The first time we texted, he offered to pay for my ride to his house, even though I said it was very far from where I was. Jersey was comparatively small, but a ride of an hour would already tire me out before I could get down to business. Nobody ever did it [compensate a hookup for driving] for me before, so I felt torn and agreed. And time again and time after that. He got my number after that, and out of courtesy, I gave it to him. He kept texting, asking to meet again, and honestly, when I was in heat, I was so tempted to travel all that distance. I did give in twice, and for the others, I did not want to say no outright; I told him I was busy with school and stuff. It got to a point when sometime in February I responded back to him, saying that I would not want to hook up multiple times as fuck buddies but rather it be an exclusive relationship. For a moment there, I thought I could scare him away for good. Turned out he was fine with it, so I was compelled to continue the texting. The truth is, as a people pleaser, I never wish to be the bad guy who goes into complete radio silence after some chats out of miscommunication, repeating countless stories I have encountered in the past. Funny thing, though, is he also went radio silent on me for about a month after I popped that question. He eventually responded, so who am I to be angry at someone who knew his way to come around? Unfortunately and unbeknownst to this unlucky guy, there I was, directing a course of leading him into an illusion, out of pity and cowardice, that there was a non-zero chance he could develop a relationship so he could have sex regularly with me.

It marked the beginning of a talking stage, a talking stage that grew out of a hookup situation where we knew each other's bodies more than our names and occupations. We texted back and forth a few times a day in what I call a mutual texting development. It was more than dragged-out late responses after three hours when we only stuck to dick appointments. He would begin the day by asking how I slept, and I would respond that I slept well. He would then ask what I would do, and I would respond to him about my agenda for the day. Nothing for his schedule, for we, after our first day of mutual texting, both knew that he works every day at a car-washing and lubing shop (emphasis on every day as he works seven days a week), so it was pretty much pointless to ask about his day. I mean, would you ask your boyfriend what you do each day if you would receive the same boring one-liner response every time? So I asked him about mundane topics, like his family, his life in general, and what he ate every day. That's how I eventually insisted on eating salad the next time we met, as he time and time again revealed himself to be a fast food binge-eater — for pizzas, burgers, and mac n cheese were among the common dishes he told me he would have for dinner. I, in turn, revealed to him a little bit about my plans in California, where I was taking my spring break, along with some trite details about my relationship ideals and where I was at in my life. He also elaborated on his four-week silence before we reconnected, and I found out he was married to a guy before breaking up four years ago. During that time, I also called him once, and we talked about my situation: whether, as an international student, I would bother him. I had long considered this a major dealbreaker for many people, as I never wanted anyone to entertain the idea that I was only with him for my own benefit. Which, as much as I wish to deny, has been on my mind for a very long time. He said no, so we continued our plain communication. We planned to see each other again when I came back to Jersey.

It went on like that for about two weeks, throughout my spring break stay at my aunties' place in the sunny South Bay Area, a six-hour flight from his place in Jersey. 

When I came back on a Tuesday, we ended up meeting on Friday of the same week, and I would stay overnight. He drove me halfway from the mall where I parked my car on his way back from work, to his house (again, since it was way too far), then to the mall where we bought dinner, and finally back to his place. We ate a little bit, then watched an action movie about some ballerinas fighting against the bad guys. Sex came next. I knew from our past times that there was only pure physical with no affection whatsoever, and this time it was no different. My heart felt cold as always, as it never planned to cooperate or put any effort into the practice. My body was there to take all of his sexual frustrations, all in stride. As soon as he released and came down from his high, I knew this was never going to work. If faking my heart wretches me once, I won't be able to grasp the magnitude of the aftermath that his broken heart will bear once the truth comes clean. For then I would certainly be the bad guy. So I swallowed it inside and focused on the moment we were enjoying. Even if we never saw each other again, I could have gone home patting myself that I had given him some undeniably great pleasure. With that in mind, I gave him some sloppy oral so we could go for round two, since he promised to do so to me in our texts. He delivered. 

All good things must come to an end, or at least that's what people say. What do people who had nothing in common, who did not have any chemistry together, even do when the sole activity that connected them together finishes? I laid on his bed, spent and spread out, mentally still aching and yearning for that true boneless satisfaction. It was only 8pm, and we still had the whole night, but we had already run out of things to do. I never thought that our chemistry was that nonexistent. I dreaded having small talk. He was awfully quiet, focusing on the movie with its distant white noise reeling from the television. We were not even cuddling. I wrapped my arms around my naked self, aimlessly letting my eyes wander between the ceiling fan and the movie itself while my mind was playing 5D chess, trying to come up with what to do next and wondering if he was feeling awkward, too. Coward me could not even tempt myself up to wrap my arms and cuddle with him, so who was I to blame him for acknowledging the tension and refusing to do the same?

Being the party pooper that I am, of course, I had to make it descend into a full-blown disaster by giving in to the urge to open the hookup apps. Was I cheating? Obviously, no, since we were not exclusive (yet). If he said he was open to dating, that would not mean we were automatically in a relationship. There is a clear distinction between them, and I'm willing to die on that hill. Anyways, despite having already blocked him on Grindr, he found out I was checking out people on there and on Sniffies too (how he found out I still don't know, but I guess I never checked whether he had an anonymous profile) while lying next to him. He laid all of that insight off his chest, out in that whole mini-essay of a message I mentioned at the start of this account. That my ultimate decision to take an Uber home after complaining about the prices AND turning down his offer to take me back hurt him, and he was disappointed that I could even focus on the movie, which we initially planned to do. I guess it was too much for him (as it should), and was the final nail in the coffin of his affection for me. Who could forgive another after such a blow to his trust and honest heart? I finally "scared" him off for good, as I wanted months ago. I just never thought it would end up being this way. I mean, how could he not be disappointed? He was so sweet and never deserved to be dealt such a rotten blow to his self-esteem.

With that being said and done, would it be ridiculous to say I was not one hundred percent hell-bent on saying "no" to a relationship with him? Sure, physically, he was not my type at all, but I believed then that his personality could somehow make up for it. I was dead wrong. I'm so ashamed to repeat my mistake, because I already once tried to delude myself into liking a guy who had a sweet personality, but a lot of his habits were on my non-negotiable (turn-off) list. By saying that, I never meant that they were bad people; they were just not for me. People can be bad guys in ways and frames they never imagined themselves to be judged, or even meant to be. It happens as frequently as dirt. ~~Oedipus unknowingly killed his father and married his mother.~~ Anyway, why does it even matter? The foundation of our connection was shaky and baseless already: he was a safety net in case I could string along when I could not get attention on the apps. I was glad he was brave and smart enough to call out my bullshit, because I could never summon the courage to break his heart. When he ripped that Band-Aid off, I was seething out of the blow to my self-esteem on the surface, but deep down I was also relieved and thankful. The initial madness that burst from the inherent, reflex response to criticism gave way to a guilt released and finally tied up with a cute bow of thankfulness for him to teach me a lesson I'd never forget. What was done was done and irreversible. I could have shown remorse and apologized, but in this case, it wouldn't really matter, right? He would not hold any grudge against me, and I against him, as time will heal all wounds and numb all negative feelings. We'll feel lighter, and eventually we'll become strangers again.

<h3>Part II: Love Bombs</h3>

He who never had a taste of authentic romantic love. He who never had known what mutual affection is like. He who fell so foolishly for love-bombing. He who thought he deserved it because of karma. He who was putting “The Smallest Man Who Ever Lived” on blast. He who though “Foolish One” was the only song that could exactly describe him at the moment.

He was still wondering if all those sweet smooth words were even authentic. He was still in disbelief, consumed by embarrassment and shame of falling too fast for someone who was never serious with him to begin with. He went on a date that was so wonderful that he thought he saw stars and a future with the other person, but it turned out to be a dud. He wondered if he could ever believe in love again, but at the same time he is actively fighting to get over it. He knew the first heartbreak would always be the most devastating, but as he went through it with the thought of dying and vengeance there was a kindle of strength and growth somewhere. But how could he? It was the first time he had been desperately vulnerable like that with someone not in his bloodline, and what did the other guy do? Ran away like he just witness a monster, which was exactly how he felt like at that moment. To feel ugly in his own skin was one notion he had familiarized himself to so long, but at that time, it worsened tenfold, gnawing at him and waiting for him to come undone and crumble under its pressure. This had to be karma. Bereft of sanity, he entered the stages of grief. Why did he do it? Why did he have to bare himself so willingly and so early in this “texting relationship” that ended up bamboozled him, and daydream about having a future with a guy he had barely scratched the surface of? All of this beguiling and coddling for some fictional one-sided limerence?

Desperate for a way out, he called his friend from Vietnam. He gave her a rant, pouring all his scattered and torn-up feelings to the one he knew he could trust the most. She took it all in, and gently gave him insightful advice that he was so thankful at the end of the call he wished he could have flown over there and gave her the tightest hug ever known to men on Earth. She saved his life without fully realizing it. Not that he was heavily suicidal, which he likely was when the break-up message first caught his eyes; it was just, she successfully made him get out of his bed and pull away from the negativity consuming all his morning energy, which all but depleted despite freshly woken up from a deep slumber. He cooked lunch, finished the dried pineapple chunks in the fridge, and put on some progress on his long-awaiting book. Still then, he could not take his mind off of the fact that it all ended. Every distraction was just to remind him that he was actively avoiding it. The concept of renunciation blared in his brain, reminding him to put everything aside and honestly sit back and face with the raw emotions.

So he sat still then, mulling over the countless texts and conversations with that other guy. The wheels turning in his head, busy reasoning and rationalizing where it all went wrong and what he could have done to fix it all again. He tried to cry, because people said crying would make heartbroken people feel lighter, but no tears fell out. He was so used to bottling his emotions inside. He just weeped mentally. Between intermittent sobs, he wondered that if magic truly existed, maybe he could rewind back to the weeks before and tried to his best knowledge to undo all that went wrong, to prevent oversharing and embarrassing himself in front of a stranger. Or else, skip these grievous weeks when he knew he could think of nothing else but this incidence and this point in time, so that he could rescue himself from utmost despair. From the days of dying inside every day while faking his outward emotions, pretending that he was happy and content on his countenance and hope his facial features could not give away the messy state that he was drowning in. 

He thought about it for some time in the kitchen, and decided that it was ultimately better to skip instead of rewind time. He wanted to look back with relief that this truth was revealed sooner than later. He shuddered to imagine if this “thing” continued for weeks or months, there will be valuable time he wish he could take back. If it was already doomed from the start, why bother fixing? A broken heart could never be healed or be made whole again — it could only grow into a different shape, one that he hope for the best that another person could appreciate it enough for it to open for love again. He knew that, to survive in this battle called life, he had to navigate through webs of lies and kiss some frogs before meeting his dream Prince Charming. He learned to always keep his heart open, but never be broken by the same hand twice. That there was always a chance that the person whom he opened his heart to, to be vulnerable for, could one day leave him. That pain was, is, and will always be a part of life, and one must learn endure and triumph over it if he wishes to live life to the fullest. He could be sensitive and not be shamed for it, because it was in his nature and it made him who he was then. He could be dramatic, and honest, and be his authentic himself again, maybe one day, because he knew his value was way higher than these cheap love-bombs. He will eventually survive others. He must believe so.
