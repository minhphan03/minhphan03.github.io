*Written on the day I turn 20...*

As of writing this, I am still 19. I’m quite thankful that I was born in December because I still get to enjoy the "teenage" era until the very end of this year. In my mind, there’s a whole world of difference between 19 and 20; I don’t know why. I justify it because 20 starts with 2 compared to 18 and 19, which start with 1. I was given ten years to get used to that. But 20 is different. The older I get, the more expectations I have to bear and take on. Birthdays become dreadful when they're supposed to be a time for celebration. I am not depressed enough to step a little bit closer to a plan to kill myself, but don’t you think time is just a very cruel thing? I swear some days I don’t even notice it. 365 days are long enough to give me some room to breathe and ignore the fact that I am getting older every day just like hundreds of millions of people who are my age. Many of them are achieving something extraordinary for their age, and I find bliss in ignorance as they serve nothing but reminding me how time is running out for me to make something worthwhile out of my life.

I’m in the midst of writing an opinion piece on the movie "Soul" (2020), which will be published next year, and it called me out on my adamant, dogmatic perspective on life: I never spend time just ENJOYING life. I’m tired of treating everything as a competition, that everything has to have meaning, and I will be a failure if I don’t wake up every day and find a purpose to push myself forward. Forward to what? I don’t even know. If I were to find a purpose in my life, I would have said something like “to be a good son to my parents and a good citizen in my community.” The idea of satisfaction in life in the realm of self hasn’t been in my subconsciousness for so long I forgot what it feels like to just live. Living is different from existing, I know that all too well. Every day I wake up and find a purpose to live and not exist. In "Coco" (2017), the ghosts only finally succumb to oblivion once the last person on Earth who knows them passes away. I think about that idea every day. Am I making an impact good enough that when I pass away I would not be forgotten immediately? Man, all this existential crisis is surely grossly amplified today. I wish to recuperate from it as soon as possible, but it’s probably not going to happen until I am 60 and finally assimilate the reality that there’s nothing worth trying anymore, and I should belatedly accept all the karma and consequences of my actions and decisions.

When I wake up tomorrow at 8 am, I’ll be exactly 20. In the Vietnamese (lunar) calendar, you’re one year older when the new year arrives (Lunar New Year, of course), so I’m already 20 in my family’s eyes. I desperately cling to the Gregorian calendar instead because I refuse to acknowledge that notion. It’s not a rash idea. You see, it’s been there for years, way before I made my first birthday blog three years ago at 17. It instilled and settled in myself a universal truth that it’s not that I am going to be 20 but probably 21 and 22 and before I can register it, I’ll be 30. Such crazy numbers, don’t you think? An existential line of thinking: man, how can people endure the fact that they are 30 years old already and there’s still many more things to achieve in their lives? I’m not even 20 yet, and I’m already thinking that far. I doubt I would go back here and read these words when I turn that age, but those words I said at 17 and 18 still ring true to this day, and I suddenly realize that a lot of ideas I have, while have changed substantially over the years, still undeniably very simplistic and childlike (juvenile?) even. I still yearn for a simple life, a significant other, a home, a stability that will take me years to have. These dreams intensify as I acknowledge the fact that the road in front of me is getting rougher. I cannot disclose these plans out loud yet, partly because I don’t want to jinx it, but this terrible anticipation is keeping me on my toes. I know that only through disadvantages and woes can people thrive, but I’m so tired of everything already. How much longer do I have to try, really? It’s been four years since my 16th birthday when I got a first bitter taste of true suffering and things haven’t changed one bit. My 20-year-old self tomorrow is going to keep hanging in there until the sky gets clearer again, and damn do I hope it happens quickly.

Tomorrow then. Enough for today. I need to enjoy the last tidbits of my 19th year on Earth as much as possible…

Minh.
