I finished Never Have I Ever season 2 about a week ago and I must say that it remains one of the best shows I’ve ever watched. At first I was drawn into it by the teen drama, but I knew deep down later that what stuck were the emotional, educational values that the characters learned throughout the episodes. 

The relationship between Devi and her dad was precious and pure. I guess you could say so about any family show, but the fact that her dad was not physically but always mentally supportive of her made me shed a few tears. Devi was “crazy” because of her dad showed that how much impact he had on her. The dialogues, the actions, the relationships between her and her family, after everything, all revolved around the devastating tragedy of the Vishwakumar family. It left room for different stages of grief, hope and perseverance to flow through the image of an antsy, confused and emotional teenager. I could emphasize the baggage that Devi herself felt when she dropped her phone into the water and lost her precious dad’s voicemail. It was rare to find that tight relationship anywhere these days.

There were so many times I had to face-palm myself when Devi did something embarrassing, such as the wacky apology dance stunt to Anessa, the love triangle and her indecisiveness, or the stalking at her mom’s date. I could not understand what was going on in her head, but after some reflection about myself as a teen, I think that’s totally plausible. As teenagers we all act like that sometimes. The love triangle was because of her desperation of a boyfriend. The outburst at her mom’s date was because of her lingering of dad. And the apology was nothing but a desperate act with good intentions. She had wholesome reasons or saving face attempts to do what she did. They were just poorly executed, and so she was the one to be blamed on the consequences (honestly, she deserved it). Obviously no one could understand her actions because as teenagers, we are too shallow to mind anyone else. We all just wanted cookies to bite and drama to talk about. If not from her actions we could not have these conversations about how in the world could they go from a vow to help friends in need to the said friend break fallout in merely 10 minutes (on movie duration, of course).

Mindy Kaling and Lang Fisher were so thoughtful and detail-oriented throughout the details of this series. I admit that I’m not a film enthusiast, but in my wildest dreams I could not see how forced marriage, male privilege and K-Pop be represented through one character like Kamala. I’m not comparing this series with other any teenage drama series, but anyone who says that it is trash should look deeper under a drama series. Like I mentioned earlier, this is a story about family, coping with reality and mental health (a very coming-of-age detail), and the power of good friends. I must admit that many of the film details are so cliche and ideal (like the school settings: why is it so beautiful?) but we as viewers should look beyond these superficial matters. The series itself is a very entertaining product of Hollywood that serves the rom-com addicts well, but it’s also a very educational program, don’t you think? The diversity, the lessons (the history of Japan internment camps in the last episode of season 2), the culture (a peek into the life of an Indian family), the behaviors and thinking of a teenager (that I could somehow resonate too despite not living in the suburbs of LA), and the life of the narrators as well (John McEnroe, the Stanford alumni…) all gave me crumbs to ponder over. It’s already rare to see a POC-dominant series, let alone a viral one without embarrassing gimmicks to fish for views. Lured audiences by the teenage angst and drama but kept them by the meaningful stories behind, I confidently protect my opinion that this series is one of the best ones on Netflix (as a sucker for drama, maybe on par to Money Heist or Sex Education) that you and your family (if they’re liberal enough) can enjoy and learn more about relationship dynamics.

I’m still of the opinion that many times the characters’ reactions are kind of unrealistic and overproduced. But honestly, if they are not then it’s not a glorified Hollywood product. Big houses, cool parties, bright school halls, thinking that having two boyfriends is cool and creating all those chaos without remorse? Only in your dreams girl. Reality would smash you to death.
