**20\. Dead Man - Alessia Cara**

Alessia Cara may not be one of my go-tos when I want to explore new music, but for some reason when she released the lead single for her upcoming album *Love & Hyperbole*, I was intrigued and tuned in. It did not disappoint me. I love the jazzy influences on this song, which I feel is (or isn’t) slowly creeping back into the mainstream scene (Lady Gaga with *Harlequin*, for example, or RAYE with *My 21st Century Blues*). Some reviewers say that the song gives “Amy Winehouse-esque” vibes and I figure that’s kind of true. I love a good jazz influence executed delightfully, something like an instrumental outro preferably. Honestly that never goes wrong.

Fav lyrics: "*If you're really there / Then why can I walk right through ya? / Talkin' to a dead man*"

**19\. Stealin’ Love - Leigh-Anne** 

When Little Mix took a hiatus in 2021, a big deal that many Mixers (Little Mix’s fans) considered is how each member would venture their solo careers. Leigh-Anne, who I regarded as an overlooked gem, shined in her own way with her attempts. Her debut single “Don’t Say Love” was  my favorites out of all three member’s, and “My Love” featuring Arya Starr was a great follow-up. But “Stealin’ Love” was on another level: it was stunning, groovy (yes, I just LOVE this word), and representative of her personality. We all know Leigh-Anne’s vocals suits R&B so well, and this song shines because of those. Also, did I mention how genius it is that the song title sounds like “Still In Love”? 

Fav lyrics: "*How did we get so sentimental from the get-go? / Full of potential, tryin' to hold on / But you wanna let go / Out of the backdoor into the arms of / The one thing you're runnin' from (*From*)*"

**18\. Let me go OH OH - Tove Lo & SG Lewis** 

Tove Lo never misses with her club bangers, and this time, it’s not an exception. Her new EP *HEAT* released this pride month in collaboration with SG Lewis is packed with sweaty jams that could be played in any gay bar (and I’ve never been to one, but I assume so). “Let me go OH OH” is slow, seductive, and groovy, and can make anyone blush just like how a raunchy poppy beat at a sketchy undercover rave club assault you when you first step in. Who cares, honestly? Just gimme an earworm-y electronic groovy tune so I can dance in the club and forget everything please.

Fav lyrics: "*Rush to my heart when you spread me apart like that / Hands moving on with thе beat, yeah, I like it fast*"

**17\. Diet Pepsi - Addison Rae**

If you go and read the comment section in the music video on Addison Rae’s YouTube channel, you should see the overwhelmingly positive reception to her transformation from a TikTok influencer as how good this song turned out to be. I have no comment because I do not use TikTok, but I do agree wholeheartedly that this song is catchy and excellent. Her vocals improved substantially this time as she learned to lean in her strengths, at least compared to her debut single “Obsessed” (breathy, but maybe that’s what make the song sounds good?). However, the star of the show is clearly the writing and production, which make it sound way more catchy, lush, and addicting this time around. Someone pointed out this song sounds like something from Britney Spears’ *In The Zone* and Lana Del Rey’s *Born To Die*, and given how she overtly expressed her admiration for both artists, I’m not surprised. The lyrics are definitely inspired by Lana, though, and it’s clearly so. But inspiration is never a bad thing, as long as it’s not straight copying.

Fav lyrics: "*Sitting on his lap, sippin' Diet Pepsi / I write my name with lipstick on your chest / I leave a mark so you know I'm the best*"

**16\. SLIDE - Doechii**

I’m not a big fan of hip-hop, but I absolutely love a melodic rap song done justice. "SLIDE" is exactly just that: it reminded me of a fusion of Tinashe and early Nicki Minaj’s early pop-rap days (aka her *Pink Friday* era). R&B shines with Doechii’s vocals, and I will never get tired of a good synth tune. Her vocals are airy, floaty, and super chill (I know, what an overused word!), which made the song felt super smooth, sliding like butter on a hot pan. Her lyrical abilities shine so brightly in this. I never listened to Doechii before her collaboration with Katy Perry on “I’M HIS HE’S MINE” (that song is more forgettable, of course), but thanks Ms. Perry again for introducing me to her. She's currently my favorite contender for the "Best New Artist" award at the upcoming GRAMMYs, so if you want to get to know her more, check out her currently viral song, "DENIAL IS A RIVER". It's also so fire 🔥!!

Fav lyrics: "*I slip, you slide, I whip, you drive / You lie, I trip, we fight / I seal, you sell, I buy, drip, life / Taking me out for the ride*"

**15\. You Go Your Way - Perrie**

As a part of Little Mix, Perrie’s powerful vocal range has been proven many times. Yet her solo journey barely showcased that. Both “Forget About Us” and “Tears” were unfortunately somewhat forgettable. That changed with “You Go Your Way”: the 80’s groovy melodies are so tasty, albeit the lyrics are underwhelming (the repetitive “You go your way, I go my way” makes zero sense). That’s all right to me, though, as long as the tune hits… Aren’t songs hitting way better with catchy tunes? That bass line and electric guitar elevates the song immensely. Combined with her vocals, that last chorus is simply euphoric. 

Fav lyrics: "*Go ahead and get over me, hey / 'Cause I'll be smiling / When we look back in five years' time*"

**14\. LEVII’S JEANS - Beyoncé & Post Malone**

I’ll be honest with you: Beyoncé’s new album is not my cup of tea. I feel like it’s too bloated to my taste, and the only reason I checked it out was because of *RENAISSANCE* being so perfectly executed that tuned me in. I’m only a casual listener, so I wasn’t willing to take extended time to go through the whole album again; however, for the ones I like, I really dig them. One that stood out to me is "LEVII’S JEANS," a collaboration with Post Malone, who also happened to release a country album this year. I loved the sultry melodies, the harmonized vocals that they gave. Given that this is closer to what a typical country song on the charts sounds like nowadays, it’s easier to listen and more vibe-y than the others on the album. I especially dig Beyoncé’s vocals in the song: it is pure honey, especially when she dragged out the words in the verses. Something about those is giving country vibes, if you get it. Maybe her accent? Country is not my typical genre, so don’t judge me on this.

Fav lyrics: "*Ooh, I love you, baby, yeah, you drive me crazy / Need you all night long, you're my Renaissance / Baby, lovin' you, that's all I see / In this crazy world, you're the best of things*"

**13\. BLUE - Billie Eilish**

The first part of this song is the best part of *HIT ME HARD AND SOFT*, period. Then the transition came and then… it became less great. But still better than anything on the rest of the album, with "WILDFLOWER" being a close second. I’m not a big fan of transitions, honestly, as they tend to be hit-or-misses. "Happier Than Ever" (the song) was a hit in my bedroom, while "L’AMOUR DE MA VIE" is not. Melodically, this song is great and complex in a good way. Strings, drums, electronic, piano, you name it, once combined using the composition style that makes Billie and Finneas Eilish one of the best contemporary duo today, became something so great and addicting and fresh that made me come back for more. I especially love the use of underwater/echoing vocal effects as it ties the song to the album theme (the artwork at least), and created a beautiful atmospheric soundscape.

Fav lyrics: "*I try to live in black and white, but I'm so blue / I'd like to mean it when I say I'm over you / But that's still not true (Blue) / And I'm still so blue, oh*"

**12\. WONDER - Katy Perry**

Katy Perry’s new album may have been a dud, but among all the new songs there is a hidden gem that I revisit very often. “WONDER,” the closing track of the standard version, is uplifting, celebratory, and EDM in its highest spirit. The lyrics are positive and fits right in Perry’s lane of brainless, or even cynically, fanatically fun pop. A celebration of life and maturity and a self-empowering anthem at its core, the song features her signature loud and powerful vocals, which reminded me of her earlier eras compared to other songs in the album like “CRUSH” or “I’M HIS, HE’S MINE.” “LIFETIMES” and “WOMAN’S WORLD,” the first two singles of the era, are also purely upbeat songs for the gays and the clubs, but the production on those is just… let's just say repetitive, dated, and forgettable. 

Fav lyrics: "*Can somebody promise me (promise me) / Our innocence doesn't get lost in a cynical world?*"

**11\. Claw Machine - Sloppy Jane featuring Phoebe Bridgers**

The first time I came across this chamber pop song while watching "I Saw The TV Glow" on a Wednesday after class, I knew I fell in love with it. Dreamy, moody, and atmospherically apt for a movie as hallucinating and surreal as it was, I realized how powerful a single piano could replicate any soundscape. It was peaceful, it was resigned, and when the strings and drums kicked in, suddenly the vibes shifted to restlessness, uneasiness, and directionless. Like a claw machine player that tends to lose more than to catch a prize, dreams and hopes rarely actualizes. Being a part of a movie that uses metaphors to allude transgender lives and struggles, the lyrics are deep and heartbreaking, and Phoebe Bridgers and Haley Dahl’s harmonious vocals did the song spectacular justice.

Fav lyrics: "*I think I was born bored / I think I was born blue / I think I was born wanting more / I think I was born already missing you*"

**9\. Thirsty - Tinashe**

Tinashe always knows what she does best, and that’s R&B. A little bit experiment here and there, but R&B is hers and hers only. For *BB/Ang3l*, we detect a mixture of electronic, synthwave-vibey elements that back up the world building of the album theme itself, *Quantum Baby* continues with the use of distortion elements, those that represents and recreates the ambient soundscape of disruption, change, and wickedness underlying a somewhat lowkey, smooth surface. It’s like quantum physics, you know? Drastically unalike classical physics, yet existing right under our noses. “Thirsty” plays into its role as an album track, chill yet playful; sexy yet confident and just what you would expected from an artist like Tinashe. Someone said on Twitter that once you listen to her enough, you can identify her style right away, even if R&B has so many artists filling up the scene over the years. That’s so true in this case.

Fav lyrics: "*So thirsty, chase me when I leave / There's no such thing as too much of a good thing / I just missed my flight, I guess I'm spending the night (Guess I'm spending the night)*"

**7\. Lonely is the Muse - Halsey**

Female rage is always a sight to behold, and in this case, a symphony to rock to. Not a lot of female artists managed to express it in their songs, and Halsey is one who did. We got “Nightmare,” a punk-alternative-rock standalone single about feminist subversion and rebuttal of archetypes; “Castle,” the first track on her debut album *Badlands*, relays her desire to take over the rigid, set patriarchal imaginary kingdom that was a setting of the album theme. In her newest album, “Lonely Is The Muse” stood out among the most upbeat ones, and also the angriest ones. In this track, Halsey sang about her feeling used in the music industry, by the men that she inspired to write songs about, exploiting her image for the sake of selling albums and gathering buzz. It is angry, it is so punk-rock, and it is so cathartic. One of the best songs of the album, and probably her discography as well.

Fav lyrics: "*And I mined a couple diamonds / From the stories in my head / But I'm reduced to just a body / Here in someone else's bed*"

**6\. The Black Dog - Taylor Swift**

When Swift said that songwriting was her lifeline during her turbulent relationship drama in early 2023, she meant it. Among the songs that showcased this catharsis and desperation of one, it’s “The Black Dog.” The way she screamed the word “screaming” in the first chorus always blew my mind out, and I can’t forget the first time I heard it with my friends at our listening hangout. What initially thought to be just another piano ballad interweaved tightly with strong drum-filled explosions of instrumentals throughout the song, and it represented her emotions so well: in the space of frustration and bafflement, a result many often catch themselves out of a fresh rebound relationship, Swift used her poignant songwriting skills to self-write this masterpiece that encapsulates perfectly how tumultuous life can turn out to be and how in your wildest dreams your thought-to-be-wise mind can still be blinded by love and lust.

Fav lyrics: "*I move through the world with the heartbroken / My longings stay unspoken / And I may never open up thе way I did for you*"

**5\. Good Luck, Babe! - Chappell Roan**

If you happen to read some of the year-end songs ranked lists by the publications this year, there’s a chance that you may come across this song on so many of them. There’s no denying it: this song is a certified banger. Chappell’s vocals shine beautifully here and this song is made just for her (she knew her vocal capabilities while writing it, I can guarantee). A standard break-up song/trashing-your-ex anthem, “Good Luck, Babe!” highlights the beauty of freedom from a toxic relationship, and Dan Nigro’s masterful production did the song so much justice. It is vibrant, it is fun, and it is unique in terms of production. It sounds very 80s or 70s, but simply distinctive from 80s synth pop you normally hear on the radio. I happened to read a review while writing this and the author denoted some Kate Bush inspiration, and while I only listened to only one Bush song I think the vocals are somewhat comparable. Her vocal riffs and falsettos (maybe?) are very theatrical, especially in the post-chorus where she repeated the song title. I think that’s why? Anyways, pure eargasm. 

Fav lyrics: "*You'd have to stop the world just to stop the feeling / Good luck, babe (well, good luck), well, good luck, babe (well, good luck)*"

**4\. yes, and? - Ariana Grande** 

No matter what people say, I am a firm believer that "yes, and?" totally deserved its lead single treatment of Ariana Grande’s latest studio album *eternal sunshine*. It may be deviated from what the rest of the album sounds like, but its catchy house-influenced tune is catchy, and a catchy song is always applaudable. The intro reverb is lovely, and the lyrics are… controversial which serves to buzz up the noise for her comeback. The echoed backing vocals (the “I keep”s) are just exquisite and chef’s kiss, once again proving Max Martin’s masterly production and Grande’s vocal layering skills. The result is a bop for the gays on the dance floors and one of the best no.1 Billboard Hot 100 singles I’ve heard this year. 

Fav lyrics: "*And if you find yourself in a dark situation / Just turn on your light and be like / 'Yes, and?'*"

**3\. Close To You - Gracie Abrams** 

This unreleased fan-favorite was finally made justice on Abrams’ second studio album. I may not be a fan, but I do like some songs I got to hear. “Close To You” may be the most recognizable out of them, because it’s so upbeat and danceable. It was released this summer as a promotional single and damn was it just constantly on repeat for a very long while. It was during this time that I drove a car regularly for the first time, and it was that song that I put on every time I got in my overheated cousin’s car under the 90 degrees heat in California. That electronic bass underlying like an undercurrent, seldomly interrupted by some repercussions and synths made way for a rush of serotonin that kicked in when the drums hit in the pre-chorus (and amplified during the chorus and post-chorus). It’s lovely what an acoustic-leaning artist like Abrams can do with this more upbeat production that showcased her potential pretty well. It’s kind of sad how she grew out of it, but at least we got a taste of what it could have been.

Fav lyrics: "*And I burn for you / And you don't even know my name / If you asked me to / I'd give you everything*"

**2\. loml - Taylor Swift** 

If there is one thing that remains true to this day, that is the fact that I am a sucker for a good ass tear-jerking ballad. What qualifies to be a good ass tear-jerking ballad remains a mystery, but all I know is “loml” was a strong standout for me the first time I heard it as a part of *The Tortured Poets Department: The Anthology*. A whooping 31 tracks, mostly piano ballads, and “loml” just had that punch to catch my attention right then. The lyrics are devastating (very normal of Swift to be able to write those), especially with the outro that got me replay countless times days after. Her vulnerably soft vocals shines through the song, and honestly since it’s a piano ballad, we automatically focus on the lyrics again. Songwriting has always been about LYRICS, and it rings true in this song. The way she chose words and composed the lyrics to match the melodies perfectly (“who’s gonna STOP US from waltzing back into reKINDLED flames?”) was an example of that: key words being highlighted at the right time make all the punches extra damaging.

Fav lyrics: "*Our field of dreams engulfed in fire / Your arson's match, your somber eyes / And I'll still see it until I die / You're the loss of my life*"

**1\. June Gloom - Camila Cabello**

I took a look at all my past number songs on my favorite annual song lists and I they all bore a memorable impact on my life at a certain point: “Holy (’Til You Let Me Go)” took me back to my nights drifting through the Saigon streets in the hot humid summer nights; “Just For You” was for the cool, bone-chilling school days in Seattle, “1121” was for my depressing days in my room just belting out the lyrics for no damn reason. For “June Gloom,” again a closing album track, it was for belting out in the car under the dry Californian heat. Along with “Close To You,” they were pretty much on heavy rotation this past summer. “June Gloom” also happens to be my most played song this year according to Apple Music, and I don’t think I can’t ever come across the term “does she ever get this wet for you baby” and not belt it aloud again. The distinctive yet cacophonous outro may be my most favorite music moment of this year: it’s so gnarly, so out of place, so unexpected comparing to the rest of the song, yet for Cabello and El Guincho it ties the song just oh so perfectly into the super underrated album that is *C,XOXO*, which I featured in the music section of this website. It screams disorganization and unorthodoxy in the best damn way, and I always stand by that opinion. Gorgeous production, OK lyrics (who cares honestly?), but the autotuned vocals in this one just HITS: utilizing an artificial vocal effect while baring her soul singing about a long-term romance coming to an end, isn’t it a profound dichotomy here ladies and gentlemen?
