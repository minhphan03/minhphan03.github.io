For many people in the United States, turning twenty-one is a big deal because, as soon as you do, you can start going to clubs and bars and drink alcohol legally. To them, that means unlocking a major part of socializing in their lives. Personally, it’s a nice thing to have for me. It isn’t like I have never been to a bar before because, in Vietnam and the majority of countries on this planet, the legal drinking age is eighteen. Me being in the U.S., where the enacted drinking age is strictly twenty-one, for the majority of my time is just something I have to endure for a while until I don’t know when.  

This time, I don’t think I have a lot to write about. Socially, I’m lowkey dead at this point because college ending means that I don’t have a lot of things in common with people around me anymore, and being an introvert does not make socializing easy. I’m basically on my own most of the time. Does it feel lonely? Yeah, sure, of course, but there’s only so many complaining arguments one can lament and whine about. I got used to it; I don’t mind. I think it’s just part of life that not everyone has to have an active social life, and if someone else is fortunate to have one, I take the other end of the bargain.  

I did not type those lines down to justify why I am better living in this bubble at the moment. I just don’t feel like socializing much, and being out in the open world lowkey scares me. I can feel my social anxiety creeping up at this very second, but a couple of days ago, I went to the mall for the first time in a very long time during the Thanksgiving weekend, and it was so crowded it freaked me out a little bit. I bought myself a fro-yo and went back to my car because I didn’t feel like shopping (and my wallet would thank me profusely for that), but I guess it was enough for socializing that day-blending myself in the rowdy crowds for a couple of hours before I retreated back to my cocoon shell.  

It wasn’t like I did not attempt to try at all, but it gives me the ick sometimes how my efforts are fruitless: I used Hinge for the first time and did not match with anyone (am I too ugly for dating?), Grindr (well, the conversations went nowhere, and I’m still a virgin), and signed up for this water polo class but chickened out at the last minute. Fortunately, I met quite a few people at this tennis class every Sunday and promised myself I would come back to another kayak polo meetup where I’ve talked to some nice guys. I guess that’s it? Until I officially meet my coworkers, I’ll see how my circle ends up—for the worse or the better.

At this age of twenty-one, which in last year’s anniversary post I emphasized how meaningful this year was going to be, I ended up not seeing much of a significant change in anything, except for the fact that I moved from the West Coast to the East Coast for the first time for a first job, which led to a lot of changes in my life (see above). As much as I had a first job out of school, it is not really what I want to do for the rest of my life, so I’m going back to school, and hopefully, this time, I can figure something out for the sake of my career. These past four years were fun and memorable, but goddamn did I gaslight myself into thinking that I can pursue and be content working in the tech/data field. We’ll see how it goes next year, so I’m bookmarking this as I write… maybe I’ll have the guts to check this one out again when it comes.  

I don’t see why I should write a lot about turning twenty-one. It’s just another number, another year in this life. Ever since last year, I find myself losing interest in my birthday because I don’t have anyone to celebrate with anymore. Birthday parties are cumbersome, and I hate attention. So December 6th becomes another day in the countless days of waking up, fighting some demons and battles, and going to bed every day until they get a little bit less scary. Hopefully, by then, I can finally breathe normally. These twenty-somethings are nasty, huh? Full of competition, full of peer pressure, and lost more than ever.  

Sorry for being too negative. I have this habit of tending to assume the worst in things, and as a result, it makes me cynical. Keeping my expectations grounded that way is crucial because I am an overthinker and a planner, and this is how I have to survive to avoid getting myself into trouble. I admit I am living in fear: fear of bankruptcy, fear of losing myself, fear of rejection. It’s hanging by a thread, walking on glass, etc. Therefore, I try my best to not get involved in troublesome circumstances. If another eventful happenstance ever occurs to me, it’s probably fate. I caused two car accidents this past year alone, for example.  

Alright, what are some of the good moments this past year…?  

- **Going to the Charli XCX/Troye Sivan concert**: Sounds trivial, but it’s been a while since I’ve been to a concert, and I flew to Detroit to attend the Sweat Tour with my high school friend. They are both some of my most-listened artists, so this is a one-stone-hit-three-birds scenario: I visited downtown Detroit (briefly) and listened to two artists on my concert bucket list all in one setting. You know how expensive concerts are becoming these days, so better invest in them when I can. 

- **Having a full-time job**: It’s not an ideal job because I have to wake up at six, and the work is more labor-intensive than office white-collar, but for the first time in my life, I’m financially independent from my parents. The pay is not terrible for a first-timer, but, you know, more money is always appreciated. They’ll definitely have to fund me next year when I get back to school, but it’s nice not to feel guilty spending their money at least for a while. I’ll bet the re-transition will be rough, but when it comes, it will come. There’s nothing I can do about it.  

- **Buying my first car**: Technically my dad’s money, but I got a grey Mazda3, a lovely compact sedan. It’s been serving me well with no trouble so far, but I did replace the brake motors for quite a hefty price. At least I did it before the wheels went rogue and my worst nightmare of getting stranded on the streets came true.  

- **Learning tennis**: This is a big thing on my bucket list, honestly. Such a cool sport. I’m still a beginner more than anything, but I really enjoy it. One of my best decisions this year was to attend free tennis classes and casually play with some people I’ve met.  

So yeah, that’s some of the best moments this year. As cynical as I am, I bet there will be more in the future. In the wise words of Lana Del Rey, “you don’t know what the new day may bring.” Happy 21st birthday to me, I guess.
