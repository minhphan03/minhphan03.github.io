Given how big of a deal that Generative Artificial Intelligence (Gen-AI) has instantly bursted into the tech scene and completely taken over our lives, one may think that AI-generated content is flooding the internet any time now. Big news: it already is. I think a lot of the time people do not notice that some piece of content they come across is AI-generated, and it’s a big concern for me. I don’t know how the world is going to be once 100% of our sources of information are not written by humans anymore, but I am certain that at that point, our humanity is doomed. For thousands of years, human creativity and creation is the centerpiece of our lives: art, music, even a new food recipe or something as simple as building toy blocks. Creating things brings us joy, and by proactively interacting with the surroundings we proceed to learn effectively and eventually assimilate cultures. 

Like many of you, I experience first hand doom-scrolling (and even if so, I still cannot let it go), a passive, brain-rotting activity exclusive to the modern technology age. We consume content passively to kill time without any critical thinking about what comes across our eyes. We know that it is dangerous simply because it is a perfect indoctrination method that big companies are using to capture our attention and influence our opinions (see Palentir). It’s insanely difficult to get rid of it, and now with the emergence of AI, our data is personalized to the degree that our content feed is catered and detailed so intricately that propaganda and indoctrination, deceivingly, naturally becomes facts. There is not even a blurry line between the real and unreal, for it is so seamlessly integrated like something you would see from Perfect Blue (1999) or Inception (2010). AI may not be perfect now that many a time we can still detect it, but it will eventually be to a dangerous point of no return. Hell no, to some people it’s already undistinguishable from reality. It’s even worse because it can spit out content at the speed of light without any supervision, which human-generated edits (i.e. Photoshop) can no longer compare. Fake news? You got it. Altered images? Easy-peasy. Deep-fakes and revenge porn? We’ve already had them. Any depraved thing you can imagine a human can do is now ten times problematic thanks to the use of AI, and unless we stop trusting everything we see and read, the humanity is only going to get worse. Right now you can already witness the dumpster fires on Twitter and Facebook. The older generations are not ready to face this “pandemic” at all.

Rotting myself enough on Twitter through this election cycle taught me an important lesson: media users are trapped in echo chambers created by these algorithms and you can never sway their opinions with the hard truths and counterarguments, and even if you have proofs to show, they dismiss them as deepfakes and find other excuses to sway from the hard-to-swallow pills. AI-generated content, especially images and videos, will only serve to divide our communities even more, as one short prompt and one swift click is now all that needed to fool naïve baby boomers on Facebook these days. Given that [over 5.5 billion, or almost 70%, of the world population have access to the Internet](https://www.forbes.com/home-improvement/internet/internet-statistics/), we have a nearly impossible task on our hands to do to educate people on the new tools that malicious people on the Internet have access to.

You can see where I am going with this. This ever-rising concern of the lack of regulations for AI put our humanity as a whole at a big risk: can we even trust ourselves anymore? How do you know that the person you’re talking to over the phone is not duped by a AI-generated voice copying the actual person you’re supposed to talk to? Not everyone is tech-savvy or analytical enough to navigate these cobwebs, and even so no one can stay alert that long without calling on an existential crisis on themselves. You may chalk off this argument as hyperbolic or melodramatic, but I’ve followed too many Gen-AI tech news reporter and hype men to be afraid that its advancements are no joke. We can barely detect normal scams, so tell me how are we going to protect ourselves more sophisticated lies like misused AI generated content? With the speed at which this technology is developed, it will be eventually impossible to utilize the Turing test, a test of a machine's ability to exhibit intelligent behavior, to differentiate between artificial intelligence and actual humans ([hell, it already seemed to bypass anyway](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2313925121)). If there is no serious regulation and consideration, the consequences will be immeasurable. Therefore, when the hype of AI began to flourish and big tech companies start to push it on users through useless, unnecessary features ([Google AI summary](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jackkelly/2024/05/31/google-ai-glue-to-pizza-viral-blunders/), the [Meta AI summary feature in comment sections](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2024/05/22/meta-ai-news-summaries/), the unexpectedly comical [Apple Intelligence notification feature](https://www.dailydot.com/news/apple-intelligence-text-summary-chatgpt/)), it frustrates me to no end. There is not enough testing they have done before publishing it to users, and the news are there to prove that: [Microsoft delayed Recall four times](https://www.theverge.com/2024/10/31/24284572/microsoft-recall-delay-december-windows-insider-testing); [Google cut back AI Overviews in June before reintroducing it recently](https://www.wired.com/story/google-cut-back-ai-overviews-before-pizza-glue/); [a Character.AI bot was alleged to be responsible for a teen’s suicide](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/characterai-lawsuit-florida-teen-death-rcna176791). As someone who absolutely detests automated machines encountered through customer service calls, I just know that this tech is not ready to tackle on the too many complicated facets of real-life situations. On the contrary, it’s just another hyped-up shiny new thing to pump up stock prices amidst the saturation of (useless/misused) innovations going on in the industry (see: Blockchain, NFTs, Web 3.0/4.0, cryptocurrencies) thanks to OpenAI. Each of these hyped up products is just another cycle and inflated balloon waiting to burst at some point (I’d love to be proven wrong here, because if the stock market crashes my investments would unfortunately also take a painful sucker punch). After every bubble like that, thousands of people go bankrupted and families broken, and the riches who have the financial to pump moneys in speculation and manipulate markets take all. I don’t understand why as humans we keep behaving like moths to the fires, blinding ourselves with greed and short-term personal benefits and only realizing that when it’s too late to salvage anything. We all need to do better, seriously… 

Have you ever heard of the dead Internet theory? The theory basically states that much of the content posted on the internet today are made by bots or non-human generated content. A feedback loop of endless spam created by malicious corporations and agents to boost up traffic. Imagine how the comment you read on Reddit or an advice on some health forum was recycled by some nameless, faceless bot with a generated identity, what would you think?

Staying vigilant is one thing; we need to counter those waves of lazy content generating by learning to express our individuality and critically evaluate our viewpoints. Writing, to me, is one of the best way to combat. Choosing words and viewpoints to express your beliefs helps oneself organize ideas, explore different perspectives, and consider situations where their words may arouse unwanted reactions. It is what a lot of people regard as “research”: putting themselves in conflicting viewpoints instead of succumbing to catered, easily digestible junks is an effective way to face yourself with the truth. Come to think of it, no-one wants to surround themselves with hard truths, and algorithm developers push this universal basis to the extreme. Tech bros and corporations go for lengths to make AI the next best thing partially because it’s a big hype train and they’re betting on it being the force to drive profits and influences. Literally big chunk of the stock market right now is sustaining thanks to the investment in AI: [the utilities sector bets on data center energy consumption demand as a drive for growth](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/this-low-risk-sector-has-outperformed-tech-stocks-this-year-but-its-still-all-about-ai-c5349ac6?gaa_at=eafs&gaa_n=AWEtsqfyVw4783mvyazi35l7KJ3MwKmXw8a5vrSCQPrG1xljqbCdTBisL6CT&gaa_ts=68f1b8f3&gaa_sig=4uM_VHbamidyq7cKPvzNHoPMLbSHROwDKX3vnT6dVtDeXy9tc7pptzNvGwWW4Xukph8-NPV4y3oWp7qoiYCczg%3D%3D), and the idea that economy growth from AI is just [pure speculation](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/markets/stock-market-ai-boom-dot-com-bubble-rcna237679). They have a reason to try to push us to use it, to prove investors that these pretty shits from a butt are worth using and the slops generated by Gen AI are somehow better than real artists putting time and sweat into their art. Therefore we need to remind ourselves that the more they try to push these algorithms on us, the harder we need to push back and avoid their mind-games. We all know that data is money, that our interactions with these services can somehow one day are turned against us and help these companies design services that enshittify our lives. That means that the less we share our data, the better. Learn to second-guess everything you hear and read, and learn to find reasoning in things that matter. There is a phrase, “keep your head cool and your heart warm,” alluding to one’s necessity to keep their cool from reactivity and spontaneity, and show compassion to others. In our case, this means learning to process a piece of information first instead of hastily coming up with a counterpoint to protect one’s stance. We should all realize at this point that no one is perfect, and what we stand for may not be the right one all the time. Hence, why falling in the trap of comfort and admitting defeat to manipulative, malicious strangers on the Internet? 

Update: I have written this piece a while ago, and given how dizzying and overhyped the Gen AI development is now, some of these points may be outdated. Still, my opinion stands: this technology is unsustainable and never meant to be populated this big of a scale to the whole world. [Within two years the AI adoption has doubled that of the Internet](https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2024/sep/rapid-adoption-generative-ai), and given how sophisticated, underregulated, and environmentally challenging this technology is, I believe we should take a look into our inner self and ask whether we are truly ready to embrace it. Unlike the Internet which stimulates our exploring and encourages globalization, Gen AI chatbots were developed to spill out one-dimensional responses that cater our needs. We have been doing just fine for the past few decades without these technologies that distort our realities and confuse our mentality, and it's totally normal and even encouragin to question its authenticity now. Our brains are too bright to give in such lazy habits developed and supported by the greedy venture capitalists, don't you think so?
