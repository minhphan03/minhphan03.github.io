*My original personal statement that I submitted to University of Washington - Seattle Campus as part of my transfer student application. Now that I got accepted to UW, I decided to post this for fun!*

"Gautama Buddha once said: 'What you think, You become.' You should be sleeping. If you're still thinking otherwise, you are not going to."

"I can’t. It’s so hot!"

"Your mind can do it all. Just think about something else and you’ll forget it."

It was midnight on the porch. The paper fan and our conversation permeated throughout the open space. My grandfather had already told me bedtime stories, but then we were cut off power. Summer heat was intense that year. On the hammock, sleep suddenly became elusive. He had to lull me to sleep again. Listening to him, I ignored the sweat dripping all over my skin. I stared at the rusty layers of iron ceiling, musing the words of Buddha my grandfather had said earlier.

I dozed off some time later. Those summer days in my countryside hometown were thrilling. I remembered pedaling through the ancient town, passing the dewy rice fields. I missed the soothing waves gliding across the open sea, the salt air around the fishing villages. I would spend the day with my neighbors’ kids playing hide and seek, horse race chess and mancalas. In the afternoons we would walk to the local market and enjoy Banh Mis. When the winds were high, paper kites overshadowed the clear sky. But as soon as twilight came, I was already back at home with my grandparents in their botany-covered cottage, helping them with chores and enjoying the starry nights.

How carefree I was back then. But time flew in a split seconds and I got stuck in the city life. I got lost among the whirlwinds of city life like my parents. They got busier with work so I started going to schools by bus. I could observe people from all walks of life: old grannies chattering away amidst the jostling crowd; college students waiting by the bus stops, conversing lively. Beyond the panels, opulent officers were sipping coffees by the curbs and lottery sellers roaming the streets. Street vendors were selling all kinds of street foods that sometimes I would stop by to have some steamy sticky rice and stir-fried noodles. I felt... belonged. There were many familiar scenes that resembled my hometown that I adored. As I observed them for months, I slowly realized that nothing in life was permanent. Everything was constantly changing so there was no point in resisting alterations. My grandfather was right: people could only become Buddhas if they could let their minds be at ease.

Time is always moving, but I did not want to forget my experiences. I wanted to personally see the changes that would shape the world. Through buses I started capturing photos that reflected those changes. I used a planner to plan trips around farther places, where every stop bloomed amazements in me. A part of me has formed a strong connection to the city, but I also grew interest in devising those trips. I recorded routes that I’ve passed by. I calculated budgets for spending. Dealing with information slowly became a hobby. In high school, I participated in art club and music club, and in health organization off campus where I was keeping track of our budgets. I also made use of social media analytics, forms and spreadsheets to collect activity statistics and create reports.

Fall of 2019, I came to the United States to study at a community college. Going to college instead of finishing high school forced me to focus on my future visions. After doing some research on major options, I finally settled down to pursue an Associate Degree in Computer Science. I thought that it was the best option to work with data. Programming simple animation games and websites offered in the elective courses prompted me to use data more proactively, then I became more confident with coding using Java, Python and C++. I also reached out to do community service at the local food bank. As a data entry, I had to record customers' data into the database. It was the first time I’ve ever worked on one. The system was customized to process a gigantic amount of data, automatically single out errors and incorporating other systems in the bank to generate products like visualizations and reports. It piqued my curiosity on how people could build such powerful systems to revolutionize life. The more I delved deeper into these concepts, the more I became fascinated with Data Science. How terms like predictive analytics, business intelligence, big data, etc. became integral in our lives impressed and motivated me to bring their greater good to the community.

Preparing for the field, I readjusted my college coursework to fit more mathematics cores like calculuses, linear algebra, and sciences to my degree. But I realized the limited amount of courses provided cannot further my career path, so I began looking for transfer universities when sophomore year began. The University of Washington stood out as a strong choice with its wide range of mathematics and computer science courses that definitely satisfy my love of learning. Its Applied and Computational Mathematical Sciences major has a special Data Science option, which I believe will provide important skills for my future pathway. As a renowned research institution, University of Washington understands that data is the key to arrive at unbiased research results. I want to participate in research opportunities to learn and apply my skills to aid researchers achieve their goals. Besides developing my career, I’m also interested in some clubs like the AACUW, Data Ethics Club and Vietnamese Student Association, that will enable me to challenge myself to broaden my horizons while bolstering my identities. Thanks to the student-driven school subreddit, I was impressed with many humorous, down-to-earth people of the academia that can support me on my journey at the institution.

As I came to terms with the impermanent state of life, I strived to appreciate every change in my life. Its arbitrariness and diversity has contributed to my cultures and experiences that form my education path and the career path I wanted to pursue. Data science fascinate me because it vividly reflected those experiences: people always learn from observations. Research and data collection reveal how existences can be so logical yet so unreal. With data, we can record and preserve the beauty of life. However, for many beautiful Vietnamese areas like where my grandparents live, where community values and natural beauty remain intact, sustainability with these technologies requires visions to enhance these beautiful, ancient values.
