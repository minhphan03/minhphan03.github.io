I was seriously thinking about the fact that the U.S. population used to be able to influence their leaders’ courses of action, especially on diplomatic or foreign affairs, like what happened in the Vietnam War. [The waves of protest and anger](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/americanexperience/features/two-days-in-october-student-antiwar-protests-and-backlash/) were strong enough to eventually stop the government’s military expeditions in the country, putting a stop to their war plans. For once, they admitted defeat. Of course, I was not alive back then to fully acknowledge the scale of these protests, but eventually, they were able to achieve what they wanted. Obviously, the U.S. government did not outright admit they were in the wrong, but their efforts slowed down so they could retreat their troops and stop their intervention as their ultimate commitment to weakening the influence of communism (against North Vietnam’s communist-based party) did not go as planned. The U.S. also retreated their troops in Afghanistan later in the mid-2010s, and stopped their military interventions here and there, but only when they were directly involved and human lives were at stake. 

Therefore, I was disappointed but not surprised that they would back up the IDF’s genocidal operations in Palestine/Gaza and Lebanon, where no U.S. personnel are physically in danger. They have virtually no reason to stop supporting it, mainly because the U.S. government is a big benefactor and beneficiary in all of this: by supplying ammunition and military gadgets to Israel, the government strengthens the bond between the two big countries, gains support from Jewish donors & communities (which is critical as elections are around the corner), and makes bucks. Lobbying is a big deal, and no one should be surprised how deeply powerful Jewish people (or, for those with a sense of Zionist nationalism, Zionists) have reached inside the bureaucratic machine. During World War II, Jewish families immigrated to the U.S. to escape the growing Nazi fascist influences and have since built strong communities in the country. To prevent estrangement and a repeat of the past, they relied on maintaining committed alliances with the U.S. and ensure that lobbying was strong enough that officials would come to protect and support their interests. 

The U.S. played a big role in establishing the state of Israel in 1947; AIPAC (American Israel Public Affairs Committee) publicly demonstrated its work on lobbying bureaucrats. Between 1976 and 2004, [Israel received the most direct foreign assistance from the U.S. of any nation, approximately 0.1% of the $3 trillion overall U.S. annual budget (not allocated to foreign support)](https://www.ynetnews.com/articles/0,7340,L-3362402,00.html). No sane person in this capitalistic society would refuse that big lump of money, right? it is funny how people try to paint lobbying in a good light as if it is not some other type of corruption. Despite naysayers arguing their reasoning about why lobbying is legal, in many cases, it should be deemed an abuse of power. Politicians voicing their support for genocides did it for the money because their words speak volumes, but many of them do not understand (or ignore) how influential their opinions are. Not only do their decisions and policies matter, but in the end, a lot of people tend to trust those they voted for. I am not saying this is confirmation bias, but for those who voted, it is certainly more difficult to be disillusioned, given how divided things already are in the current political climate (for example, the problematic Donald Trump vs. the idea of voting red against blue).

I am saying this because while we’re fighting for our progressive voices to be heard, more conservative, traditional figures (and even so-called "progressive thinkers") higher up in the food chain want to keep things the way they have been for the last few hundred years so they can keep the system running for their sole benefit. The U.S.-Israel ties have been so strong, and even more so with dedicated organizations established to keep it that way, like [AIPAC (American Israel Public Affairs Committee)](https://www.aipac.org). "Thanks" to these private organizations meddling in governmental business, we have [the House of Representatives passing the "Israel Security Assistance Support Act"](https://appropriations.house.gov/news/press-releases/house-passes-israel-security-assistance-support-act), in which the mission of "supporting our great ally and U.S. national security" aligns with what they are clearly devoted to working on, or $20 billion in military aid was approved in August. Of course, these are more than just practical proofs of the relationship between the two nations: the U.S. has more than ample reasons to support Israel in this "war": to increase its presence and impact in the Middle East, and make hefty profits for the military industry. It is no stranger to interference in the area: look at the wars in Iraq, Afghanistan, Iran—these three are enough to know what the common factor is. The presence of capitalism and modern imperialism leaves little room for humanity and empathy. Everything has to be polarized in black and white instead of a nuanced gradient of gray. By taking a look at some of these sources I have linked in this article and considering the current political climate in the U.S., it should be easy to explain why challenging the genocide is simply a losing battle and a lost cause. It is just not feasible when so many of these officials are disgustingly "bought."

For a country with such a deep pride on "democracy," it is ridiculous to see how our choices and "freedom" are not ours to make but belong to the [rich](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2024/jan/10/congress-member-pro-israel-donations-military-support) and [extremists](https://www.opensecrets.org/industries/summary?cycle=All&ind=Q05&recipdetail=S), through the act of lobbying. These figures only serve the benefit of those who are too "up there" to see through the delusion they are in, and they have little to no sympathy to acknowledge that what happens in front of their eyes is inhumane and unethical. Many Jewish people were harassed despite having no involvement in these attacks or support for the genocide. As a result, these instances tainted the reputations of people who genuinely cared about these meaningless deaths and wholeheartedly advocated for change. This single-minded, misleading viewpoint on Palestine that made waves in the media clouded our judgment that this is not the only ongoing tragedy in the world. There’s genocidal incidents in [Congo](https://www.nytimes.com/2023/12/17/world/africa/democratic-republic-of-congo-elections.html), [Papua New Guinea](https://thediplomat.com/2024/04/indonesian-imperialism-is-alive-and-brutal-in-west-papua/), [Haiti](https://time.com/7000221/haiti-port-au-prince-gang-war/), [Lebanon](https://truthout.org/articles/lebanon-death-toll-rises-as-un-experts-condemn-israels-attacks-as-war-crimes/), [Bangladesh](https://www.thehindu.com/news/international/probe-starts-against-former-bangladesh-pm-hasina-9-others-for-genocide-crimes-against-humanity/article68528238.ece), [China](https://uyghurstudy.org/the-plight-of-the-uyghurs-2014-2024/), and more that these people haven’t considered. Again, everything is nuanced to an extent. Have you ever wondered why a celebrity hasn’t spoken up about this genocide? The conflict didn’t begin on October 7th -- this event is only a diminutive part of a long, brutal history. It should be treated as such. Countless of "incidents" and violent acts have taken place way way before this, but the only reason why it blew up was because Israel was the victim here.
