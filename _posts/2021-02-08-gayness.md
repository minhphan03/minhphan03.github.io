I’m infuriated. I don’t even manage to speak into words how frustrated I am right now. 

So you know I’m gay. And some people said that being gay made me less masculine. How ridiculous was that? Why can’t they judge me by being who I am and not my sexual orientation? I’ve been pensive about how they could conclude that gayness could fundamentally twist my image into some monsters whom should not approach their naive children. Could the fact that I am gay make me a contagious disease? How illogical it sounds — especially when we consider that a gay person in the closet is comparatively nothing different from an open one (presumably more self-confident and open)? Would a simple saying disclose the entire catastrophe that is enough to collapse their (homophobic) views?

So what is masculinity, exactly? What defines masculinity and femininity? According to Merriam-Webster, it was vague and ambiguous: “the quality or nature of the male sex: the quality, state, or degree of being masculine or manly.” Regardless of how much dictionaries I tried to look up this specific word, they all returned a general yet unspecific answer similar to Merriam-Webster’s. 

One of the reasons they presume that homosexuality is unorthodox originated from cultural misrepresentation. For instance, people in my country (Vietnam) have always condemned homosexuals because the only way they could recognize them was through open individuals working in lotto shows (can be comprehended as folk ensembles, but most known for “drag queens”). “Feminine” men became the butt of the joke because they were “freakish” and “unconventional” compared to normalcy. To survive in that society, they had no choice but to exhibit their queerness (transgender, roleplaying, ridiculous make-ups) under entertainers’ names. Culture plays a significant role in shaping beliefs and ideologies. For an agriculture culture existed for thousands of years in Vietnam, entertainers were useless in a pragmatic economy, which left them almost at the bottom of the social class. Over time, these open homosexuals had become the misrepresentation of the whole community.

The lack of gender education and exposure negates counteractive efforts. Gender socialization plays a primary role in how boys and girls perceive masculinity and femininity. It all started from birth when children are exposed to parent’s perceptions about gender roles. Boys dress in blue and girls in pinky garments. Some years later, toys came to life and then having toy companies familiarizing them with distinct lines of toys. They choose words to advertise their products in a way that clearly shows how the society expects newborns to perform gender roles: if boys are expected to feel enthralled with “stealthy” gun toys and “special-force” superheroes and their lethal, bulky weapons, girls are not. On the other hand, they “should” spend their time boring over-imaginative, lavish lifestyles of their skinny plastic dolls. This stereotype ruins how children freely choose their lifestyles and are content with it; in fact, it caged them in gender distortions manipulated by adults (and will generationally affect the so-called children when they grow up, as an effect of that notion). They suffered (especially when they started to acknowledge their preference) in questions galore why boys could not express and expand their creativity roleplaying and cooking, and sporty girls could not help but being persecuted anomaly. Those reproaches linger to today. Notwithstanding more openness and cross-culturalization (westernization), most Vietnamese implement passively archaic stereotypes that gay people are “HIV spreaders,” sex maniacs, and unfaithful. 

Put yourself in their shoes. Would you feel ashamed and infuriated? This is the problem of stereotyping, and toxic masculinity is a prime candidate for this. When you assess somebody’s masculinity throughout their sexual orientation, or their violence, or their judgments, you are manifesting toxic masculinity. If you believe that a man should be emotionless and deal with his problems alone, stoically facing his demons without asking for help, you advocate for toxic masculinity. When you render somebody masculine by acquainting him with violence to answer every conflict and regarding bloody faces and bruises on others as a victory, you are astray in toxic masculinity. People suffer from it because stereotypes like themselves are mostly inaccurate (hence leading to negative meaning.) Not everyone was born to be heavily muscled and engage to fighting on the streets and bully other children. Furthermore, not every man is born to love women. By looking through a crooked, askew yet superficial perspective, stereotypes only prompt to be worse. That’s why some gay men rather live in the closets for the rest of their lives.

Another stereotype: people blindly believed that there are only two genders, two sex. Gender is fluid, not binary. There are male, female, non-binary, and beyond. How people proclaim that gender beyond those first two and incite our society to look down on and repress other types of gender and/or sexual orientations is beyond my knowledge. Fully understood that gender (not sex) is fluid would make “bearing” easier because LGBT+ stereotypes are rigid, and diversity never harms anyone.
