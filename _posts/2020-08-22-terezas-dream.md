Tereza’s dream of Tomas shooting her to death into the pool among soulless, uniform bodies, to me, did not only imply merely about her fear of losing his love as the notion of lightness and weight. Undeniably, she was fearful of losing his loving (instead considered as compassion), but this fear stated more of the never-ending irreconcilable conflict between lightness (the randomness of life and unparalleled beauty) and weight (the act of assigning meanings and values to notions and beliefs). I believed that the author tried to convey a sense of randomness in those dreams, with pools, guns from nowhere to appear in her dreams. In those dreams was the lightness truly existed in its pristine form: randomness, meaningless, unbiased, uncared, and euphoric. And Tereza’s reactions to it clarified the boundaries of the dichotomies.

------

**First, about what the link between dream and lightness** To Tereza, the dream symbolizes (temporary) emancipation. Either death or dreaming can only achieve the deliverance from life. While death is not a very amusing choice, dreaming is (apparently) a more achievable yet satisfactory more than death. By escaping into dreams, you look for ‘lightness’ away from the burden of reality and its responsibility. Dreams are contrasting to the latter because you are neither bound to any ground of reasons nor being adversely affected on a profound level. Since reality can be unbearable sometimes, many people look up to dreams to lighten up their burdensome and motivate their will to live. Since dreaming just happened without premonition or conscious and can happen anywhere, it symbolizes lightness. There are no strings attached, for you cannot retain its remnants after you wake up. Lightness can only be achieved when you are detached from your reality, which dreams only partially fulfilled. I stated “only partially achieved” because dreaming can also end up in nightmares despite meaning emancipation. Dreams are not controllable. You cannot tell whether you will have blissful dreams to escape to or nightmares to stay away from. Hence it’s unbearable being “light” sometimes. Having no strings attached means you cannot hold yourself against any boundaries of the dreams you had. They are uncertain, unpredictable, aimless, and infinite. Nightmares fright people (on a superficial degree), but people can do nothing to prevent them from happening. Drawing to the symbol of lightness, we see that lightness has its negativity. Lightness is not just floating away from reality; it also strips yourself of control on your life. As a result, this notion drives the whole image to more of a darker state of being. 

**Second, about the images in her dream** The ladies’ identical naked bodies swaying around the pool stands for her mother’s perceptions of the woman body: all equal and mediocre. As a woman, Tereza valued her body beauty, but her mother’s ruined beauty and her continuation of her mother distorted her views. She habitually checked herself out in the mirror to remind herself that the body was special. Still, she could not help but reminisce back to her mother’s egotistic, snarky comments on the woman’s body. She could not extricate herself from mulling over her heavy heartstrings because she was, unfortunately, had to be a child to that immodest woman. The mother’s immodesty and hatred for her daughter destroyed the child, reckoned her devalue herself, and lose interest in changing her fate. Seeing herself among those bodies reminded her that hers was just “one of them,” unwanted. On top of that, Tomas’ image and a pistol on his hand only confirmed her theory. She thought herself of a mistress, whose body was just a tool for the man to pleasure himself on top of her despite going to lengths to change her fate. She felt vulnerable because Tomas was fateful yet powerful to turn her life upside down anytime (pulling the gun and killed her), and she didn’t dream to end up like that. However, she couldn’t help but believing her body was just an unremarkable tool for the man, just like what her mother had told her. The bond between her heart and her mother’s fateful quotations was so ample that it even crept into the subconscious haunt her for it.

The dream could have had implied how Tereza regarded the connection between her body and soul. Because her mother’s views impacted her substantially, she wanted to escape it. Ironically, her deliverance to dreams hindered her from it. It enclosed her in an everlasting loop of her dark past rooted deep inside her despite numerous attempts to extricate. She believed that her soul was different (and hence the body) from the others in that dream, yet it was never seen. The body and soul were intertwined, but only the body is visible to the eyes and is the only one judicable. Even if she wanted to prove that her body (and soul) was unique from the other mistresses’ in that dream, her mother’s generational body said otherwise. As a continuation of her mother, the immodest lady tainted her child despite the other’s relentless attempts to block herself from those conceptions. Tereza’s apprehension of a prim, merciless Tomas in her dreams only served to lower her image and imbued her mother’s predominant beliefs on her. Yes, my body was different, but it wasn’t special enough to be treated differently. It was just a tool for the man’s sexual pleasure. The soul deprived. She was euphoric on the outside, but her heart was depressed.

We can refer to the body and soul as weight and lightness, respectively. From what we had analyzed, this why lightness is unbearable: people have no control over their dreams, and it backfires any attempt to set free from their woes. Her body kept their love burning, but her soul only annihilated it. She couldn’t help but fret and dream about her past. She could have lived freely with Tomas now that she had run away from her past, yet recollections and modifications couldn’t stop overflowing. Owning to the fact that people can’t prevent the formation of dreams, Tereza might never escape the loop. Her body was beautiful, yet only her soul linked it to her mother’s genetic form. The body was tangible, but since the mind (the soul) was intangible, it could escape everywhere and nowhere to cook up her emotions, just like lightness: groundless, uncertain, and dubious.

**Last, the fear of randomness and the passion for assigning** The dream illustrated an unavoidable dilemma that Tereza has to sacrifice: whether she wanted to stay with Tomas, her fateful womanizer, or stay away from him to drift away from unpreventable jealousy afterward. She feared that her beloved husband’s doubts would only intensify through time as she replayed those dark, twisted, beautiful nightmares — that has carefully crafted into legends. If she had left, her heart would break, but how could she bear the demons in her heads that cast her into pathetic characters every night, begging her philanderer (and his mistresses) to stop his infidelities, to the rest of her life? She would visit them without consent, thinking that one day those signals would not only stop at being “something” and the dire inevitable would perish her. She knew that when the bright sun arises, and reality swept back into her consciousness, they would evaporate, but how could her mind be at rest? In a closed circulation of fighting and crying on the bed at night after nightmares, life became unbearable. She has doubted her weight because she started to see the signs of infidelity. 

Tereza has always been a dreamer, but she was too emotionally connected to those imaginations. Her falsified notions (like believing in fate, coincidental numbers, etc.) were incorrigible, but it guided her through her mother’s dark past. In her dreams, her body should have glowed with her spirit of a faithful, righteous woman, but she was just a resemblance of somebody else she found distaste in real life. The dream reflected her hatred because she had been so deeply integrated with it, that it only fueled her hatred. Nevertheless, that dream also gave her the good side of desire: she got more reasons to keep Tomas at all costs. Her body was the reason that drove her to Tomas, her fate, and that reason alone saved her. The salvation was from her body, hopping on a train to Tomas and changed her life. 

Tereza’s perceptions like that were just an example of why she is considered weight. It was her fault that she mulled it over in her mind, replaying the fading images that her subconscious hatched out from nowhere. Sure, it could have reflected her life and her fear of losing her love, but it didn’t forecast anything, really. If only she realized that her nightmares were transient and unsubstantial, she would truly enjoy its hidden beauty. She would certainly not going to cry in front of Tomas if she was not too engaged in her toxic thoughts and let it polarized her soul. If she had dropped her beliefs in fates, invisible strings, possessiveness, and the ultimate million-dollar question “What are the meanings of those events?”, she would have enjoyed the randomness of the dream in a comprehension that it was nothing more than just the incorrigible past she could not revoke. Only then she could finally came to bathe herself in its fully lightness, bearable and exotic self way more than she could ever do. 

------

In a world where lightness hardly ever becomes weight and vice versa, lightness becomes unbearable if you tried to assign meanings and weight to everything you think of. Your exaggerated thoughts only burden your mind and drive you crazy. Just enjoy life and see what the future has in store for you. You only live your life once, so don’t assign any meanings to your reality: there’s no way “it should have happened that way” or “it is an prophecy” because who would those “that way” and “omen” would result in anything if you didn’t try to actualize them? Hence, “es muss sein” or thinking with your heart is unnecessary weight, and in contrast, believing in logic makes life more bearable. 

I don’t know if I have mentioned the title of the book, but it’s “The Unbearable Lightness of Being” by Milan Kundera.
