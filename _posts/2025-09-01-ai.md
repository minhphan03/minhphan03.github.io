Given how big of a deal Generative Artificial Intelligence (Gen-AI) has instantly become in the tech scene and how completely it has taken over our lives, one may think that AI-generated content is only now beginning to flood the internet. Big news: it already is. I think a lot of the time people do not notice that some piece of content they come across is AI-generated, and that’s a big concern for me. I don’t know how the world is going to be once 100% of our sources of information are no longer written by humans, but I am certain that at that point, our humanity is doomed. For thousands of years, human creativity and creation have been the centerpieces of our lives: art, music, even a new food recipe, or something as simple as building toy blocks. Creating things brings us joy, and by proactively interacting with our surroundings, we learn effectively and eventually assimilate cultures.

Like many of you, I experience firsthand doom-scrolling (and even so, I still cannot let it go) — a passive, brain-rotting activity exclusive to the modern technological age. We consume content passively to kill time without any critical thinking about what comes across our eyes. We know that it is dangerous simply because it is a perfect indoctrination method that big companies use to capture our attention and influence our opinions (see Palantir). It’s insanely difficult to get rid of it, and now with the emergence of AI, our data is personalized to the degree that our content feed is catered and detailed so intricately that propaganda and indoctrination deceivingly and naturally become facts. There is not even a blurry line between the real and the unreal, for it is so seamlessly integrated — like something you would see in *Perfect Blue* (1999) or *Inception* (2010). AI may not be perfect now, since we can still detect it many times, but it will eventually reach a dangerous point of no return. Hell no — to some people, it’s already indistinguishable from reality. It’s even worse because it can spit out content at the speed of light without any supervision, something human-generated edits (i.e., Photoshop) can no longer compare to. Fake news? You got it. Altered images? Easy-peasy. Deepfakes and revenge porn? We’ve already had them. Any depraved thing you can imagine a human doing is now ten times more problematic thanks to AI, and unless we stop trusting everything we see and read, humanity is only going to get worse. Right now, you can already witness the dumpster fires on Twitter and Facebook. The older generations are not ready to face this “pandemic” at all.

Rotting myself enough on Twitter through this election cycle taught me an important lesson: media users are trapped in echo chambers created by these algorithms, and you can never sway their opinions with hard truths or counterarguments. Even if you have proof to show, they dismiss it as deepfakes and find other excuses to avoid swallowing hard truths. AI-generated content, especially images and videos, will only serve to divide our communities even more, as one short prompt and one swift click are now all it takes to fool naïve baby boomers on Facebook. Given that [over 5.5 billion, or almost 70%, of the world’s population have access to the Internet](https://www.forbes.com/home-improvement/internet/internet-statistics/), we have a nearly impossible task ahead of us to educate people about the new tools that malicious actors on the Internet now have access to.

You can see where I’m going with this. This ever-rising concern about the lack of regulations for AI puts our humanity as a whole at great risk: can we even trust ourselves anymore? How do you know that the person you’re talking to over the phone isn’t being duped by an AI-generated voice copying the actual person you’re supposed to talk to? Not everyone is tech-savvy or analytical enough to navigate these cobwebs, and even so, no one can stay alert that long without calling an existential crisis upon themselves. You may chalk this argument up as hyperbolic or melodramatic, but I’ve followed too many Gen-AI tech news reporters and hype men to believe its advancements are a joke. We can barely detect normal scams, so tell me — how are we going to protect ourselves from more sophisticated lies like misused AI-generated content? With the speed at which this technology is developing, it will eventually be impossible to use the Turing test — a test of a machine’s ability to exhibit intelligent behavior — to differentiate between artificial intelligence and actual humans ([hell, it already seems to have bypassed it anyway](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2313925121)). If there is no serious regulation and consideration, the consequences will be immeasurable.

Therefore, when the hype around AI began to flourish and big tech companies started pushing it on users through useless, unnecessary features ([Google AI summary](https://www.forbes.com/sites/jackkelly/2024/05/31/google-ai-glue-to-pizza-viral-blunders/), the [Meta AI summary feature in comment sections](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2024/05/22/meta-ai-news-summaries/), or the unexpectedly comical [Apple Intelligence notification feature](https://www.dailydot.com/news/apple-intelligence-text-summary-chatgpt/)), it frustrated me to no end. There isn’t enough testing done before releasing these features to users, and the news proves it: [Microsoft delayed Recall four times](https://www.theverge.com/2024/10/31/24284572/microsoft-recall-delay-december-windows-insider-testing); [Google cut back AI Overviews in June before reintroducing it recently](https://www.wired.com/story/google-cut-back-ai-overviews-before-pizza-glue/); [a Character.AI bot was alleged to be responsible for a teen’s suicide](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/characterai-lawsuit-florida-teen-death-rcna176791). As someone who absolutely detests automated machines encountered through customer service calls, I just know that this tech is not ready to tackle the many complicated facets of real-life situations. On the contrary, it’s just another hyped-up, shiny new thing to pump up stock prices amidst the saturation of useless or misused innovations in the industry (see: Blockchain, NFTs, Web 3.0/4.0, cryptocurrencies) thanks to OpenAI. Each of these hyped-up products is just another cycle and inflated balloon waiting to burst at some point (I’d love to be proven wrong here, because if the stock market crashes, my investments would unfortunately take a painful sucker punch). After every bubble like that, thousands of people go bankrupt and families are broken, while the rich — who have the financial means to pump money into speculation and manipulate markets — take it all. I don’t understand why, as humans, we keep behaving like moths to flames, blinding ourselves with greed and short-term personal benefits, only realizing it when it’s too late to salvage anything. We all need to do better, seriously…

Have you ever heard of the Dead Internet Theory? The theory basically states that much of the content posted on the internet today is made by bots or non-human-generated content — a feedback loop of endless spam created by malicious corporations and agents to boost traffic. Imagine if the comment you read on Reddit or advice on some health forum was recycled by some nameless, faceless bot with a generated identity — what would you think?

Staying vigilant is one thing; we need to counter those waves of lazy content generation by learning to express our individuality and critically evaluate our viewpoints. Writing, to me, is one of the best ways to combat this. Choosing words and viewpoints to express your beliefs helps organize ideas, explore different perspectives, and consider situations where your words may arouse unwanted reactions. It’s what many people regard as “research”: putting themselves in conflicting viewpoints instead of succumbing to catered, easily digestible junk is an effective way to face the truth. Come to think of it, no one wants to surround themselves with hard truths, and algorithm developers exploit this universal tendency to the extreme. Malicious uses of deep learning technology can generate fake content, and it’s becoming more disastrously realistic and “natural” over time. It’s even worse than—

Tech bros and corporations go to great lengths to make AI the next best thing, partially because it’s a big hype train and they’re betting on it being the force to drive profits and influence. A large chunk of the stock market right now is sustained thanks to investment in AI: [the utilities sector bets on data center energy consumption demand as a driver of growth](https://www.marketwatch.com/story/this-low-risk-sector-has-outperformed-tech-stocks-this-year-but-its-still-all-about-ai-c5349ac6?gaa_at=eafs&gaa_n=AWEtsqfyVw4783mvyazi35l7KJ3MwKmXw8a5vrSCQPrG1xljqbCdTBisL6CT&gaa_ts=68f1b8f3&gaa_sig=4uM_VHbamidyq7cKPvzNHoPMLbSHROwDKX3vnT6dVtDeXy9tc7pptzNvGwWW4Xukph8-NPV4y3oWp7qoiYCczg%3D%3D), and the idea that economic growth from AI is just [pure speculation](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/markets/stock-market-ai-boom-dot-com-bubble-rcna237679). They have a reason to push us to use it — to prove to investors that these shiny products are worth using and that the slop generated by Gen-AI is somehow better than real artists putting time and sweat into their work. Therefore, we need to remind ourselves that the more they try to push these algorithms on us, the harder we need to push back and avoid their mind games. We all know that data is money, and that our interactions with these services can one day be turned against us to help companies design services that further enshittify our lives. That means the less we share our data, the better. Learn to second-guess everything you hear and read, and learn to find reasoning in things that matter. There’s a phrase: “keep your head cool and your heart warm,” alluding to one’s necessity to remain calm and compassionate. In our case, this means learning to process a piece of information first instead of hastily coming up with a counterpoint to protect one’s stance. We should all realize by now that no one is perfect and that what we stand for may not always be right. So why fall into the trap of comfort and admit defeat to manipulative, malicious strangers on the Internet?

**UPDATE**

I drafted this piece a while ago, and given how dizzying and overhyped Gen-AI development has become, some of these points may be outdated. Still, my opinion stands: this technology is unsustainable and was never meant to be scaled this massively across the world. Emphasis on “this big of a scale,” because if developed with the right purposes, it can be a very useful tool: Google DeepMind, the company’s AI research lab, has collaborated with Yale University to successfully develop a model that [generated a novel hypothesis about cancer cellular behavior, which scientists experimentally validated in living cells](https://blog.google/technology/ai/google-gemma-ai-cancer-therapy-discovery/). This project took years to come to fruition, and we should treat consumer product development the same way. This isn’t just a race for capitalists to see who will gain the most market share in a new field — it should also be about ethics. But we all know capitalism mostly cares about profit, and according to the renowned economist Milton Friedman, the only social responsibility businesses have is to satisfy their shareholders. This trend proves his view.

As surveys show that [within two years, AI adoption has doubled that of the Internet](https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2024/sep/rapid-adoption-generative-ai), and given how sophisticated, radically life-changing, underregulated, and environmentally challenging this technology is, I believe we should look within ourselves and ask whether we are truly ready to embrace it. Unlike the Internet, which stimulates exploration and encourages globalization, Gen-AI chatbots were developed to spill out one-dimensional responses that cater to our needs. We have been doing just fine for the past few decades without these technologies that distort our realities and confuse our mentality. Therefore, it’s totally normal (and even encouraging) to question their authenticity now. Our brains are too bright to give in to such lazy habits developed and supported by greedy venture capitalists, don’t you think so too?
