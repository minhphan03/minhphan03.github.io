Some context on my decision to apply and eventually join Rutgers: 

After graduated from UW in 2024, I moved to New Jersey for work. However, I was not fulfilled with the work that I did, so I decided to go back to school. Rutgers, being the state school of New Jersey, seems like an obvious choice. I applied to all three campuses, but only Rutgers-Camden gave me a scholarship, so I moved to the area last year to pursue accounting. The cover image is the Rutgers-Camden Robeson Library, one of the two libraries on campus. I made a lot of good memories here.

It's funny how, at the point I'm posting this, I already decided to drop out of Rutgers and enrolled in a Graduate program elsewhere. More information later.

---

As someone who started taking college classes at the age of sixteen through a dual enrollment program to finish high school, I faced the big question of picking a career path to pursue at a very young age. I did not know whether I would stick to a career fifty years into the future, so I picked a field using a simple elimination game: if I could sustain this field, I could do it. I stuck with that mindset for a few years. As time flew by, however, my skepticism about whether that was a good idea grew. I was good at what I do: I breezed through classes and projects, but the thrill and passion to aim for the extra mile never came to me. The pleasure and satisfaction I got never measured up to the effort I put in, so naturally, I asked myself the big question: Did I choose the right path? With hundreds or even thousands of different career choices out there, what were the chances I would end up with a fulfilling career I would be proud of decades later as planned?

In hindsight, accounting has always been lurking somewhere as an option. My fascination with numbers and data manifests in many ways: I track my own music listening history, movies I’ve watched over time, and my finances with a near-religious obsession—especially the latter, as I learned about investing and saving for retirement earlier last year. However, it was not until I had the opportunity to take an economics class and volunteer in a tax preparation program that I realized my obsession with spreadsheets, tables, and numbers signified something more meaningful than what I was pursuing at the time. I was not miserable with my career choice at the time, but when I chose to take those two opportunities, I subconsciously knew change was in the air. I love mathematics and am quite good at statistics, but I have learned over the years that I prefer applying these subjects in a practical field rather than sticking to theories and formulas for a living. Changing my major was no longer an option, so I stuck it out for a few years. It was not easy: I fought against regrets, doubts, and the feeling of wasted time to confidently quit what I was doing and start over with a clean slate.

Therefore, as I am given another chance to explore a career, I aspire to get the most out of this upcoming chapter of my life. As I sit here typing this statement of interest, fresh with a clear direction of what I truly aspire to this time, my decision to apply to Rutgers is a no-brainer: the university’s academic reputation and its top-ranked business school, regarded as the highest-ranked public business school in the Northeast U.S., promise to maximize my potential and propel me toward my dream of becoming a tax accountant. I am especially impressed with the dedicated Office of Undergraduate International Student Recruitment and Advising at Rutgers, which guarantees strong support for international students like me, whose barriers extend beyond academic and career planning. Ensuring that accessible resources and opportunities are available means a lot to someone who has experienced challenges securing roles and engaging in professional networking. Additionally, the proximity of the campuses to one of the busiest economic-financial hubs in the United States poses an advantage for my eagerness to explore networking and learning opportunities that I am more than ready to embrace. With these stellar resources and top-notch educational quality, I am confident that my education at Rutgers will enable me to successfully pivot to this career path I’ve been dreaming of and serve the communities that have shaped me along the way.
