*This title refers not only to the homeless but also to people engaged in charitable begging forms like street singing (a Vietnamese-only form), "mobile grocery store," and lottery selling.*

It occurred to me about a month ago, amidst the unpredictable weather of Ho Chi Minh City (or Saigon, if you're unfamiliar), as I walked past a homeless man on my way home from the bus station. He was holding a bunch of lottery tickets, signifying he had to sell all of them to receive a new one. I walked right behind him, feeling uncertain about whether I should help him or not. 10,000 ₫ is not a significant amount of money. In fact, with that, all I could buy is a cup of iced lemon tea at school. The problem is, I've observed that most people treat the homeless poorly. They either ignore them, shake their heads, or simply say, "Sorry, no." I found myself questioning why they behaved this way toward others who had even lower living conditions than themselves. I pondered this issue for a long time, continuing to observe how my parents and others treated the poor in a similar manner. Is poverty a crime? If not, why is there a "standard" behavior when encountering the poor, the homeless, or the disadvantaged seeking help?

Sometimes, they're not just asking for money and selling lottery tickets; they engage in activities like singing to sell candies, selling fruits and nuts, and more. Creativity might have emerged from their darkest times, finally seeing the light. But creativity isn't the solution to their situation. And since then, I've been questioning, answering, theorizing, and striving to find an explanation not only for the problem I've described above but also for the contradictory ways in which people respond. In Vietnam, my home country, the poor have to be proactive in begging for help. In contrast, in developed countries like the US, homeless people simply sit and wait, holding out a hat or something, for others to put money in. The total difference lies in each society. From my contemplation, I'd like to present my viewpoint on this topic today: why should or shouldn't we help the homeless in this way, along with an explanation.

Sympathy is the key word. When we see the disadvantaged, it's natural for us to feel pity for them; it's a moral obligation. This feeling is innate; it's in our hearts and blood. We live in communities, in collectives, not as individuals. This collective existence breeds the desire to help others who are less fortunate than us. This practice has roots dating back to the early stages of humanity. By evoking sympathy from others, the widespread practice of begging has become overwhelming, not through traditional means but through natural acts: we feel poor => we can't work and labor => we have to beg for money to eat. It's a natural behavior for survival.

However, begging isn't the only approach. That's why lottery tickets came into play. In my speculation, many beggars sell lottery tickets because they are a kind of "dope"—relying on luck, the tiny chance that a person might win the jackpot. This allure prompts people to buy lottery tickets, hoping that one day their lives will change, allowing them to live in wealth and affluence, capable of buying anything they desire. The eagerness for such a chance might be why lottery tickets are used in Vietnam, a developing country. Many people are desperate and attempt to seize a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity. Beggars, who might be successful in hitting two birds with one stone—finding a new way to "do business" and a chance to survive by begging—use lottery tickets. Consequently, lottery tickets have become a symbol of beggars in Vietnam.

Again, this is my own poorly developed theory.

Returning to sympathy, like the tip of an iceberg, what's visible might seem small, but what lies beneath is unknown. The same applies to the homeless (or beggars, or the individuals I mentioned earlier). Their appearance doesn't fully reflect who they are. I recall reading an article about a Westerner who posed as a beggar during the day and spent his nights at the bar. He profited from local people's attention to his "fake" situation. He claimed he was a stranded tourist in Vietnam and asked for money to "return to his homeland." However, he stayed longer than expected, raising suspicions. What is done in darkness eventually comes to light. He was exposed and had to pay a hefty fine. These terrifying, ugly scandals have led people to stop trusting beggars or lottery ticket sellers, severely impacting those who genuinely need help from others. How can people continue to encourage those who deceive their good hearts? It's difficult to avoid discussing this undoubtedly serious issue, but how can we distinguish between those genuinely in need and those seeking to exploit others' sympathy? How can we differentiate between those controlled by others, serving as puppets and subjected to labor only to cater to others' leisure, those trampled and abused because they are weak, uneducated, and impressionable? What about the children who are kidnapped, forced to use drugs to remain "high" all day, completely under the control of beggars, drug users, and criminals? Many more questions arise, revealing a complex, hidden, dangerous, twisted, and unresolved community existing beneath the surface of normality: a deep, unseen, and perilous underworld not understood by the common class in society, primarily the uneducated.

What do you expect at this point? A solution? NO. Nobody can contend with these "social devils." It's inevitable. No one can solve this. And no one ever will. Everything has two sides. If there are good people, it's not wrong to say there are bad people. Our hearts are no exception. Just because someone is warm-hearted doesn't mean they are entirely pure. There must be a dark side deep within their hearts, waiting for the right moment to surface. Society, much like wolves and rabbits, cannot always discern.

In the future, what can we do to address this ongoing, serious problem? Children are out there, uneducated and begging for food, begging for a decent life. Many others are unemployed, resorting to theft, degrading societal security. If more people end up like this, the problem will persist and even worsen. Consequently, society will have to deal with them more harshly, leading to class discrimination and hatred. What can we do?

It's sympathy that should be used smartly and judiciously. In life, feeling pity is something rare, just like many people. It's understandable why my parents acted the way they did towards those people.

BECAUSE THEY DON'T KNOW HOW TO DISTINGUISH BETWEEN PEOPLE THEY SHOULD HELP AND THOSE WHO SHOULDN'T BE HELPED.

IT'S TIME TO ANSWER THE QUESTION: SHOULD WE FEEL PITY FOR THOSE WHO ARE POORER AND FACE GREATER HARDSHIPS? BUT ALWAYS BE L.I.C.
