So I just had finished a book on Wattpad, and it was so crushing that I thought I should write something to feel lighter. Different from other books I’ve read on the site, this book ended with a slump of tragedy, and I was so impressed with this complexity of the matter of mental illness attached with the characters that I decided to write this. Enjoy reading!

The book I read was called “Building Bridges,” and it recalls a sorrowful story between two men — Silas and Derek. Silas was temporarily lost all of his memories after attempting to commit suicide, and Derek had his heartbeats drumming for him when he first saw the poor man at the hospital. Over time, Silas gradually regained his memories with the help of his boyfriend and later fiancée Derek, before the incident with the “help” of his second identity “Syris,” which was created to “protect” him from the trauma he had from his childhood — including being sexually abused at a very young age — messed up with his head again. Being under the manipulation of the identity, Silas did things that he could not redeem from killing and raping women, slaughtering animals, and almost have killed his brother. Despite Derek’s exdedication to help him out of his woeful abyss, Silas came to the final decision to kill himself to bring his mind at peace. Devastated by his fiancée’s death, on the supposed extensive second wedding anniversary that they both planned before Syris brought Silas away from him, Derek committed suicide beside his grave, as a promise to “see you later” he said before while holding Silas’ dead body. In an imaginative scenario, they both met at heaven, where Silas has freed from his cruel, wicked identity forever, and they lived happily ever after.

It’s hard to imagine what would happen to me if I were like Silas: always feeling untrusted and doubtful at everything, and still being scared under the vision of being unconsciously controlled by a completely different person inside the same mind. It’s completely restless how things have happened to him when bridges burn. He had nowhere to go but the bucolic woods where no one could see him fighting for his overpowering, for his freedom of his mind and his own identity over the wicked liar that tried to lure him into believing the one-sided sugarcoated beliefs of “I am you, so believe me.” Things seem so closed, but in reality, so far away. That something so close to Silas’ identity could be so different, so evil. He had cried almost every night because he was useless in front of his rival, his self. He tried to live for his lover, to please his “Turtle”, to open to his financée, to melt into his heart and become one with the one he was in love for, but he could not compromise with his seeming voice that accompanies most of his life. Can you imagine that just a moment in his childhood could cause so much trauma and uneasiness for the person in the future? It shows how much mental illness can impact on people. You may ignore this message, but if you read the book, assimilate how haunting the voice in Silas’ head can be (imaginary as it may seem, it somehow reflects accurately how people with Dissociative Identity Disorder feel with their illness).

Mental illness was underestimated in many countries. It’s the sad truth. They don’t care about mental illness because they think mental illness (including depression) can be cured by the win of the mind and over the practice of positive thinking. It’s not that easy. Forgetting trauma and making attempts to dump them is not easy-peasy. If it’s easy, 8 million people would not be dead by it every year, according to nib.gov. People need to know that some severe cases of mental illness have patients use medication for the rest of their lives, just like dealing with ARV (antiviral therapy drugs) for HIV patients, who have no choice but drinking drugs every day if they don’t want their condition to worsen. People love to mock mental illness patients that they were “weak,” but it took a lot of strength from them to admit themselves to be a patient. Illness does not come from a visible abnormality in the body; it is beyond the invisibility. That being a mentally ill person requires him to unbury the symptoms from his consciousness and shred the camouflage, it was bravery. Bravery because no one wants to be sick, but decided not to suppress what could be suppressed is more than just mere itself: it is revealing your identity and opening to your true self.

Having a mental illness is suppressing, sure, but it is also burdening to people around. You may have your wounds wound up, but seeing somebody going wild with no bruises on their body was way more insufferable. Bruises could not spread, but mental illness could: overthinking and negativity are signs of mental illness, and since this society has created many potential occasions for mental illness to develop, especially the youth, who are vulnerable in the harshness of life. People may say that there was no life depression in the past, but the fact that they don’t focus on it then in the past does not mean that there was no life depression in the history. PTSD, BPD, anxiety disorder, etc., have been on records as the aftermath of the war, and it shows how impactful these disorders could leave on these poor men. Mental illness has always been a hurting problem, an itch of the healthcare, and with the ongoing pressure of monetary bills, societal expectations and global issues, sooner or later adulthood is going to be severe to sustain. Silas and Derek might die for their love and their peace of mind, but the majority of the human race was not ready to commit suicide easily like that. That’s why depression in particular and mental illness was still going to last, and there’s nothing we could do to it if we continue to ignore it like our ancestors used to be. Be wiser and open to others as much as we can. People may die down for freedom (of mind), but their sacrifices would be way more meaningful if we start to respect the values they left for the later generations.

Such a sad ending as Building Bridges was, I am thankful because it awakens in me a sense of familiarity and interest in matters like this — things that people are rarely interested in.
