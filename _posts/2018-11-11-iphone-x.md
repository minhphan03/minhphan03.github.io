Tim Cook once said: "We were surprised somewhat that through all of this period of time that the iPhone X winds up as the [best] selling, most popular [device] for every week ... since the launch". In Q2, the quarter report stated that "...iPhones: 52.21 million, up 3 percent on the year-ago quarter..."

But how can a one-thousand-dollar phone can make the most successful year for Apple? Not tryna hard to say, but iPhone X did make a trend -- and its breakthrough (from the earlier iPhones) snatched hard to the customers' needs to change to a new device. Many analysers call this the iPhone super-cycle, and that's why these iPhone X (s) made it the big year for them -- normally people have the propensity to buy cheaper stuffs, but compared to the iPhone X, the ten-year-anniversary iPhone device, iPhone 8 (Plus) absolutely has nothing attractive. It is almost as the same as iPhone 7: Home Button, same design, horizontal dual camera (for the Plus version) and single camera (for the standard version) with the very familiar True-tone flash on the side, many people would make it clear that iPhone 7 is unarguably a better option for them (cheaper but almost the same features). The only two differences in design are the use of glass (for wireless charging) and the new gold. So what's the point here? The super-cycle customers need a phone they deserve to have, which is the iPhone X with no home button, the advanced, unique Face ID and a seamless, completely new design (they started to use stainless steel for this phone, a very superior feeling yo) and the edge-to-edge display with the OLED, HDR technologies. The only shortcoming is the notch, which is packed with the complicated machines of Face ID, but who cares? The whole phone is a completely redesign, and that is fascinating enough for users to upgrade for a new phone they had been waiting for. Over the rumors, they knew the time was coming, and the $1000 -- as it might be expensive, but a revolution from in to out made a "concept" that many people still thought it was affordable for a phone, just a small device that they may change for a new one continuously. That wasn't a scam, but the Apple marketing campaigns did well that time. 
 
As a result, iPhone X started to widespread everywhere. From the streets to the malls and restaurants, I found it constantly on many people's hands. Many students in my school also use them. The coverage was real, as it was abnormal for such an expensive gadget to have such a great reputation and sales success. This is, in my deepest feelings, a jealousy. Many people want this and I'm not set out. For me, the iPhone X is a symbol of wealth and power: you've got that and you're powerful -- in both means of purchasing it yourself of being given one. I love iPhone X because of those assumptions, too, and its package with so many advanced technologies and the complete difference from previous iDevice models. 
 
Every time I see someone use an iPhone X, my first expression are always "wow" and take a pause in what I'm doing in order to look at it eagerly. I know it's unreasonable and graceless and rude and unnecessary for me to beg my dad buying me one -- I have my own iPhone 7, but that the resistlessness that Apple was successfully created for the market users. A $1000 phone is unimaginable, and so create a desire in everyone to have it, to "show off". I love iPhones, and especially iPhone X. Nevertheless, the "show off" is not really my type, and if I have, I would use it as an normal iPhone, like my dad do with his device. On the other hand, MacBook - the PC model line of Apple, is more favorable to me, and more appealing. Compared to iPhone X, the MacBook Air - the most economical MacBook, now starts from $899, is way cheaper than iPhone X but including more technologies and bigger size. So, between a Mac and an iPhone, I would choose a Mac. But an iPhone X is still my favorite.
