Another year is about to end, and per tradition, it’s time for another year-end favorite songs list! You know I’m an avid music listener, and I associate a lot of the music I have listened to my experiences and how I have vibed with them throughout the year, so unlike last year, beginning from this year’s list and in the future, I will also provide a side story, if possible, to some of the songs I put on the lists! Ok, that’s all, so let’s dive in!

**15\. This Is A Life – Son Lux (Feat. David Bryne & Mitski)**

Whereupon Everything Everywhere All At Once’s critical and commercial success this year, it’s totally valid to talk about how the movie’s soundtrack adds value and grace to it. “This Is A Life” is a powerful duet of a ballad on the freedom of life and consummates the movie so well: there are so many ways one thing can happen in the multiverse, but we’re so lucky to live this life on our own, choose the things we believe that reflect ourselves, and learn the greatest lesson that to live and to make mistakes bring you the greatest experiences. Mitski and David Byrne’s powerful yet sensual voice blended so sweetly and translucently that it exudes peace, a tender, bittersweet closing moment of the grand masterpiece that is the movie itself.

Fav lyrics: *“This is a life / Slow and sudden miracles / View of other worlds from our window sills / With the weight of etеrnity at the speed of light”*

**14\. mùa hè 1994 (summer 1994) - Phùng Khánh Linh**

“mùa hè 1994” showcased Linh’s songwriter capabilities and vocal delivery rarely seen from any Vietnamese artist. The bittersweet, nostalgic sax dragging along the tune recaptures the melancholic feel of the lyrics and highlights the piano-ladden layer interweaving it. One of the best songs from her, period, with crisp lyrics elevating the mood and nostalgia that the song effortlessly enraptures and tries to capture (as suggested by the title itself). And of course for incredible songs like this, she deserves better, popularity-wise.

Fav lyrics: *“Vì sao anh còn buồn? / Vì sao quay lại tìm em? / Anh rong chơi phiêu lãng / Theo cơn mơ xa vắng / Giết chết trái tim mỏng manh”*

**13\. Maroon – Taylor Swift**

Putting Maroon here almost feels like a crime: it is so so so so so so good. Crisp and extremely brilliant lyrics, and her low vocal range is chef’s kiss. Instant favorite from my first listen, and “it’s a real fucking legacy”? Transcendental. Unprecedented. Unparalleled. Can we also talk about how Taylor referenced the color red in 7 different shades, namely ruby, burgundy, blood, scarlet, maroon, rust, and rose? I mean, who did it like her? Exactly!

Fav lyrics: *“You were standing hollow-eyed in the hallway / Carnations you had thought were roses, that’s us / I feel you, no matter what / The rubies that I gave up”*

**12\. Quiet – Camila Cambello**

Quiet is beautifully loud and stands out nicely in Camila’s third studio album, Familia. I may be very subjective here but the reason why I love it so much is because I can actually sing along to as it’s one of the English songs (there are some Spanish songs in the album that are quite catchy too but I don’t understand what she were singing about, so sue me). Also, I really dig the drums that scatter throughout the song, simple but effortlessly addictive. It haven’t been popping up to my mind lately but most of the songs that are on my rotation are fused with drums, some of which even settled into my top 3 of this year (which you will read later). Anyways, back to Camila. Well, it’s a cute song with cute lyrics, and one that you can put on when you’re in the shower to sing along when you’re in love (i’m not in love but i still put it on repeat, what now?)

Fav lyrics: *“And my, my mind’s made so much noise for so long and it’s gone / ‘Cause when you kiss me, it’s quiet (Yeah, yeah) / Oh, it’s quiet”*

**11\. PURE/HONEY – Beyoncé**

Pure/Honey: Beyonce really came to prove what a queen she was huh? A dance record inspired by the Black community was signature Queen Bee, and Pure/Honey was an exemplar for this. From vouge and house music’s heavy influences to Moi Renee’s “Honey” and Kevin Aviance’s October 1996 track “Cunty,” no wonder why it’s so prime, so lively, grand, and so aptly cultured. Pure/Honey, as a fusion of Black excellence, is addictive, classy, sexy, and full of quirks. She is bloody coming for that Album of The Year for RENAISSANCE next year, no joke. 

Fav lyrics: *“It should cost a billion to look that good / But she make it look easy ’cause she got it / You can find the one when the tempo’s good / Four, three, I’m too fucking busy”*

**10\. God Turn Me Into A Flower – Weyes Blood**

From the artist of the album that is currently featured in the Music Corner section at this point of this post, “God Turn Me Into A Flower” is hauntingly beautiful, sad, quiet, and simultaneously loud. If you read the lyrics of the song, it sounds like Weyes Blood conveys a feeling of resignation and wistfulness (“It feels so good to be put down”). Some synths here and there, and strings on the full instrumentals that (somehow) gratefully occupy two-thirds of the length of the song are some of the shiniest highlights of the album. It creates wonders and wanders deep into what I call an endless nature labyrinth of my mind, where birds chirping and water flowing sound effects created a soundscape unmatched and detached from the outside world. Whenever I listened to the song, I had to stop everything to let Mering (Weyes Blood’s real name) lure me into her world and let me feel the melancholy, wistfulness, and simplicity of the nothingness and nature. Her voice was bliss and serenading, making me feel like I was mentally falling into an abyss, or a folklore of sorts, because it was just magically unreal! It really embodies the title of the album, because in the darkness and gloominess of present life and unnecessary meaning we attach on everything, hearts aglow when we allow ourselves to have (by willingly listen to her album ♡).

Fav lyrics: *“As long as I stand to face the crowd / To know my name, to know its sound / It’s good to be soft when they push you down / Oh, God, turn me into a flower”*

**9\. Midnight Rain – Taylor Swift**

Midnight Rain, which many Swifties regarded as the theme track of her smashing, milestone 10th studio album Midnights, is sad, haunting and reminiscent. It’s everything Taylor we’ve loved and something new. The “something new” consists of distorted vocals reminded us was jarring yet so fitting, an absence of a bridge (thankfully we still have an outro), but it’s undeniably classic Taylor: sharp, cutting-glass lyrics, deep vocals and high notes at “midnight rain”s and pianos layers that have done anything wrong before. I also love when the instrumental effects (the “twisted” pianos and buzzes) appeared throughout the song and generated a unique audio-sphere that replicated “midnight” troubles so beautifully. It’s reminiscent in how it wholeheartedly reflects the album theme “13 sleepless nights of my life” by one simple lyric “and I never think of him except on midnights like this”

Fav lyrics: *“I guess sometimes we all get / Some kind of haunted, some kind of haunted / And I never think of him / Except on midnights like this”*

**8\. Anxious – Carly Rae Jepsen**

Carly Rae Jepsen must be the only musician I’ve known so far to put so much bangers into B-sides or discarded them into bonus tracks, and “Anxious” is the strongest evidence of it all (I’m looking at you, “Never Get To Hold You”). The crazy bass line is an instant highlight of the album, reinforcing Carly’s reputation in the pop girl music scene as an consistent bop maker. I mean, “The Loneliest Time” is just too good and so brimmed with masterpieces that she have to cut “Anxious” out the standard tracklist, and it’s understandable, but at the same time I feel like it deserves a greater exposure to the casual listener who would definitely overlook songs that are branded “bonus” in the album. I would also believe her if she thought that maybe it sounds too much like her old stuff, being synth-pop and all, but Carly’s been known for it and why not showing some embrace for it?

Fav lyrics: *“But you didn’t even notice (Even notice) / I was jealous of your company (Company) / Ooh, boy, you got a reputation (Reputation), mm-mm”*

**7\. Grapefruit – Tove Lo**

Tove Lo is undeniably underrated. What else can I say? She simply is, considering how much effort and contributions she has done throughout the years, from her first and only commercial hit Habits (Stay High) to sensual club bangers “disco tits” and “hey you got drugs,” she is a force of nature, a powerful and effortless one. With “Grapefruit” and the new 5th studio album “Dirt Femme,” she continues to dominate the pop scene, albeit quietly, with powerful vocals, energetic beats drenching sad and desperate lyrics, which, in this song, is about eating disorder, mental health, and body dysmorphia. The electric soundscape it creates made me wanna dance on her confessions, which is laughingly funny (I meant to say ridiculous, but it’s her artistic choice and there’s nothing wrong with that).

Fav lyrics: *“One, two, grapefruit / How am I back here again? / Three, four, lose more / I know my mirrors are lyin’ / Five, six, hate this / Take back the body I’m in”*

**6\. Minor Feelings – Rina Sawayama**

Music is one of the rare topics whose ties with politics are almost non-existent — unless you create a song to hype up some random campaign that fills with awkward lyrics and unnatural amount of patriotism vibes, or to declare how aware you are of social issues. But “Minor Feelings” is about racism, and it feels effortless. It hits close to home, and it does not feel out of place at all in Rina Sawayama’s sophomore album “Hold The Girl.” As the album discusses therapy, self-ignorance, and self-acceptance on past trauma, Sawayama’s self identities come into place for dissection. As the title of the song suggests, it is about micro aggressions as an Asian in an Eurocentrism culture. Her voice gradually becomes heavier and more exasperated as she sang, demonstrating the buildup of micro-aggressions that minorities have to face in everyday life, until she bursts out at the end and faltered into self-defeat.

Fav lyrics: *“All my life, you took me for a fool / All my life, you told me what to do / All these minor feelings / Are majorly getting to me now”*

**5\. American Teenager – Ethel Cain**

The title “discovery of the year” must go to Ethel Cain, with only two songs needed to triumph this honorary award: “American Teenager” and “A House In Nebraska,” which will be discussed later. Wistful and tragic, this song is a reminiscence to the ever famous American Dream, which is hitting too close to home for me right now. Like many of my all-time favorites, I was not able to warm to it at first, like it felt flat somehow. Give it a couple of more tries and I got hooked way too fast: the breathless vibes and the natural desperation feel of her vocals, the rawness and angst of them, it just… fits. I once commented on a Pitchfork’s low-score review of her album “Preacher’s Daughter” that this song alone made this album a 9/10. I mean, I love the coming of age theme a little bit too much, unashamedly. Can you blame me?

Fav lyrics: *“The neighbor’s brother came home in a box / But he wanted to go, so maybe it was his fault / Another red heart taken by the American dream”*

**4\. Go Find Yourself Or Whatever – Carly Rae Jepsen**

If there is one thing that Carly Rae Jepsen cannot do, it’s making a bad song. Like’ I’m not even kidding. All of her songs are so freaking good, and I don’t care if she didn’t think they were, the production on all of her songs are always on point. Known for making pop bangers, it would seem weird to single out “Go Find Yourself or Whatever” over many equally instant classics on her newest project “The Loneliest Time.” Only a true Carly stan would understand how magical her ballads can be, from her “Tug of War” days to the fan-favorite All That. It’s so nice to see ballad Carly again in a very instrumentally creative (synthesizer, drums, mandolin, and sitar?) slow tempo song for a change, and honestly this one is one of the best ballads I’ve heard for a while. The song also brings up a windy, airy country vibe around itself, which really suits the subject of the song itself, a lover who is losing himself in sorrow and uncertainty.

Fav lyrics: *“I wake up hollow / You made me vulnerable / So go find yourself or whatever / I hope it treats you better than I could do / And I’ll wait for you”*

**3\. Constant Repeat – Charli XCX**

There is a comment on the comment section of Charli XCX’s “Constant Repeat” audio video about how they really loved addictive songs that mention phrases about “repeating” or “looping” because it is a real amusing manifestation. Nevertheless, I don’t think Charli has to do witchcraft or “manifest” for “Constant Repeat” to be a global hit — truth is, it has a very chance to be, thanks to (degradingly) her label Atlantic. Charli herself is way overqualified to not being able to drop a crazy cr@ck-lined beat, and the outro at the end proves it. Just a single line “Got me on repeat” repeatedly sang damped in a crazy bass line, drums, and autotune, and whatever Lotus IV had in their brain to produce such a perfect record was enough for me to put this song on play for an unhealthy amount during my trip to San Francisco last March.

Fav lyrics: *“When you’re lonely (When you’re lonely) / Am I ever on your mind on constant repeat? / Is it me that you want?”*

**2\. A House In Nebraska – Ethel Cain**

I stumbled upon “A House In Nebraska” on a Reddit discussion posted on r/popheads about “songs that enthralled you” and the song was recommended. I remembered so vividly how I have put it into my “listen later” playlist and played it as I waited at a bus stop and how it changed my life ever since. If I have to name a song that has haunted me for the longest time, it has to be this song (though “Pretty When You Cry” by Lana Del Rey comes super close). It started pretty slow like any slow ballad, but Ethel Cain’s vocals (again, I know I talk so much about vocals but there’s no denying that vocals set up an important part in the soundscape of the songs themselves)  and the lower piano registers immediately piqued my curiosity. You have to witness how my jaw dropped when the electric guitar instrumentals picked up at the bridge/outro of the song that instantly reminded me of “Happier Than Ever” by Billie Eilish, another masterpiece that I have put on my last year-end list. Unlike Eilish’s, Ethel’s lyrics is way more stirring and depressive, which I am unafraid to say that this is what I really dig into. We love talking about death here babes.

Fav lyrics: *“Your mama calls me sometimes / To see if I’m doing well / And I lie to her / And say that I’m doing fine / When really I’d kill myself / To hold you one more time”*

**1\. Holy (‘Til You Let Me Go) – Rina Sawayama**

There is just something about this song that is so unlike anything I have heard before, europop and all, since my music taste is not that diverse to listen to those kind of stuff (and I’m too lazy to discover new music, sue me). There’s a reason why I love this song so much, because it came to me on the very last days I stayed in Vietnam this summer. The song sounds so grand, so broad, so glistening, and so sad-dance-bop — ugh whatever terms I have for this — but now it also carries a wistful feel. I remember listening to it riding my motorbike home after my last friend group hangout and screaming the lyrics in the Saigon night winds on the vacant streets at 11pm, trying to forget how soon I have to leave behind that summer and move on with my life. The chorus of the song popped off so hard it was unbelievably how catchy it was. On the other hand, religion trauma, the subject of the song, may not be relatable to me at all since I kind of dissociate myself from religion long enough to not experience much trauma from it, but it’s trauma anyway and I can list some trauma resulted from cultural experiences from the top of my head. But why should it be a thing? It’s my personal best song of the year already, it does not matter why I have to come to lengths to justify my obsession with it. It is just the way it is.

Fav lyrics: *“Oh, you saw a light starting to shine / Wanted it only for your eyes / Older and wise, God on your side / (I was the martyr who paid for your life)”*
