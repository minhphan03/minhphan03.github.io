Please prepare for yourself before you read this piece, because it's nothing but a rant: so disorganized. I wanna keep it as raw as possible like a stream of thoughts. Of course I would edit out some words and phrases, but the structure is kept as is.

I stumbled upon a phrase, catch-22, when I was watching Soul last year. Yes, it took me almost a year to post a review for a movie because I had to come back and forth to edit things out (although sometimes I don't, especially when I have spent too much time working on drafting it). For some reason I didn’t like the movie itself the first time, so I rewatched it and turned out it wasn’t so bad. I was also curious why the main character was named 22 because it was such a specific number, so I looked that up and turned out one of the theories was that she was based on a play on the phrase. Another theory was that Soul was the 22th Pixar original feature-length film. Anyways, it was the catch-22 phrase that piqued my interest, and instead of finishing the movie, I’m writing these words down so animatedly just to remember what to jot down later!

Back to the main topic: the reason why I paused everything to write this piece stemmed from the definition of what a catch-22 is. According to Merriam-Webster Dictionary, it's "a problematic situation for which the only solution is denied by a circumstance inherent in the problem or by a rule." Sounds like something that I am dealing with at the moment! Does it ring a bell in your head what issue I am hyper-obsessive about? Bingo! It's the job application process!

Well I sound like a scratched record keeping talking about this matter all the time, but if my therapist is not tired of me being a whiner all the time I don't think you would start to feel tired yet. I mean, I bet she was tired but she did not say it out loud, because you need to be discreet and show some decency and sympathy if you want to keep your job and not lose your customers. She was, in a way, in a better situation than me, and I know that this is not the right time to say that out loud like this, but I feel like I'm so disconnected from this situation. She might only be my only lifeline, my sole mental support, but I am not willing to show how dogmatic and rigid-thinking I am. People say that's so unfair that job applications are so grinding and mentally exhausting, and I understand that I am not the only one, and there are thousands of people out there who are also silently suffering from all these mental barriers. To be honest, there's no point lamenting over something you cannot do anything about. You just have to get used to the game and play by its rules. In the words of Taylor Swift, you need the mentality of *"I can do it with a broken heart."*

To give you an example of how rough recent times had been for me, I just got an email about failing my interview for the intern position I ALREADY GOT LAST YEAR. I don't know I'm fucking disappointed because did my mentor feel like I'm not worth it to return to this position, and even with the trust that I built with her I'm still not worth it? Literally the worst feeling ever, and what do I have to do even now? Did I say something in my interview that tip her off? Maybe there's better people than me, and maybe I'm not that an interesting person to contribute to her work anymore. I'm growing out of this. In the back of my mind this is a safe bet, that I believe I would garantee my spot to return as she invited me so, but damn it hurts when you got rejected and even she did not say a single word about it. I sent her an email and she ghosted me; what am I even doing now? For all she knew I wanted so desperately something to move forward, to gain some experience even it is the smallest thing, so that I can at least update my resume and make it seem like it is worth all of my agony and woes barely functioning as a human, let alone a student. Something to get out of this deadly cycle of a catch-22. Until then, I still have to try, to do it with a broken heart, but damn if it isn't so heartbreaking. 

In the same song, Taylor sang: *"I'm so depressed I act like it's my birthday every day / I cry a lot but I am so productive, it’s an art."* This feels like a manic episode when you are too numb from the mental pain you keep going on even if you know that you need to let go. It's kind of my coping mechanism, quite honestly: whenever I get a rejection, I just mass apply to a bunch of other jobs to numb that abject, horrible aftertaste away. I became productive because I could not bear feeling numb. I do it so I would feel like I'm not useless, and hiding the disgusting denial of how heavily something like that affects me mentally. Now that I face the truth that time is ticking for an international student to find job and stay in the US, I'm just so fucking desperate for something. I know my therapist empathizes my situation, but she is in no position to help me with any of the excruciating matter. She is just there, supports me. I am grateful for her, and even if I don't remember a single thing she told me during these therapy sessions, the emotions that crept in and stuck in my mind just listening to her dissecting my turbulent and disordered state of mind give me chills. If it isn't for her these manic episodes will surely continue. I guess at the end you cannot change things, so take everything with a grain of salt.

This idea of catch-22 or intellectualizing my feelings of rejection all boils down to the fact that I am a severe overthinker. I never let my feelings dictate, because I used to believe that crying is the worst thing I could imagine. I also just haven't cried for a long time. I always reserve crying to moments where I am at the most bottom of all botttom moments. The moments where I relinquish the idea of thinking, which is scary. That's why I never got used to it. Depressed as I am, I can't cry that easily. I feel like I should blame toxic masculinity for this lack of emotional release, as its truthers regard crying as a weakness. The typical patriarchal society seems to follow these ideas very well, so I naturally just stopped doing that as I grew up. My therapist Kat told me I should cry more, which was lowkey funny, because if everything she said was true, at least my heart would be lighter and I would not think about these stuff as much. She said that crying released endorphin to calm me down. But I just couldn't. I just overthink. I still do. I overthink because I have a need to take control of my future, and don't you think with how possessive people are it's not that easy to let it go and let fate decide how it is going to be. I cannot guarantee that I can just drop that habit like a hot potato, but subconsciously I think I'm making some slow progress. I admitted to her that I felt like the fact that I go tback to square one did not seem like it, but to her as long as I did something worth trying, it was not a waste of time. And even if I got a broken heart dealing with this shitshow of a market, at least I tried, and I will do it again and again because it is the only course of action I can perform in my power. To mass apply like thousands of ohter applicants out there looking for somethng. We're all in for a lonely, humiliating experience, and I feel dreaded a lot of the times comparing myself to my friends who already got their jobs figured out, but I guess I have always known I am in for a long ride. Don’t blame yourself, blame the game. But everyone’s stuck in the same catch-22 game and suddenly pitying yourself seems small. 

I have gone to therapy for over a year now. I don't know how I survived without it before, but I am glad I sought help. I was diagnosed with mild depression. Given the past few months of volatile emotions, that was damn sure to be the case, and I was damn sure needed help. In hindsight all of this overthinking and catch-22 intellectualization stem from the fact that my actual problem was that I lacked a somebody to confide all of my weakness in. I sure have friends, but they are not paid to listen to my issues. Therapists do, and my school insurance covers all therapy sessions and I took advantage the hell out of it. My current therapist, Kat, was such an inspiring woman and probably one of my favorite people ever. I know that our relationship is strictly professional and based on monetary terms and contracts, but the core of the matter is I just needed somebody to truly listen and give me advice on what to do. She's like a mentor to me. 

Sometimes, I feel envy towards people whose mental health is good enough that they don't need therapists. Maybe they had jobs and they had better life security than I had lol. I hate that my mind is too weak and I am so easily discouraged and swayed away from the whirlwinds of life, but in a life that is so unpredictable, expectations and hopes rarely last. Kat told me to instead hope and expact bad things happening and intellectualize, try to absorb the accept the bad feelings. Feel instead of think. For example, I told her how I grew agitated and disappointed in myself after realizing I did not enjoy a friend-coworker's company like I thought I would. Sounds like a trivial matter, right? Probably, but to me it was a broken 'hope'. I always wanted to hang out with her for a very long time because she was a super cool queer and we vibed so much at work, but we went to watch a movie and after that she went on her phone full-time and left me feeling like a burden. Like I was wasting her time with her friends or something. I'm in my head again, and that's for sure 99% not what she felt about our hangout. I told Kat all of that and all she said was "how do you feel?" It was not the first time she asked that, obviously. I bet on my life that's their signature conversation starter, but somehow I never acknowledged that she was applying consulting techniques into these ubiquitous small-talk-vibe questions. I took some time to come up with a response to that question. I honestly couldn't describe it. As an international student, I struggle with words a lot of the times, and then it finally dawned on me why I could not vibe with that friend: we just don't have much in common. She's a native and comes from a culture that is so drastically different from mine, and my barrier language does not help. I did try, and Kat told me that it was always valuable to be able to try and learn from experiences. Then she proceeded to ask if I knew what was the most effective to learn. I did not. The answer was to feel, to naturally memorize these painful realities so that one will remember not to make those mistakes again. I was hung up on those words, because I either tried to rationalize and blame others on what happened, or dismissed them. I fixtated on swallowing my problems and forcefeeding my suppressed feelings so that when another one comes, I bursted like a popping bubble and ended up dealing with the aftermath. 

I told her that was too much, and I did not have time for all of those mental processing. She retorted, advising me to begin with baby steps. She pointed out how I said "whatever" a lot during our sessions and encouraged me to drop those verbal cues. That was my subconscious speaking, and once I learn to re-engineer it I would be able to learn how to feel and truly take in the fact that I am struggling with my career right now. I need to get off my high horse and get my ass up to accept that bad things always happen and if you want to grow wise, you have to fail and stumble upon dead-ends. Like this old guy I met when I was volunteering for tax preparation: he escaped Vietnam when the Fall of Saigon happened, stranded upon the sea after the Malaysian government marooned and refused to rescue them, until he ended up in Italy and eventually the United States. It was a dangerous, hopeless time for him; however, as the saying goes there's always light at the end of a tunnel. It's funny how I never get tired of thinking shit like this! Just constant self-pitying and self-consolation. An immaculate pattern of overthinking. If overthinking is a competition and I am the runner-up then no one can be the champion. I did not explicitly say that out loud to her, but she knew how overthinking wacked me. That's why it was so hard for me to let my feelings in, because it's more tolerable to not see myself crumbling and succumb to weakness -- some patriarchial, hypermasculinity shit that we have been echoing around. If I can just swallow everything in, pushing through my rejections, and blindly go over things I have to do, I can do it with a broken heart. But now I'm not so sure anymore.

So here I am, writing this entry, to remind myself to focus on my feelings and let them all out. Learn to tolerate my messy side and develop a carthatic yet ugly emancipation of my psychological being so that I could exhaust any sadness and move on. 

P/S: Here's a note from my therapist the day we had our last session two days ago. Yesterday was my graduation day. To be frank, I was skeptical to post something somewhat private on here, but this is a great way for me to remember it. To Kat, from the bottom of my heart thank you so much for everything you did to me.
