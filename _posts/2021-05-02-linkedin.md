I created my LinkedIn account three days ago, and it was an experience, to be honest. I didn’t know what to expect when I created that account, because, for a seventeen-year-old boy like me, I used to think it is too soon to create an account for the sake of finding jobs. Is it always so hard to find a job that you have to create an account just for that? I mean, I don’t object to people praising and telling others to use LinkedIn or similar professional networking platform, because a resource is a resource, and nonetheless useful, if not to one, can be to another. But because there was no harm in creating one, I hesitantly did. I was a student, so internships and part-time jobs are necessary to acquire some precious experience for future references.

There were a lot of things to put in the profile: resumes, connections, references, featured articles and links, skills, etc. It is exactly an online, digital version of a resume: more informative, intuitive and interactive. Hence, it is no stranger that I put in a lot of time for that.

And then it comes to networking and creating connections. Obviously, I started at zero, and when I used my email to look up for some possible people to connect with, I found a lot of my friends having LinkedIn! Turned out I was not the only one, and being the insecure self that I am, looking at their huge amounts of connections irks me up. I’m sure I didn’t have enough 90+ connections on this site because my circle is tight and impact. Or that most of my friends didn’t know its existence or they didn’t bother to make one, bearing the same assumption as I had some time ago. I felt miserable. I wish I had done it sooner, so I could establish more connections.

Coming up next was the “skills” section. I had to fill in skills that I found relevant to my profile. I was studying Computer Science at my college right then (and still am), so it was natural that I would put my course languages on there. And as expected, it does not stop there: I had to prove that I had those skills — by taking assessment quizzes from LinkedIn itself. They were not hard, but they touched some of the topics I never heard before. I had a Microsoft PowerPoint assessment test failed one time and I could only take it twice, so in the most pathetic attempt, I searched some of the questions online (be quick though, because each question only stayed for one minute and a half). I did pass the quiz in that final attempt though, fortunately. But then later I found out that there was this whole GitHub repository that answered all the questions in the quiz, and unsurprisingly I felt disappointed. I mean, what’s the whole point of these tests when you can easily search for solutions with a simple click?

It’s unhinged not to discuss resumes, because despite how much effort you put into the profile, everyone will eventually ask for your resume, and you will be cast aside if you don’t have a digital copy of your resume to attract possible headhunters. Fortunately, I had already downloaded a resume template I got by browsing through Facebook two years ago (and didn’t even bother to do anything with it), so I opened Google Drive and got dirty with the most intimidating obstacle ever. I say “intimidating” because through many workshops I attended on job hunting and resumes, you have to put in a lot of creative control and time to have a resume (or curriculum vitae, or portfolio, your choice) to stand out from hundreds or thousands of other candidates in the competitive job market nowadays. In short, it was a painstaking process. It was no joke. I constantly open tabs and tabs of thesaurus webpages to find “cool” action verbs to put into my resume, and even then I couldn’t judge for myself whether the resume was good enough. I planned on having my supervisor review it sometime next week, but that’s a story for another time.

I belatedly realized that I have caught the LinkedIn Imposter Syndrome. But then again, isn’t it possible (I’m not bragging myself or anything) that somebody else may feel intimidated by my profile? After all, we are all awful, pathetic individuals in this chaotic whirlwind of trying to impress headhunters, and unintentionally hurting others’ self-confidence as a side effect.
