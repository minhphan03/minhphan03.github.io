My first solo trip to Thailand was not what I would call a disaster, but there were some things that could have gone better. I mean, I should have asked somebody to come with me because sometimes solo travel is kind of lonely, i.e., my aunt. At the same time, though, it’s a lesson of maturity, so I’m glad I made the decision and embraced the challenge. Overall, I enjoyed it. Definitely would do it again!

This is a summary of what happened:

<h3>Day 1</h3>
Arrived at Don Mueang International Airport. Took the bus to CentralWOrld. Thankfully the conductor knew a little bit of English, so I could pay for the fare easily when I asked her how much was the trip. Waited for another one to take me back to my hotel but after almost 20 minutes it hadn't arrived yet. Once I decided to take the light rail back instead, the bus appeared at the stop and I was too tired to run back and catch it. Went to the light rail station, super confused on how to get on the platform. Figured it out. Got to the hotel. There were so many tourists and non-Asians on the subway I was so surprised. The hotel receptionist was kind of rude but who cares— at this point I just want to settle my stuff down and prepare for going to downtown, which was farther than I thought. Went back to downtown. Took this river boat (eh, some kind of ferry, I’d say) to Wat Arun Ratchawararam Ratchawaramahawihan (literally copy-pasted this name omg) temple across the river. Paid 100 baht just to take some random pictures and left because I wasn’t a big fan of any of that. Thought of trying a tuktuk to another place, but thought I could do it some other time. Went to Lhong 1919. It was ok, kind of cool tho. Thought of going to Chinatown for dinner but the town was right across the river and if you take public transportation it’d take an hour so I called a GrabBike instead. He was very cool and drove so fast lol I get it now. Arrived in Chinatown. Ate Pad Thai. It was ok but not that different from my favorite place in HCMC. Well, their egg-covered style was super cool though! Searched for Thai dessert next, but all they got was fruit juice stands and ice cream rolls. Found a decent one but not really Thai. Tested my social anxiety and stroked a conversation with this Poland gay eating next to me. He left Bangkok the next day, and apparently he hasn’t visited a proper gay bar so that was awkward. Went back to the hotel. Thinking of watching a movie, so I turned on the TV and oh so conveniently the last guest forgot to log out their Netflix so I decided to watch a super boring movie while I got down on my homework. It was “To All the Boys I’ve Loved Before” part 2. Terrible plot, but I didn’t care too much as I was busy crying (figuratively) trying to read and understand what the hell the assigned readings for my queer studies class were talking about. Fell asleep.


<h3>Day 2</h3>
Planning to go to Ayutthaya early in the morning, but I overslept until 10 a.m. Hesitated if I should go but decided to pull the trigger because I hate to ruin my plans over trite accidents. Made it to the train station, delighted on the way there because I got an opportunity to sit on the subway for the first time. Didn’t know it would also be the last time. The station was like ten times as big as the one in HCMC, judging from outside, and empty as hell. Paid a ticket for 20 baht, like that was crazy cheap compared to the light rail. Bought two Dunkin' Donuts, because screw Krispy Kreme. No support for genocide enablers. Aboard the train, sitting in the plainest, barest, rustiest, old-school car possible in the area reserved for monks but there were no monks so we could sit. I guess that’s what you get for 20 baht. Talked to these two Japanese guys whose English was so incomprehensible I couldn’t make out the words “college” and “colleague.” Never saw them again after arriving in the city. Ate my two donuts after leaving the train. Crossed the street. Listened to this guy trying to sell me a tuktuk tour for 700 baht, then let him down slowly. Rented a bike for 60 baht. Felt so smart for the decision. Crossed the river with the bike, feeling dumb again because I should have rented one after crossing it. Lines of rental stores wrapped the street to the pier. Biked around. It was okay. Stopped at some distinct spots to take some perfunctory photos so I wouldn’t feel bad for coming. Had coke. They still sold coke in glass bottles, which was pleasantly interesting. Haven’t seen any in so long. Bought another melon-flavored slushy with boba. Would be down to have more, but the portion was so small though. Detoured and explored this endless narrow road, feeling good because it was void of tourists. Made it back. Visited another empty ruin of a temple. Decided to visit the museum, but it was also empty as hell, so I decided to call it a day. Returned the bike. Lost the bike chain somewhere, so I paid another 60 baht. Returned to Bangkok. Went to the wrong exit, so I had this guard telling me to go another way, but he didn’t know I was trying to exit, not enter any train, so I walked away despite him signaling me to come back. Went to Jodd Fairs, thinking it was a cool place to get some street food, but it turned out to be gentrified as heck. Ate pineapple rice with some grilled skewers. Walked around for half an hour back to another light rail station because I couldn’t justify overspending my budget; I barely had 300 baht left in my pocket. Traffic at 7 p.m. was crazy. Lines and lines of traffic painted red my whole walk. Bought a Starbucks cup on the way there (as much as I hate to support them, I have a dedicated collection of their “you are here” collection. Screw capitalism always, but damn if it wasn’t so irresistible). Asked the cashier/barista if they still had the planner, but they sold out months ago. I asked my sister to buy me one in Vietnam, but I was unsure if it was still available on the shelves. It’s been two months. Anyway, paid for the cup (more like an ornament because it was too small to be usable, and it had a small bow on the handle) with Pay because that small cup cost more than 300 baht, and I needed cash to pay for my trip back to the hotel. Went back to the hotel. Bought a strawberry slushy from a 7-eleven store at the station and asked them to reuse my cup of coke I bought in the morning instead. Felt proud for doing good for the environment. Unbeknownst to me, there was an exact store across the street that I could have bought it from without crossing back to my hotel, but well what was done has been done. Watched a movie. Fell asleep.

<h3>Day 3</h3>
Woke up earlier than the previous day, but only because I felt like I need to do something with my college coursework piling up because I did not do anything effective before that. I emailed my professors to excuse my late arrival in in-person classes, then read some notes from my applied mathematics course which I had absolutely no clue what he was writing about (that was so messed up). Showered. Worked out a little bit in my room. Put on my favorite outfit and got ready to go out. Feeling extra nice that day, so I decided to take the bus to the Grand Palace, but it turned out to be the worst decision ever. It took two hours, and I almost missed the entry because it closed at 3:30 and I arrived there at 3:27, but luckily there were still a lot of people coming in so I could safely release my breath. Bought my ticket for 500 baht which I thought was costly as hell, but damn it for the experience, it's not like I could go to Thailand all the time. Took some nice pics of myself. The place was so crowded, though. Definitely not my thing. I hate crowds. Planned to take a tuktuk to Warehouse 30, which was recommended by someone on Reddit, but he charged me 500, way over my budget, and I wasn't in the mood to bargain, so I decided to take the ferry instead. The conductor was hella aggressive, and I was so confused because she just kept pushing me back into the line without any explanation, and I doubt she understood much English so I just waited and waited. For 20 baht, you got to endure some things. Still got onto the ferry and landed safely at the destination pier, though. The 'warehouse' was 3 minutes walking from there. It was a huge letdown… aside from a small museum (it’s not even a museum, more like an exhibition/gallery) there was nothing, so I took some cool pictures of a Jesus-lookalike parting his gown to reveal his butt and left. Waited at this bus stop to go to CentralWorld next because I promised my sister to buy her some sweets, but got onto the wrong bus so I ate dinner somewhere instead. Took another bus to CentralWorld. Didn't know there was a supermarket in there so I left to the huge Big C superstore across the street. Called my mom while shopping. Searching everywhere for this cookie brand for my friend to no avail, so I paid for the sweets and left. Bought this durian ice cream downstairs. It had durian sprinkles on top too, that’s nutty. Felt sad because I couldn’t visit Lumphini Park in time because it was 7 already and I doubted I could see anything. Decided to hit the gay bars, which people said were fun so I went to Silom 2 and 4 expecting a lot of gay activities, turned out it was just bars and bars of white people chitchatting. Again, took the bus because I was done with the tuktuks. Walked through the alleys feeling like a catwalk model, lol. Everyone was eyeing me like I was an alien. I was the only one walking through it, so I guess that was somewhat weird. Did they expect me to sit down in some bar and wait for gay orgies to unfold before my eyes? Saw a street sign for a boytoy (I’d assume nakey nakey wink-wink) show. Low-key fascinated. Got bored real fast, so I wandered and visited this street market nearby and bought a dish of mango sweet sticky rice instead. Went home after lingering around a drag show bar because it started at 9 and I still had stuff in my hands that I didn’t want to carry with me back home at 11. Bought boba with brown sugar. Watched another movie. Regrets creeping in because what if I could lose my virginity at that sketchy place? Maybe I should have downloaded some hookup app. Fell asleep.

<h3>Day 4</h3>
Woke up again. Took a shower. Watched another movie because I had nothing to do until checkout time at 12 p.m. Checked out and returned the keys. Went to the airport. Checked in at the airport to Chiang Mai. Ate at KFC because I didn’t have breakfast earlier, and also because I haven’t had KFC in so long. 240 baht was not a bad price for two drumsticks and two wings. The wings were so spicy, though, but thankfully the soda cup was large enough to save me. Planned to buy Krispy Kreme again after that but figured I should save money so bye. Boarded the plane. Arrived in Chiang Mai. Exited the airport. Waited for this bus to arrive but it never did so I took a taxi back to the hotel. The hotel (guesthouse) was crappier than I thought. I should have done more research… By the way, it was paid by cash in person and I didn’t read the agreement so that was my loss. Anyways, back to the crappy room: the bathroom fan screeched so loud every time I turned on the lights, so I showered in the dark for the rest of the stay. There was also no AC and fridge. The only good thing was the super gigantic bed and great location in downtown. The hosts were also nice, though, and I talked to them to register for a hiking tour to Doi Ithanon the next day. Took a light nap after *********ing. Walked to this weekly Sunday night market. Bought some dim sum and chicken satay, but the soy sauce spilled on my favorite white shirt so I ran back to the guesthouse to change and took that shirt to the nearest laundry. Lost my wallet somewhere on the way. Everything went awry. Thankful for the AirTag I put into the wallet before going on the trip, I followed its direction to the lost-and-found camp at the end of the street. The device kept losing the connection so it took me 20 minutes to get there. This is not a commercial for the thing but damn I was so thankful I put it into my wallet it saved my life or else my mama would smack me on the ass for losing my wallet countless times now. Saying thank you in my head to the good person who decided not to steal the rest of the money I had in there. Rushed to the laundromat. Paid 50 baht for washing and ironing a shirt, but honestly I would pay more than that if they wanted because I could not endure watching my favorite shirt get an ugly stain on it any more I already had my fair share of trauma. Planned to go to this ladyboy show outside of the old town area, but it was too early again, so I walked back and came across this tattoo parlor with a starting price of 1000 baht. I was so excited I decided to have a smiling face tattoo on top of my own hand tattoo near my left shoulder. Had a nice chat with the owner. Felt super grateful for him. Gave him a stellar, extolling review on Google because he deserved everything and more. Went back home and watched another movie. Attempted to finish my queer studies reading but had enough and dropped it to pick another introduction to queer studies class. Worked on my economics homework while talking to my friend. Fell asleep.

<h3>Day 5</h3>
Woke up at 6:30 because the tour guide would pick me up at around 7:30. By 7:30, they hadn’t arrived and the hosts hadn’t woken up, so I attempted to shoot a WhatsApp chat but the guide was there right when I was about to send it. Picked up some other people on the way. There was this rough-looking French guy I thought was legit homeless. Some solo travelers just like me. Another group of 5 people who I later knew were on a work trip (in Chiang Mai out of all places??) and stayed at InterContinental because their company paid for everything. Around 15 people in total. The shuttle bus stopped at this rest stop out of nowhere. Had a rice dish with pork belly and some young bamboo root stew. Vietnamese vibes all again. Back on the shuttle. Visited these double temples that represented the king and the queen. Took some cool photos of myself which I only posted one on Twitter. Hit the trail next. It was a circular trail and fairly easy to walk. Took a group picture with the mountain sign, but I doubted it would ever see the light of day. Bought these two rice custard-filled buns before hitting the actual trail. Talked to some people on the way, including the tour guide. She was very sweet, and we talked about traveling to Vietnam because she was thinking about doing that with her family and I was internally surprised because she didn’t look like she was old enough for a family, but who was I to judge? The sights were breathtaking. Had this Korean girl take some great pictures of me which I will post on Instagram later after I’m done editing my pictures, which I absolutely need to rush because Adobe is locking my cracked Lightroom app in around three days and I haven’t finished anything [Update: it’s still here]. Small-talked to this Canadian family and asked them why the letter 'r' was so difficult to pronounce while waiting for the rest of the crew to arrive. Took some silly selfies on the trail there when there was nobody looking. Finally finished it, not too long but I guess it was worth the money. Got on the van that took us to an air market nearby. Bought a bag of persimmons for my family for no clear reason. I don’t think any of my family actually ate that. Then we had lunch. Ate rice again, this time with grilled chicken, some native soup, and fried egg and veggies that looked just like a typical Vietnamese lunch. Southeast Asian countries really took their roots seriously. Talked to this French guy (iirc) about his company, and he revealed that he was on this work trip with four other people, doing sales (and sponsored for the trip god me when) and well we then never spoke again. It was cool. I asked for the company name from the Indian woman next to me on the way to the next stop because I was so obsessed with LinkedIn. Arrived at this native place where there was a woman sitting and weaving fabrics. It was cool; I appreciated the dedication. Some people took pictures of her; I felt bad. I didn’t really assess what my feelings were at the time, but I thought she was used to the objectification as an exotic being getting discovered by invasive species around the world. Real Native American vibes, you know when white people voyaged across the Atlantic Ocean and discovered the “new” human species, then they went back home and reported to their masters to prepare attack plans just because imperialism was so rampant at the time, also just because the village belonged to a minor ethnicity (Hmong, again iirc). ANYWAYS back to the main details. We then sat to hear a guy talk about coffee tea (tea made from fermented coffee shells) and tried some tea and coffee. Of course, it sucked because I hate coffee. Bought two small containers of candle scent because why not. Visited a waterfall next, but there was nothing interesting to take pictures of so I just climbed down the rocks and sat next to the stream and felt the fresh water running through my hands. Arrived back to the main city, said bye to some people, picked up my clothes, and bought this rice and marinated pork legs (I thought the sauce was seasoned, but damn it was bland af) and pad Thai to eat back in my room. Put on some movie and enjoyed the rest of the night. Fell asleep.

<h3>Day 6</h3>
Didn't have to wake up early today, but it was the last full day in Thailand so I woke up early to do my homework, took a shower, and discovered the city properly in the daylight because I didn't have the chance to do so. Walked to these thrift stores outside the city center and the clothes there were interesting to say the least. I don’t know why people would buy most of them, but there was a reason why they were there I guess. Walked back to this market where there was a cooking school I booked to attend. Took a while to navigate, but I finally saw them. The horror every second I got when I saw that more and more white people came in. I was one of four non-whites there. What the actual fuck…? To reminisce on that statistic I feel like it’s fair because I don’t think most Asians do these kinds of activities. Or maybe the cooking school advertised to white people mainly. Also thought we would attend the class in the market, but it turned out that we would be transported to a mini-village, super luxurious place outside the city. We cooked five dishes. I made fried rolls, pad Thai (yesss I cannot get enough of pad Thai sue me), green curry, tom yum soup, and mango sticky rice. It was super chill. The instructor was real friendly and funny. I loved her a lot. But overall the vibe was not there. I talked to this girl and her girlfriend whom I thought was her mother to my utmost embarrassment. Eavesdropped on this US couple where the husband was a private pilot and the wife was a dentist talking to another pair from the UK. I didn’t get to hear what the other couple was doing for a living, though, but damn everything stressed me out. The two other couples were also from the UK. I was the only single person who arrived on my own in that group. How lonely is that ell oh ell. Anyways this girl from one of the UK young couples asked me how my English had an American accent, and I told her that because I studied English from American expats who came to Vietnam because they would exploit our culture and could feel better for themselves than at home where they would not be regarded as high as Vietnamese did. I thought it kinda made our conversation go sour because of that, but it was all in my head. I was glad I got to experience some culture, because the variety of ingredients that went into each dish would make a white person cry. Got driven back to the market so I could go back to my hotel. Watch another movie (?). My social battery was dead dead. Fell asleep.

<h3>Day 7</h3>
Last day in Thailand. Woke up late this time because I did not have to arrive at the airport until 12, but still had to pack everything early because the checkout was 11 a.m. I thought it was 12… The host knocked on my door when I just finished showering; it was that hectic. I literally just crammed everything in my duffel bag. It looked like the zipper would come undone at any minute. Checked out of the room thirty minutes after 11, but thankfully she did not charge me extra for that. Booked another Grab to the airport because I still had one (1) discount coupon left, better use it because there was no chance I would come back to Thailand in the near future. Damn, the traffic was crazy. Anyways, we arrived at the airport. Had to wait for like fifteen minutes for the check-in because Vietjet Air did not have an online or self-check-in counter, so it was really messed up. Vietjet only allowed 7kg of carry-on but from what I felt on my shoulder my bag seemed to be way heavier than that. Sneakily removed the bag of persimmons from the weighing scale but the woman behind the counter did not seem to look at it anyway so yeah I got passed that. The bag was actually 10kg, screw my life. Passed the security and visa check, and finally just waited for the plane to arrive. Of course, it would arrive late. It’s literally Vietjet Air’s specialty. Tempted to buy Burger King but damn that stuff was expensive af (typical airport prices). The plane eventually arrived an hour later than the actual departure time… Finally finished my trip to Thailand. I got like 200 baht left, so I was happy I did not buy the Krispy Kreme at the Don Mueang airport. The trip overall was pretty eye-opening, and totally different from what I experienced ten years ago. I did not remember anything back then, anyway, so consider it my first time.
