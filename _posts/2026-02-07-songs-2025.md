**17\. Ojos Tristes - Selena Gomez & benny blanco (with The Marías)**

I like how nostalgic this song is, period. It feels like the kind of song that you’ve heard in the 70s-80s despite not even being born by then, but that is probably attributed to the fact that this is almost a cover of the original song “El muchacho de los ojos tristes” by Jeanette back in the early 80s. The dream pop track is just ethereal and floating, thanks to the acoustic guitar, soft drums, and Gomez’s light, breezy vocals infused with The Marías' haunting vocals.

Fav lyrics: "El muchacho de los ojos tristes / Vive solo y necesita amor / Como el aire, necesita verme"

**16\. Damn Right - JENNIE (with Kali Uchis & Childish Gambino)**

Not to mention the super addicting R&B loops in the choruses, the highlight in this song has to be Kali Uchis’ angelic, sensual vocals beginning in the solo second verse and her harmonizing with JENNIE for the rest of the song that magically adds a sultry touch to the slow-jam punchy track. Childish Gambino’s verses were a cute addition, too.

Fav lyrics: "'Cause I do it so effortlessly, do it my way / Do it so good, I'll make your troubles disappear / Damn, I do it so well, make the time stand still / Could I give myself tens when they're clearly due?"

**15\. Your Love - JISOO**

JISOO’s EP was featured on my Music page earlier this year thanks to its gorgeous production effort, and “Your Love” is definitely the best one here. A full English track, she made a great effort to enunciate the lyrics, which I genuinely appreciate. Talking about the production, I like how she and her team mastered the ingredients of an undeniable pop track: the full transformation into a booming chorus, and the electronic elements that were apparently playing into her vocal strengths that are not all about technique or power, but are clearly deep and unique.

Fav lyrics: "I swear that we'll be young forever / The world slows down when we're together"

**14\. Sports car - Tate McRae**

McRae's signature baby-voice, breathy vocals, and cursive singing style often prevent her from growing on me despite how many potential jams she has, but “greedy” and “Sports car” are seemingly outliers. I might as well take back my critique and admit that her vocals are made for this song: partially because her enunciation is clearer this time, and with this song’s topic being innuendos, you kind of need that whispery style to deliver. The production from Ryan Tedder is a killer too, just in case you haven’t heard it from me yet.

Fav lyrics: "So my type (Type) / Got butterflies (Oh) / So good it hurts (Hurts) / Thinkin' 'bout what we did before this verse"

**13\. Killah - Lady Gaga & Gesaffelstein** 

Another electronic banger has hit the tower. If many regard “Abracadabra” as Lady Gaga’s return to form and even tout it as the best song from the album, I only feel lukewarm and rather prefer listening to “Killah” on repeat, for its banger post-chorus instrumental drop that gets more banging every iteration. Her vocals on the groovy track especially shine here, radiating eerie robotic, electronic zombie, Frankenstein vibes (I’m literally bullshitting here in case you haven’t figured it out). I just can’t fully describe it, but it’s very The Fame-era Lady Gaga to me if she ever went EDM…

Fav lyrics: "I'm a killah / And, boy, you're gonna die tonight / Oh, killah, killah, killah, killah" (I know these lyrics are bland but the way she sang it... \*chef's kiss\*)

**12\. Midnight Sun - Zara Larsson**

Touted as the “Twitter vibes” song of the year, “Midnight Sun” initially went viral on the social media platform for months before it actually took off recently thanks to Zara’s signature hit “Lush Life” randomly going viral again on the internet. Her belting at the end chorus was pure eargasm and one of the biggest song highlights this year for me. The lush, summery production starts out with minimal instrumentation for the first pre-chorus that lets her vocals shine, then explodes at the first post-chorus when she belts, and lingers with additional production elements through the second part of the song, gradually building up a masterpiece that concludes with the aforementioned mind-blowing belt.

Fav lyrics: "I'm walking barefoot, feel the grass in between my toes / Bombshell, wind in my hair, baby, let it blow, yeah / It's been a while since I cried over something so nice (So nice)"

**11\. Fast - Demi Lovato**

Sorry not sorry (if you get the reference) for one more electronic song in this list. “Fast” was fun, a certified club banger (in my opinion), and was on repeat in my head for one month straight. For an artist who has barely experimented with electronica, she did such a great job delivering this song. The production is so early 2010s, certainly ageless in my opinion and giving a one-two punch taste of the pinnacle of EDM. The deliberate stops and vocal alteration in the song (“I wanna go / I wanna go fast / I wanna go hard”) in the second chorus were chef’s kiss, delaying the gratification when the full synths kick in. The final extended instrumental satisfied that itch with an expansion of that glossy, neon-bright, nocturnal production, full of euphoria and a well-rounded culmination of undeniable fun at the peak of a club dance—fast at a pace that can only be described as a rush full of dopamine where you can leave all your worries behind and just live in the moment.

Fav lyrics: "I wanna go fast, I wanna go hard / I wanna go anywhere, anywhere you are / No matter how close, no matter how far / I wanna go anywhere, anywhere you are"

**10\. Times Like These - Addison Rae**

A complete 180 from “Fast” is “Times Like These.” With lyrics so retrospective and a sound that is even more so, Addison Rae and her production team played her vocal strengths so well here. A little bit of Lana here, a little bit of Britney there, atmospheric reverbs, and drizzles of electronic synths throughout have layered together to create such a specific, moody, laid-back listening experience. Its rhythmic repetitions getting busier over time act like a premonition of a seismic shift bound to happen, as Addison questions her life decisions and comes to terms with the contemporary flow of time. “No matter what I try to do, in times like these, it’s how it has to be,” she muses, accepting that life is just a closet full of skeletons, and as much as she regrets things in the past, it’s all over now and she just has to live with it. The song fit so perfectly in her debut album: a set of songs that perfectly encapsulate what nostalgia means: appreciation of the past sprinkled with some what-ifs. That kind of album that you only need to listen to one song to know that it is produced to provoke retrospection.

Fav lyrics: "Should I jump in the unknown / Or is it better to know how it unfolds? / Am I too young to be this mad? / Am I too old to blame my dad?"

**9\. My Man on Willpower - Sabrina Carpenter**

Personally, there is nothing much to say about this song. It is just a well-rounded, well-crafted pop song that includes the signature Sabrina cheeky, flirty punchlines in the song lyrics and her I-hate-men attitude (which I completely agree with, by the way). I do want to specifically shout out to/compliment the structure and production of this song, which are truly perfect with euphoric post-choruses that once again prove the credibility of John Ryan (“Night Changes” and “You & I” by 1D, and “Feather” and “Taste” by Sabrina herself), Amy Allen (“Adore You” by Harry Styles, “Without You” by Halsey, and “pretty isn’t pretty” by Olivia Rodrigo), and Jack Antonoff (should I even have to list?). I especially love the use of light strings on the outro which contrasts the more rugged and industrial programming (?) of the intro.

Fav lyrics: "My man's in touch with his emotions / My man won't touch me with a twenty-foot pole / My man's forgotten his devotion / Where he's gone, God only knows" (the whole bridge, basically)

**8\. Lost In Devotion - Carly Rae Jepsen**

I have noticed that every time Mrs. Carly Rae Jepsen (yes, you’ve read that correctly) releases something, she never fails to appear on my year-end favorite songs list ever. Which remains true this time, too, given how the ten-year anniversary of E•MO•TION manages to add quite a few good tunes here, especially “Lost In Devotion,” the final original track on the album (we’re not talking about the remixes as I haven’t even touched them and I don’t think I will). It’s just a delectable pop song overall with such a banging bridge (the build-up is wowza, I’m serious), nothing crazy, but the memories associated with this song could knock my socks off every time I listen to it. A time that I yearned so hard over someone who I knew would never be mine… The final paragraph in the “Crush” article refers to this song, just to let it out.

Fav lyrics: "And I'm not scared of feeling for you now / And I'm not scared of looking back at everything / I'm not scared of feeling for you now / I'm just scared of missing out on everything"

**7\. Plastic Box - JADE**

A somewhat dedicated fan of Little Mix, I feel somewhat terrible that the only member I seem to feel some kind of excitement over their solo debut is JADE. I absolutely adore and live for her experimental and thorough mastery of the pop craft. If “Angel of My Dreams” and “Midnight Cowboy” were a lukewarm introduction to her style IN MY OPINION, I was blown away by “IT Girl” and, after two more “downs,” she reignited and set my enthusiasm ablaze again, for real this time, with “Plastic Box.” With no unexpected, experimental elements, “Plastic Box” effectively showcases her aforementioned mastery, with fast synth-fused beats on the verses and choruses, minimal build-up, but still grabs ears. I love the whispering “oh’s” she layered in the choruses, signifying a note of vulnerability that is clearly conveyed in the lyrics. I just love these sad bangers so much.

Fav lyrics: "It's irrational and impossible / 'Cause I know you had a life before me / But I'm jealous of your exes"

**6\. Relationships - HAIM**

If the previous was only one of the big highlights in its album, “Relationships” is definitely the biggest and only highlight on HAIM’s new album “I quit.” Produced by the renowned Rostam, it features one of the catchiest choruses I have heard this year (and probably this decade). It was such a standout track that it overshadows all other songs in the album. I tried to get into it, but imagine my dissatisfaction (and disappointment, if you want my honest reaction) when I realized that almost none of the rest of the album sounds like it, with an exception of like two other tracks. The song itself is very HAIM, like if you listen to their singles you would kind of expect this type of production from them, but at the same time, it’s not. The structure is fairly simple, like a song that is only crafted around its absolute gem of a chorus. Still, I also appreciate the pre-choruses where Danielle prolongs the “why’s” which weirdly scratches an itch somewhere in my ear.

Fav lyrics: "Fuckin' relationships / Don't they end up all the same / When there's no one left to blame? / I think I'm in love, but I can't stand fuckin' relationships"

**5\. twilight zone - Ariana Grande**

The lead single for her expanded edition of eternal sunshine, twilight zone is a big jam. What feels like an uplifting melody representing a twilight is accompanied by such devastating lyrics. With further analysis, it dawned on me that this song perfectly captures the appearance of twilight, during the sunset when she faces the truth of a breakup and during the sunrise when she recollects her memories spent with her lover in their happy years. Grande’s vocals continue to shine, adding dreamy, atmospheric layers to the bass line representing the heaviness of her lover moving on. Echoes of her vocals at the outro further support the dichotomy, bringing listeners back to reality.

Fav lyrics: "It's not like I'd ever change a thing / 'Cause I'm right here where I'm meant to be / Not that I'd call you, I won't / Sometimes, I just can't believe / You happened"

**4\. ĐIỀU EM KHÔNG MUỐN - Phùng Khánh Linh**

A Vietnamese song has never been in my top five favorite songs list ever, but this year not one but two songs are in here, which is as rare as it can be. A song that could easily be seen in the lane of Sufjan Stevens, “ĐIỀU EM KHÔNG MUỐN” highlights Linh’s talent and growth as a songwriter, not just in lyrical aspects but also in choice of production and her knowledge of the album’s main sonic themes of dream pop and shoegaze. The atmosphere built in this song is just extraordinary, with whispering, airy, breezing, and shaky vocals that lay and support the heavy, despairing emotions conveyed in the lyrics. I want to shout out to the instrumental break that replaces what could have been a second verse, for it expands the effective atmospheric build-up after the first chorus. It brings out the kind of emotion that “Fourth of July” by Stevens does: surrender, prolonged sadness, drowning and reveling in loss. That kind of song that you know you have to put on when you get in the feels.

Fav lyrics: "Nổi loạn chỉ làm mọi thứ tệ hơn / Mối đêm em dằn vặt mình bằng làn khói bủa vây / Xác xơ phai tàn là vạn cảm xúc đã đổi thay"

**3\. The Fate of Ophelia - Taylor Swift** 

It never occurred to me that I would have a Taylor Swift single in my year-end list, let alone a lead single, but here we are, breaking the norms again with “The Fate of Ophelia.” A masterpiece of a jam, full of sound bites of a chorus, and viral videos on the internet, it’s really THE song of the year for me in terms of commercial success. The first time I heard it, it did not impress me that much, probably because I just finished revisiting The Tortured Poets Department right before hitting play, and the sonic switch up had not fully registered with me yet. Then I watched the music video and replayed it some more and gradually it became my favorite song of the album. Swift really chose the perfect lead single here, not just because the song itself is a banger, but it is also a rather decent introduction to her return to full pop production. Synth-pop at its finest, with co-production credits from her old collaborators Max Martin and Shellback, the result is a ten-week and counting reign on the US charts, a feat that she never had before, even when she already had a twenty-year legacy full of hits under her belt.

Fav lyrics: "The eldest daughter of a nobleman / Ophelia lived in fantasy / But love was a cold bed full of scorpions / The venom stole her sanity"

**2\. Quá Muộn - MIN**

Not only is this dream-pop ballad masterpiece criminally underrated (AKA not being a single), it is also buried in an average album. For Dear Min, the singer’s sophomore album, only three songs are listenable out of eight, and thankfully those three are pure excellence (like PURE). MIN’s sweet, angelic vocals cannot be any more suitable for this slow-tempo pop song, sprinkled with synths and bass and produced by none other than her frequent collaborator, the legendary Khắc Hưng, who was behind some of the most popular V-pop hits in the past decade (“Tìm,” “Trên Tình Bạn Dưới Tình Yêu,” “Nơi Này Có Anh,” “Người Hãy Quên Em Đi,” etc.). It reminds me of the highlights in Ariana Grande’s eternal sunshine, a floating, weightless, atmospheric, lush, dreamy feeling that also lingers and sears itself deeply in one’s mind (especially the bridge and last chorus).

Fav lyrics: "Giờ đã quá muộn màng / Em sẽ không bao giờ nhớ thêm / Những chiếc hôn kia nồng ấm từng đêm / Nhìn thời gian trôi mau để mình tạm quên cơn đau"

**1\. Current Affairs - Lorde**

The soft electronic bass line (or whatever that is IDK I’m not a producer) in Current Affairs drew me in first, then the explicit lyrics, the outro, then the experimental sample in the chorus (because who was that?), then Dexta Daps’ 2014 single “Morning Love”, the switched-up key in the second verse (that was sooooo good), and finally Lorde’s echoed screams in the background. Just so many sick elements that made up this beautiful mess of a masterpiece and there is not a single second that bored me. The intro is just retrospective and feels nostalgic, and her deep vocals added so much depth to it. The electronic vocal sample played a signature role too, albeit sounding weird to me at first but ended up so addictive. The pre-chorus just hits with every single line as Lorde stresses and elongates the last words, leaving me yearning and reeling for an amorphous, liberating life I have never experienced. When the sample kicks in, my heart races, already missing the rush of sweaty bouts of intimacy and youth. This was legit the song I most looked forward to listening to live when my friend and I drove to Ohio for her concert, because it just hit different with the live band and expansive sonic experience. I almost shed a tear; it was THAT pleasurable.

Fav lyrics: "That's how it tends to start / You're in the light, then you're in the dark / Then someone throws a flare / You tasted my underwear / I knew we were fucked"
