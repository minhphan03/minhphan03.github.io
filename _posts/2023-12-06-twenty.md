Three years ago, on my first birthday post, I wished I had a boyfriend. Today, I still haven’t had one. I continue to wish upon the brightest star. I have finally accepted the fact that I am either just a coward for fearing dating and having social anxiety or a late bloomer. People say love only comes when you least expect it. Searching for love only makes you lonely, so I would say, "well," I guess I'll continue living this way. In gay people’s terms, I figure that it seems even more impossible to meet a single available gay person who would hit on you. So, I'm at a dilemma: how do I signal to people that I'm available without sounding desperate?

In the grand scheme of things in life, looking for relationships at the age of twenty is nothing. You don’t have to have experience in relationships at twenty, although some will say there is something wrong with you. In the long term, this is a time when you should think about establishing your career, knowing what you’re doing, and figuring things out for yourself. I thought I did that already, but it turns out I did not. I’m seriously thinking about switching careers to accounting, like my mom did, but maybe not until I settle down in the US. They do not want international students to succeed here, and damn, did I realize that fact late. I mean, it’s kind of crazy to think about how society expects you to learn about all these things and be mature enough to navigate this twisted, manipulative world. All this anxiety stems from this overthinking mess in my head, and it can only worsen from here. What do you mean I almost melt my brain off over this idea of paying a $30 fine for not returning a book in time? I got so worked up and anxious over the possibility of getting out of line and catching myself in said circumstances because I knew better than troubling people's business. I obsess over the need to play my cards right and carefully to not crash again. When I say again, look at the price I paid out of pocket for my surgery in the US that I would lament until the day I die (another post about it soon-ish!). I went bankrupt. All of my savings, gone. I mean, it’s low-key the worst thing I’ve dealt with this year. If there is one thing I can retain from the memories in 2023 (which is most of my 19th year on Earth), I’d say that’s the one.

What did I achieve in my 19th year on Earth?

**I had my first true internship.** The first one I had was not really an internship, more like a learning experience at a company. I did not work on any project worth impactful enough for it to count as one. This one, while at a corporate one (unfortunately, because it would certainly help me break into the industry better), but it’s remote, so I can go home and take a summer break. I had a great time working and learning new technologies, so no complaints on that. I wish I had learned more, though.

**The surgery.** No kidding — I said it was the worst thing, but it’s also the best thing that has ever happened to me for a long while. I anticipated this for so long, and I am so glad I got it done and over with. For years and years, I was self-conscious about my jaw and facial structure, and I swore I would do anything to change it. This surgery made me feel more comfortable with myself, and I’ve been way happier because of it. No more negative thoughts about my appearance (aside from occasional thoughts about how frail I am and comparisons to gym guys), so that’s a good start.

**Going to therapy.** I finally did something about my mental health issues, and therapy was that first step. Thankfully, my school insurance paid for everything, so I didn’t have to pay for anything for that. I can barely afford food (I’m on a budget here!), and school job payments are only so manageable that I seriously need to think far ahead about what I can afford. Vinyl records are the only things I’m collecting right now, so yeah.

**Dedication to watch more movies.** Some new kind of entertainment, I guess. I wasn't big on movies, but now I’m on my mission to watch 365 movies this year, one that I’m about to achieve. It’s cool to embrace a new culture and learn about the rich history of cinema as it reflects and critiques our lives. Watching movies is rewarding: it makes me feel alive and acculturated. I can make excuses to spend time spent on social media, which I’ve been trying to do for years. It also tackles my attention-deficit habits, albeit very excruciatingly, by forcing me to focus on pictures for a long time. Look at how great the lengths I went to watch movies (see [Inside the Yellow Cocoon Shell](https://minhphan03.com/2023/10/16/cocoon)). *Side note:* I’ve had lots of favorite movies from this year, which I’ll write about in a future blog.

**Visiting Europe for the first time.** I transferred at Istanbul, Turkey this summer on my way home, and man was it a beautiful city. A super cool short trip around the city to observe people’s daily activities and meet some cool people on the way. Hopefully not the last time.

Those were memorable. The nicest memories of my last teenage year, because I am now not a teenager anymore. Isn’t it saddening to say it out loud like that? It’s like I’m losing some kind of identity, to be honest. Now I am forced to look forward to think about these young adults in their twenties in the US, where I am going to spend a lot of my future, and how privileged they are to pace so much more quickly than I am in these trying times. I feel pitiful, but I hate to be pitied. I think everyone does. It’s like I am estranged into a new territory I have no means or preparation to fight. Biologically, I don’t feel any change. I’m one year older than yesterday when I was still a teenager. Unless somebody asks, nobody knows I’m fighting these new battles as a twenty-year-old guy (it still feels strange to write that out.) I guess I still have to survive. Man, do I really have to survive now. There will be many surprises in store for me for the next year before I turn 21, but hey, there’s nothing I can do.

Man, do I really have to survive these days of twentysomethings.
